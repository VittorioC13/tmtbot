### 2. MARKET DYNAMICS & SENTIMENT

The TMT (Technology, Media, and Telecommunications) sector is currently navigating a landscape marked by cautious optimism and strategic acquisitions. The overall sentiment reflects a blend of growth potential and challenges, influenced by macroeconomic factors, technological advancements, and regulatory scrutiny.

@@@ Subsector Breakdown:
- **Technology:** The technology subsector remains vibrant, with significant activity in enterprise software. For instance, Hg's acquisition of OneStream, valued at approximately $6.4 billion, underscores the demand for robust financial software solutions. This deal is expected to close in H1 2026, indicating strong investor confidence in the software market.
- **Media:** The media sector is undergoing transformation, with companies adapting to digital consumption trends. However, traditional media continues to face challenges from digital platforms, impacting advertising revenues.
- **Telecommunications:** The telecom sector is innovating with advancements in 5G technology, focusing on enhancing user experiences. Companies are investing in infrastructure to support new applications and services.
- **Fintech:** The fintech space is thriving, with investments in innovative solutions. Wingman's recent investment in InterProse, a debt collection software firm, highlights the ongoing consolidation and growth in this sector.
- **AI:** The AI subsector is experiencing rapid growth, with companies across various industries integrating AI solutions to enhance operational efficiency and customer engagement.

#### Key Market Drivers and Headwinds

@@@ Drivers:
- **Technological Advancements:** Continuous innovation in software and AI is driving growth across TMT sectors. The acquisition of OneStream by Hg exemplifies the strategic focus on enhancing enterprise financial solutions.
- **Increased Investment:** Venture capital and private equity investments remain strong, particularly in tech and fintech, as investors seek to capitalize on emerging trends.

@@@ Headwinds:
- **Regulatory Scrutiny:** Heightened regulatory scrutiny, particularly in the tech sector, poses risks to M&A activities and market valuations. Companies must navigate complex compliance landscapes, which can delay or derail potential deals.
- **Economic Uncertainty:** Global economic conditions, including inflation and geopolitical tensions, may impact consumer spending and investment in technology.

#### Subsector Performance Analysis

- **Software:** The software sector is performing well, driven by demand for enterprise solutions. The acquisition of OneStream reflects the ongoing consolidation trend in this space, as companies seek to enhance their software offerings.
- **Media:** Media companies are adapting to changing consumer preferences, with a notable shift towards digital content consumption. Traditional media continues to face declining revenues from advertising.
- **Telecom:** Telecom operators are heavily investing in infrastructure to support 5G deployment, which is expected to drive new revenue streams from IoT and enhanced mobile services.
- **Fintech:** The fintech sector is thriving, with innovations in payment solutions and digital banking. Wingman's investment in InterProse highlights the consolidation trend in this space.
- **AI:** The AI subsector is booming, with applications across various industries. Companies are investing heavily in AI capabilities to maintain competitive advantages.

### 5. TECH TRENDS

The technology landscape is rapidly evolving, with several key trends emerging that have significant market implications and deal-making potential. This analysis will focus on the following trends: Artificial Intelligence (AI), Healthtech, and Deeptech. Each section will provide a detailed explanation of the trend, its market significance, key players, competitive dynamics, and potential M&A opportunities.

#### Artificial Intelligence (AI)

- **Trend Explanation:** AI encompasses technologies that enable machines to perform tasks that typically require human intelligence, such as understanding natural language and recognizing patterns. The global AI market is projected to grow significantly, with estimates suggesting it could reach $997.8 billion by 2028, growing at a CAGR of 40.2%. 

- **Key Companies:**
  - **Hark:** An AI startup focused on developing human-centric AI models. Recently, Hark hired former Apple designer Abidur Chowdhury, indicating its commitment to strong product design and user experience. The company is backed by $100 million from its founder, Brett Adcock, and aims to release its first AI model soon.
  - **Figure AI:** A robotics startup also founded by Brett Adcock, which achieved a post-money valuation of $39 billion in a recent financing round. The company focuses on humanoid robots and advanced AI infrastructure.

- **Competitive Landscape:** The AI market is highly competitive, with major players like NVIDIA (NVDA) and Google (GOOGL) investing heavily in AI technologies. The emergence of startups like Hark and Figure AI indicates a trend towards specialized AI applications, particularly in consumer-facing sectors.

- **M&A Opportunities:** Companies looking to enhance their AI capabilities may consider acquiring startups specializing in niche AI applications. For instance, the acquisition of Hark or similar startups could provide established companies with innovative AI solutions to integrate into their product offerings.

#### Healthtech

- **Trend Explanation:** The healthtech sector is witnessing a shift towards integrated care models, with a decline in funding for standalone digital solutions. Healthtech funding in India fell to $447 million in 2025, down from $707 million in 2024, as investors increasingly favor models that combine technology with care delivery.

- **Key Companies:**
  - **PB Healthcare Services:** Raised $161 million in 2025, the highest for a healthcare startup in India, focusing on integrated care solutions.
  - **Neuberg Diagnostics:** A clinical laboratory and diagnostic services provider that secured $109 million, emphasizing the importance of offline, integrated healthcare solutions.

- **Competitive Landscape:** The healthtech space is becoming increasingly competitive, with traditional healthcare providers like Apollo Hospitals acquiring startups to enhance their service offerings. The shift towards integrated care models is reshaping investment strategies, with a focus on companies that can deliver comprehensive patient outcomes.

- **M&A Opportunities:** Established healthcare providers may seek to acquire tech-enabled health startups that align with integrated care models. The acquisition of Onco by Apollo Hospitals illustrates the trend of traditional players absorbing innovative startups to enhance their service delivery.

#### Deeptech

- **Trend Explanation:** Deeptech refers to startups focused on advanced technologies such as robotics, AI, and aerospace. There is a growing interest among seasoned entrepreneurs from the consumer internet space to pivot towards deeptech, which often requires longer development timelines and significant capital investment.

- **Key Companies:**
  - **Manav:** A robotics startup founded by former Ola Electric executives, focusing on industrial applications and building prototype machines.
  - **General Autonomy:** Co-founded by former Sharechat executives, this startup has secured $3 million in seed funding to develop robotics solutions.

- **Competitive Landscape:** The deeptech sector is characterized by a mix of established players and emerging startups. The transition of consumer internet founders into deeptech indicates a maturation of the startup ecosystem, although the path remains challenging due to the complexity of the technologies involved.

- **M&A Opportunities:** As deeptech continues to evolve, established companies may look to acquire startups that offer innovative solutions in robotics or AI. The involvement of experienced founders in deeptech ventures could lead to strategic partnerships and acquisitions that enhance technological capabilities.

### Actionable Insights for Bankers and Investors

@@@ Focus on identifying startups within the AI and deeptech sectors that are led by experienced founders, as they may have a higher probability of success.

@@@ Consider the shift towards integrated care models in healthtech when evaluating investment opportunities, as startups that align with this trend are likely to attract more funding.

@@@ Monitor the competitive landscape closely, as established companies may pursue acquisitions to bolster their technology offerings, particularly in AI and healthtech.
