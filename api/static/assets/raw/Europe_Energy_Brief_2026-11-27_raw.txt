@@@ Key Data Points:
- **Survey size:** Approximately 2,000 U.S. consumers.
- **Holiday spending intentions:** 35% expect to spend more on holiday gifts and products than last year; 23% expect to spend less. The net difference is 12 percentage points, up from 7 percentage points last year.
- **Inflation’s role:** 54% of consumers planning to spend more cite inflation, indicating higher prices—not necessarily more items purchased—are driving much of the increase.
- **AI adoption:** 52% of holiday shoppers have used or plan to use AI tools this season, up from 45% last year. Adoption rises to 65% among consumers aged 16–34.
- **Automated AI purchases:** Only 3% of holiday shoppers are comfortable letting AI make purchases automatically.
- **Forecast holiday growth:** eCommerce spending is expected to grow 8–10% year over year; spending at general merchandise, household goods, and food retailers is expected to grow about 4–5%.
- **Airfare:** Airfares are up more than 20% year over year, while travel demand has held up.

@@@ Main Insights:
- **Spending growth may overstate volume growth:** Higher prices are expected to account for much of the increase in retail spending, while consumers may buy roughly the same—or fewer—items.
- **Consumer outlook is mixed:** More consumers plan to spend more than less, but inflation is also squeezing budgets and prompting some shoppers to cut spending.
- **AI is primarily a shopping aid:** Consumers are using AI to find deals and compare prices, but remain reluctant to delegate purchasing decisions.
- **Retail performance may diverge:** eCommerce is forecast to grow faster than broader retail categories, with higher-income households contributing to stronger spending intentions.
- **Freight volumes have not confirmed a broad demand pickup:** Available freight data do not yet show a meaningful increase in shipping volumes.
- **Travel remains relatively resilient:** Consumers continue to prioritize travel despite substantially higher airfares.
