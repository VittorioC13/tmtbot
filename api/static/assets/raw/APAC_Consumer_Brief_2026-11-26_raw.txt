### 7. MACROECONOMIC UPDATE

@@@ Key Data Points:
- **Survey size:** Approximately 2,000 U.S. consumers.
- **Holiday spending intentions:** 35% expect to spend more on gifts and products than last year; 23% expect to spend less. The net balance of 12 percentage points compares with 7 percentage points last year.
- **Inflation as a spending driver:** 54% of consumers planning to spend more cite inflation, indicating higher prices—not necessarily more items—as a key reason for increased budgets.
- **AI use for holiday shopping:** 52% of shoppers have used or plan to use AI tools, up from 45% last year. Adoption reaches 65% among consumers aged 16–34.
- **AI-enabled purchasing:** Only 3% are comfortable allowing AI to make purchases automatically, even within set guidelines.
- **Holiday e-commerce:** Forecast to grow 8–10% year over year.
- **General merchandise, household goods, and food retail:** Holiday spending is expected to grow about 4–5%, with higher prices accounting for much of the increase.
- **Airfares:** More than 20% higher than last year, while travel demand has held up.
- **Freight:** Available data have not yet confirmed a meaningful pickup in shipping volumes.

@@@ Main Insights:
- Holiday spending intentions are modestly stronger than last year, but inflation is influencing both consumers who plan to spend more and those who plan to spend less.
- Higher nominal sales may not reflect higher unit volumes: consumers may spend more while purchasing the same number—or fewer—items.
- AI tools are increasingly used to find deals and compare prices. These tools may also encourage additional purchases by helping shoppers discover products and savings.
- Consumers remain reluctant to delegate purchasing decisions to AI, preferring recommendations while retaining control over checkout.
- Morgan Stanley expects e-commerce to outperform broader retail spending, supported in part by AI adoption and stronger spending intentions among higher-income households.
- Travel demand has remained resilient despite materially higher airfares.

@@@ Market Commentary:
- “This year holiday sales may rise even if shoppers leave with fewer bags.”
- “The real test of consumer strength is how much more people buy, not how much more they pay.”
- Morgan Stanley’s outlook points to an uneven holiday season: stronger e-commerce growth, more moderate growth across major retail categories, and limited evidence so far of a meaningful increase in freight volumes.

@@@ Consumer & Retail Sector Relevance:
- Retailers may report higher holiday revenue without corresponding unit-volume growth, making pricing, mix, and comparable-sales volumes important indicators of underlying demand.
- Persistent price pressure could constrain discretionary purchases, even as it lifts reported consumer spending.
- E-commerce retailers and platforms with effective deal discovery and price-comparison tools may benefit from rising AI use, although consumers largely retain control over purchases.
- Higher-income households are expected to provide relative support to holiday spending.
- Travel-related consumer spending appears comparatively resilient, with demand holding up despite airfare increases of more than 20%.
