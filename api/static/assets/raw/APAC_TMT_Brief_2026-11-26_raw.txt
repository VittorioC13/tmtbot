- **AI use in holiday shopping:** 52% have used or plan to use AI tools, up from 45% last year; adoption reaches 65% among consumers aged 16–34
- **Automated AI purchases:** 3% are comfortable allowing AI to make purchases automatically, even within specified guidelines
- **Expected holiday e-commerce growth:** 8–10% year over year
- **Expected growth at general merchandise, household goods, and food retailers:** 4–5%
- **Airfare:** Fares are up more than 20% from last year

@@@ Main Insights:
- **Spending versus prices:** Higher holiday spending does not necessarily signal consumers are buying more; inflation is driving higher budgets for many shoppers and forcing others to cut budgets.
- **Uneven consumer outlook:** Spending intentions are modestly stronger than last year, but price pressure remains a constraint on household purchasing power.
- **AI supports deal-seeking:** Shoppers are increasingly using AI to find bargains and compare prices, though most prefer to retain control over purchases.
- **Limited volume evidence:** Freight data have not shown a meaningful pickup in shipping volumes, consistent with spending growth driven substantially by higher prices rather than more units sold.
- **Travel remains resilient:** Consumers continue to prioritize travel despite sharply higher airfares.

@@@ Market Commentary:
- **Morgan Stanley’s assessment:** The holiday season may produce higher sales even if shoppers buy fewer items; the key measure of consumer strength is how much more people purchase, not simply how much more they pay.
- **Retail outlook:** E-commerce is expected to grow faster than spending at general merchandise, household goods, and food retailers, with AI adoption and higher-income household spending intentions providing support.
- **Airline outlook:** Travel demand has held up despite fares rising more than 20% year over year.

@@@ TMT Sector Relevance:

- **AI commerce:** Adoption is rising, but only 3% of shoppers are comfortable with automated purchasing; near-term commercial value appears more concentrated in recommendations and bargain search than autonomous checkout.
