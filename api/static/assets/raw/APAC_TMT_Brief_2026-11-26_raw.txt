### 7. MACROECONOMIC UPDATE

@@@ Key Data Points:
- Approximately 2,000 U.S. consumers were surveyed.
- 35% expect to spend more on holiday gifts and products than last year; 23% expect to spend less. The gap is 12 percentage points, up from 7 percentage points last year.
- Among consumers planning to spend more, 54% cite inflation as a reason.
- 52% of holiday shoppers have used or plan to use AI tools this season, up from 45% last year.
- AI usage reaches 65% among shoppers aged 16–34.
- Only 3% are comfortable allowing AI to make purchases automatically, even within set guidelines.
- Holiday eCommerce spending is expected to grow 8–10% year over year.
- Holiday spending at general merchandise, household goods, and food retailers is expected to grow about 4–5%.
- Airline demand has held up despite fares rising more than 20% year over year.

@@@ Main Insights:
- Higher prices are driving both increased and reduced holiday budgets. Many shoppers expecting to spend more are paying more for similar purchases, rather than buying more items.
- The outlook for holiday spending is modestly brighter, but sales growth may overstate underlying volume growth.
- AI is increasingly used to find deals and compare prices. These tools may also prompt additional purchases by helping shoppers discover products and savings.
- Consumers remain cautious about delegating purchasing decisions: AI recommendations are more acceptable than automated checkout.
- Freight data have not yet shown a meaningful pickup in shipping volumes, consistent with holiday sales growth being driven substantially by prices rather than higher unit volumes.
- Travel remains a relative bright spot, with consumers continuing to prioritize airline travel despite higher fares.

@@@ Market Commentary:
- Michelle Weaver said that holiday sales may rise even if shoppers leave with fewer bags; the key measure of consumer strength is how much more people buy, not simply how much more they pay.
- Morgan Stanley’s outlook points to uneven holiday spending: eCommerce is expected to grow faster than spending at general merchandise, household goods, and food retailers.
- The commentary highlights a gap between AI adoption for shopping assistance and consumers’ willingness to let AI make purchases autonomously.

@@@ TMT Sector Relevance:
- The projected 8–10% eCommerce growth and broader use of AI shopping tools are supportive of digital commerce platforms and retailers’ online channels, though growth may reflect higher prices as well as transaction activity.
- AI-powered search, product discovery, and price-comparison features could influence customer acquisition and conversion for eCommerce platforms and retailers.
- Low consumer comfort with automated AI purchases suggests that near-term commerce applications are more likely to assist shoppers than replace human purchasing decisions.
- The absence of a meaningful freight-volume pickup cautions against interpreting retail sales growth as evidence of a broad increase in physical goods volumes.
