### 7. MACROECONOMIC UPDATE

@@@ Key Data Points:
- **Survey size:** Approximately 2,000 U.S. consumers.
- **Holiday spending intentions:** 35% expect to spend more on gifts and products than last year; 23% expect to spend less.
- **Net spending outlook:** The share expecting to spend more exceeds the share expecting to spend less by 12 percentage points, up from 7 percentage points last year.
- **Inflation as a factor:** 54% of consumers planning to spend more cite inflation, suggesting higher prices—not necessarily more items—are driving much of the increase.
- **AI adoption:** 52% of holiday shoppers have used or plan to use AI tools, up from 45% last year; adoption reaches 65% among consumers aged 16–34.
- **AI purchasing comfort:** Only 3% are comfortable letting AI make purchases automatically, even with a budget or guidelines.
- **Holiday e-commerce forecast:** Spending is expected to grow 8%–10% year over year.
- **General merchandise, household goods, and food:** Holiday spending growth is expected to be about 4%–5%, with higher prices accounting for much of the increase.
- **Airline fares:** Fares are up more than 20% from last year, while travel demand has held up.

@@@ Main Insights:
- **Spending growth may overstate volume growth:** Consumers may spend more while purchasing the same number of gifts—or fewer—because prices are higher.
- **Inflation is squeezing budgets in both directions:** It is cited by consumers planning to spend more and by those cutting their holiday budgets.
- **AI is increasingly used for deal discovery:** Shoppers primarily use AI to find bargains and compare prices; savings and product discovery could also prompt additional purchases.
- **Consumers retain control over transactions:** Shoppers appear more comfortable accepting AI recommendations than allowing AI to complete purchases.
- **Holiday demand is uneven across channels and categories:** E-commerce and travel are expected to show relative strength, while broader retail growth is expected to rely substantially on price increases.
- **Shipping data do not yet indicate a meaningful volume pickup:** Freight trends reinforce the distinction between higher sales value and stronger unit demand.

@@@ Market Commentary:
- **Michelle Weaver, Morgan Stanley:** “This year holiday sales may rise even if shoppers leave with fewer bags.”
- **Michelle Weaver, Morgan Stanley:** “The real test of consumer strength is how much more people buy, not how much more they pay.”
- **Morgan Stanley’s outlook:** Holiday e-commerce growth is forecast at 8%–10%, supported in part by AI adoption and stronger spending intentions among higher-income households; broader retail categories are expected to grow about 4%–5%.

@@@ Consumer & Retail Sector Relevance:
- **Retailers:** Reported sales growth may be driven more by inflation and pricing than by higher unit volumes, making transaction and unit data important indicators of underlying demand.
- **E-commerce and digital tools:** Wider AI adoption may help shoppers compare prices and find deals, potentially benefiting platforms and retailers with strong digital discovery capabilities.
- **Consumer budgets:** Inflation is prompting both higher nominal spending and budget reductions, pointing to continued pressure on value-oriented purchasing.
- **Travel:** Resilient demand despite fares rising more than 20% suggests consumers continue to prioritize travel, supporting airline-sector demand.
