### 7. MACROECONOMIC UPDATE

@@@ Key Data Points:
- **Survey sample:** Approximately 2,000 U.S. consumers.
- **Holiday spending intentions:** 35% expect to spend more on gifts and products than last year, while 23% expect to spend less—a net difference of 12 percentage points, up from 7 points last year.
- **Inflation as a spending driver:** 54% of consumers planning to spend more cite inflation, indicating that higher prices—not necessarily more purchases—are driving much of the increase.
- **AI adoption:** 52% of holiday shoppers have used or plan to use AI tools this season, up from 45% last year. Adoption reaches 65% among consumers aged 16–34.
- **Automated AI purchases:** Only 3% of holiday shoppers are comfortable allowing AI to make purchases automatically, even within set guidelines.
- **Holiday eCommerce outlook:** Spending is expected to grow 8–10% year over year.
- **General merchandise, household goods, and food:** Holiday spending growth is expected to be approximately 4–5%, with higher prices accounting for much of the increase.
- **Airfares:** Fares are up more than 20% year over year, while travel demand has held up.
- **Freight volumes:** Freight data have not yet confirmed a meaningful pickup in shipping volumes.

@@@ Main Insights:
- Higher prices are lifting nominal holiday budgets, but the transcript suggests shoppers may buy roughly the same amount—or fewer items—for more money.
- Inflation is a factor for consumers on both sides of the spending outlook: it raises spending for some through higher prices and squeezes budgets for others.
- AI is increasingly used to find deals and compare prices. Those tools may also encourage additional purchases by surfacing savings and new products.
- Consumers remain reluctant to delegate purchasing decisions: they are more comfortable using AI for recommendations than for automatic transactions.
- Holiday spending growth is expected to be uneven, with eCommerce and higher-income households supporting the outlook.
- The lack of a clear freight-volume pickup is consistent with a holiday sales increase driven more by pricing than by a substantial rise in goods purchased.
- Travel is a relative bright spot, with demand holding up despite materially higher airfares.

@@@ Market Commentary:
- Morgan Stanley’s Michelle Weaver described the holiday outlook as modestly brighter, while emphasizing that inflation is driving both higher planned spending and budget cuts.
- Weaver said holiday eCommerce spending is expected to grow 8–10% year over year, while spending across general merchandise, household goods, and food is expected to rise about 4–5%.
- Weaver’s central test of consumer strength was the volume of purchases: holiday sales can rise even if shoppers leave stores with fewer bags.

@@@ Consumer & Retail Sector Relevance:
- Retailers may report nominal sales growth without a corresponding increase in units sold, making pricing and volume trends important to distinguish.
- Consumers’ price sensitivity and interest in deal-finding tools may benefit retailers with competitive pricing, promotions, and effective digital comparison experiences.
- The expected 8–10% eCommerce growth outlook points to continued channel opportunity, while the broader 4–5% retail growth outlook suggests more moderate expansion across general merchandise, household goods, and food.
- Resilient travel demand despite airfares rising more than 20% may support travel-related consumer spending, even as shoppers manage merchandise budgets closely.
