### 7. MACROECONOMIC UPDATE

@@@ Key Data Points:
- **Consumer survey:** Approximately 2,000 U.S. consumers were surveyed.
- **Holiday spending intentions:** 35% expect to spend more on gifts and products than last year; 23% expect to spend less. The net difference is 12 percentage points, up from 7 percentage points last year.
- **Inflation as a factor:** 54% of consumers planning to spend more cite inflation as a reason.
- **AI use for holiday shopping:** 52% of holiday shoppers have used or plan to use AI tools this season, compared with 45% last year.
- **Younger shoppers:** 65% of shoppers aged 16–34 have used or plan to use AI tools.
- **Automated AI purchasing:** Only 3% are comfortable allowing AI to make purchases automatically, even within set guidelines.
- **Holiday e-commerce forecast:** Morgan Stanley expects spending to grow 8–10% year over year.
- **General merchandise, household goods, and food retailers:** Expected holiday spending growth is 4–5%, with higher prices accounting for much of the increase.
- **Airfares:** Fares are up more than 20% year over year, while travel demand has held up.

@@@ Main Insights:
- **Higher prices, not necessarily more purchases, are driving higher holiday budgets:** Consumers planning to spend more often attribute that increase to inflation, while inflation is also prompting other shoppers to cut budgets.
- **The outlook is modestly brighter but uneven:** The share expecting to spend more exceeds the share expecting to spend less by a wider margin than last year.
- **AI is primarily a shopping and comparison tool:** Shoppers are using AI to find deals and compare prices, but very few are comfortable delegating purchasing decisions.
- **AI could support spending:** Easier deal discovery and product recommendations may encourage additional purchases, although the transcript does not quantify that effect.
- **Shipping volumes have not confirmed a meaningful pickup:** This supports the view that higher retail spending forecasts may reflect price increases more than a broad increase in goods volumes.
- **Travel is a relative bright spot:** Consumers continue to prioritize travel despite materially higher airfares.

@@@ Market Commentary:
- **Michelle Weaver, Morgan Stanley:** “This year holiday sales may rise even if shoppers leave with fewer bags.”
- **Michelle Weaver, Morgan Stanley:** “The real test of consumer strength is how much more people buy, not how much more they pay.”
- **Morgan Stanley outlook:** Holiday e-commerce is forecast to grow 8–10% year over year, while spending across general merchandise, household goods, and food retailers is expected to increase 4–5%.

@@@ Industrial Sector Relevance:
- **Freight and logistics:** The lack of a meaningful pickup in shipping volumes is a cautionary signal for freight operators and logistics businesses that depend on growth in physical goods movement.
- **Consumer-facing manufacturers and suppliers:** Retail spending growth driven mainly by higher prices, rather than more units sold, may limit volume growth for manufacturers and suppliers serving general merchandise, household goods, and food retailers.
- **Airlines and travel-related businesses:** Resilient travel demand despite airfares rising more than 20% year over year points to continued consumer support for travel-related activity.
