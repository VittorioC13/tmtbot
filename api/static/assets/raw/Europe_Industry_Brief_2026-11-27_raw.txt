### 7. MACROECONOMIC UPDATE

@@@ Key Data Points:
- **Consumer survey:** Approximately 2,000 U.S. consumers participated.
- **Holiday spending intentions:** 35% expect to spend more than last year; 23% expect to spend less, a net difference of 12 percentage points versus 7 points last year.
- **Inflation’s role:** 54% of consumers planning to spend more cite inflation as a reason, indicating higher prices—not necessarily more items purchased—are driving increased budgets.
- **AI use in holiday shopping:** 52% of shoppers have used or plan to use AI tools, up from 45% last year; adoption reaches 65% among consumers aged 16–34.
- **AI-enabled purchases:** 3% of shoppers are comfortable allowing AI to make purchases automatically.
- **Holiday e-commerce outlook:** Morgan Stanley expects spending to grow 8–10% year over year.
- **Broader retail outlook:** Spending at general merchandise, household goods, and food retailers is expected to grow about 4–5%, with higher prices accounting for much of the increase.
- **Airline demand:** Airfares are up more than 20% year over year, while travel demand has held up.

@@@ Main Insights:
- **Nominal versus real growth:** Holiday sales may increase even as shoppers buy the same or fewer items; price increases appear to explain a significant share of higher planned spending.
- **Consumer outlook is mixed:** More respondents expect to spend more than less, but inflation is also constraining budgets for consumers planning to reduce spending.
- **AI is primarily a shopping aid:** Consumers are using AI to find deals and compare prices, but remain reluctant to delegate purchasing decisions.
- **E-commerce remains a relative bright spot:** Expected growth of 8–10% is supported partly by AI adoption and stronger spending intentions among higher-income households.
- **Volume signals remain cautious:** Freight data have not yet confirmed a meaningful pickup in shipping volumes, tempering the positive signal from nominal retail growth.
- **Travel spending is resilient:** Consumers continue to prioritize travel despite substantially higher airfares.

@@@ Market Commentary:
- **Morgan Stanley’s assessment:** “This year holiday sales may rise even if shoppers leave with fewer bags.”
- **Consumer-strength test:** The report argues that the key indicator is how much more consumers buy, rather than how much more they pay.
- **AI and spending:** Deal discovery and product recommendations could encourage additional purchases, although most consumers want to retain control of checkout and payment.

@@@ Industrial Sector Relevance:
- **Freight and logistics:** The absence of a meaningful pickup in shipping volumes suggests that higher retail sales forecasts may not translate into stronger goods volumes or a broad-based near-term boost for freight operators.
- **Manufacturing and distribution:** Retail growth driven mainly by prices, rather than unit volumes, may limit incremental demand for production, warehousing, and transportation capacity.
- **Airlines and travel-linked businesses:** Resilient travel demand despite airfares rising more than 20% year over year supports a stronger outlook for passenger volumes and related travel services than for goods-shipping activity.
