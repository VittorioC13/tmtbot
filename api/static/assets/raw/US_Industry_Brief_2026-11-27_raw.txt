### 7. MACROECONOMIC UPDATE

@@@ Key Data Points:
- **Survey sample:** Approximately 2,000 U.S. consumers
- **Holiday spending intentions:** 35% expect to spend more than last year; 23% expect to spend less—a 12 percentage-point gap, up from 7 points last year
- **Inflation’s role:** 54% of consumers planning to spend more cite inflation and higher prices as a reason
- **AI adoption:** 52% of holiday shoppers have used or plan to use AI tools this season, up from 45% last year; adoption reaches 65% among consumers aged 16–34
- **Automated AI purchases:** Only 3% of holiday shoppers are comfortable allowing AI to make purchases automatically
- **Holiday e-commerce:** Expected to grow 8–10% year over year
- **General merchandise, household goods, and food retail:** Expected holiday spending growth of approximately 4–5%
- **Airfares:** More than 20% higher than last year

@@@ Main Insights:
- **Higher prices are lifting nominal budgets:** Many consumers expect to spend more while purchasing the same gifts or fewer items, rather than increasing purchase volumes.
- **Inflation is affecting shoppers on both sides:** It is a reason cited by consumers planning to spend more, but also a key factor behind budget cuts among those planning to spend less.
- **AI is primarily a shopping-assistance tool:** Consumers are using it to find deals and compare prices, but remain reluctant to delegate purchasing decisions.
- **AI-enabled bargain hunting could support additional purchases:** Easier deal discovery and product recommendations may encourage shoppers to spend more overall.
- **Holiday demand is uneven:** E-commerce and higher-income households are expected to provide support, while freight data have not yet shown a meaningful increase in shipping volumes.
- **Travel remains a relative bright spot:** Consumers continue to prioritize air travel despite substantially higher fares.

@@@ Market Commentary:
- **Morgan Stanley’s Michelle Weaver:** “This year holiday sales may rise even if shoppers leave with fewer bags.”
- **Morgan Stanley’s assessment:** The key test of consumer strength is whether shoppers buy more items, not simply whether they pay higher prices.
- **Morgan Stanley’s outlook:** E-commerce spending is forecast to grow 8–10%, while spending across general merchandise, household goods, and food is expected to increase about 4–5%, with much of that growth attributed to higher prices.

@@@ Industrial Sector Relevance:
- **Freight and logistics:** The lack of a meaningful pickup in shipping volumes suggests that higher retail spending forecasts may not translate into comparable growth in goods volumes or freight activity.
- **Consumer-facing industrial demand:** Retail, packaging, and distribution businesses should distinguish between price-driven sales growth and increases in physical unit volumes.
- **Airlines and travel-related businesses:** Resilient travel demand despite fares rising more than 20% points to continued consumer support for air travel.
- **Planning implication:** Businesses exposed to holiday goods demand should monitor shipment volumes and unit sales alongside nominal revenue growth.
