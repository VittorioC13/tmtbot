- **Holiday spending intentions:** 35% expect to spend more on holiday gifts and products than last year; 23% expect to spend less. The net difference is 12 percentage points, up from 7 percentage points last year.
- **Inflation as a spending driver:** 54% of consumers planning to spend more cite inflation and higher prices as a reason.
- **AI shopping tools:** 52% of holiday shoppers have used or plan to use AI tools this season, up from 45% last year; adoption reaches 65% among people aged 16–34.
- **Automated purchases:** Only 3% are comfortable allowing AI to make purchases automatically, even within set guidelines.
- **Holiday eCommerce:** Expected to grow 8–10% year over year.
- **General merchandise, household goods, and food retail:** Expected holiday spending growth of approximately 4–5%.
- **Airfares:** Fares are up more than 20% from last year, while travel demand has held up.

@@@ Main Insights:
- **Higher prices are lifting budgets:** Many consumers expecting to spend more appear to be paying more for similar purchases, rather than buying more items.
- **Inflation is squeezing both sides of the spending outlook:** Higher prices are cited by consumers planning to increase spending as well as those cutting their holiday budgets.
- **AI is increasingly part of bargain hunting:** Shoppers mainly use AI to find deals and compare prices, though easier discovery and perceived savings could prompt additional purchases.
- **Consumers retain purchase control:** Shoppers are more comfortable receiving AI recommendations than delegating transactions.
- **Holiday performance is likely to be uneven:** Higher-income households and eCommerce are expected to support spending, while freight data have not shown a meaningful increase in shipping volumes.
- **Travel remains a relative bright spot:** Consumers continue to prioritize travel despite substantially higher airfares.

@@@ Market Commentary:
- **Michelle Weaver, Morgan Stanley:** “This year holiday sales may rise even if shoppers leave with fewer bags.”
- **Morgan Stanley’s consumer survey:** The increase in planned holiday spending is being driven in significant part by higher prices, not necessarily greater purchase volumes.
- **Morgan Stanley’s investor takeaway:** The key test of consumer strength is how much more consumers buy, not simply how much more they pay.

@@@ Consumer & Retail Sector Relevance:
