### 7. MACROECONOMIC UPDATE

@@@ Key Data Points:
- Morgan Stanley survey of approximately 2,000 U.S. consumers: 35% expect to spend more on holiday gifts and products than last year; 23% expect to spend less.
- The share expecting to spend more exceeds the share expecting to spend less by 12 percentage points, up from 7 percentage points last year.
- 54% of consumers planning to spend more cite inflation and higher prices as a reason.
- 52% of holiday shoppers have used or plan to use AI tools this season, up from 45% last year; adoption reaches 65% among people aged 16–34.
- Only 3% of holiday shoppers are comfortable allowing AI to make purchases automatically, even within specified limits.
- Morgan Stanley expects holiday e-commerce spending to grow 8–10% year over year.
- Expected holiday spending growth across general merchandise, household goods, and food retailers is approximately 4–5%.
- Airline fares are up more than 20% from last year, while travel demand has held up.

@@@ Main Insights:
- Inflation is driving both higher and lower holiday budgets: some shoppers expect to spend more because prices are higher, while others are cutting budgets in response to price pressures.
- Higher expected spending may not indicate shoppers are buying more items; price increases are expected to account for much of retailers’ sales growth.
- AI is increasingly used to find deals and compare prices, though shoppers remain reluctant to delegate purchasing decisions to AI.
- AI-enabled savings and product discovery could prompt additional purchases, but the transcript does not quantify that potential effect.
- Freight data have not yet shown a meaningful pickup in shipping volumes, supporting the view that higher retail spending may reflect prices more than increased purchase volumes.
- Higher-income household spending intentions and AI adoption are among the factors supporting the e-commerce outlook.

@@@ Market Commentary:
- Michelle Weaver of Morgan Stanley said the holiday outlook is “modestly brighter overall,” but emphasized that inflation is lifting both spending plans and budget pressures.
- Morgan Stanley expects an “uneven holiday shopping season,” with e-commerce growth outpacing broader growth in general merchandise, household goods, and food.
- Weaver’s central distinction: holiday sales may rise even if shoppers leave with fewer bags; consumer strength should be assessed by how much more people buy, not just how much more they pay.
- Travel is a relative bright spot: consumers continue to prioritize it despite airline fares rising more than 20% year over year.

@@@ Consumer & Retail Sector Relevance:
- Retailers may report nominal sales growth without corresponding growth in units sold, limiting the extent to which revenue gains signal stronger underlying demand.
- Consumers’ price sensitivity and budget cuts create pressure on discretionary purchases and increase the importance of promotions and value messaging.
- E-commerce businesses may benefit from projected 8–10% growth and shoppers’ use of AI for deal discovery and price comparison.
- Retailers should distinguish price-driven sales growth from volume growth when assessing demand and inventory needs.
- Resilient travel demand, despite higher fares, suggests consumers are continuing to prioritize travel spending.
