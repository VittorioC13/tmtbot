@@@ Key Data Points:
- **Consumer survey:** Approximately 2,000 U.S. consumers were surveyed.
- **Holiday spending intentions:** 35% expect to spend more on gifts and products than last year, while 23% expect to spend less—a 12-percentage-point gap, up from 7 percentage points last year.
- **Inflation’s role:** 54% of consumers planning to spend more cite inflation, indicating higher prices—not necessarily more items—as a key reason for increased budgets. Most consumers planning to spend less also cite inflation.
- **AI adoption:** 52% of holiday shoppers have used or plan to use AI tools this season, compared with 45% last year; adoption rises to 65% among people aged 16–34.
- **AI purchasing autonomy:** Just 3% of holiday shoppers are comfortable allowing AI to make purchases automatically, even within specified limits.
- **Holiday e-commerce outlook:** Morgan Stanley expects spending to grow 8–10% year over year.
- **General merchandise, household goods, and food:** Morgan Stanley expects holiday spending growth of approximately 4–5%, with higher prices accounting for much of the increase.
- **Air travel:** Demand has held up despite airfares rising more than 20% from last year.
- **Freight:** Available freight data have not yet shown a meaningful pickup in shipping volumes.

@@@ Main Insights:
- **Nominal spending may overstate demand growth:** Consumers may spend more because prices are higher, even if they purchase the same number—or fewer—items.
- **Holiday demand is mixed:** Spending intentions have improved relative to last year, but inflation is pressuring budgets for both consumers expecting to spend more and those expecting to spend less.
- **AI is primarily a shopping aid:** Consumers are using AI to find deals and compare prices, but remain reluctant to delegate purchasing decisions.
- **AI could support incremental spending:** Easier deal discovery and product recommendations may encourage additional purchases, though the transcript does not quantify that effect.
- **Higher-income households are a support:** Morgan Stanley expects their stronger spending intentions to contribute to e-commerce growth.
- **Travel remains comparatively resilient:** Consumers continue prioritizing travel despite sharply higher airfares.

@@@ Market Commentary:
- **Morgan Stanley’s consumer takeaway:** The key test of consumer strength is “how much more people buy, not how much more they pay.”
- **Morgan Stanley’s retail outlook:** E-commerce is expected to outperform broader retail categories, while much of the projected 4–5% growth in general merchandise, household goods, and food reflects higher prices.
- **Morgan Stanley’s logistics observation:** Freight data have not confirmed a meaningful increase in shipping volumes, consistent with the view that higher retail spending may not translate into substantially higher unit sales.
- **Morgan Stanley’s travel observation:** Airline demand has remained firm despite fares rising more than 20% year over year.
