@@@ Key Data Points:
- **Consumer survey:** Approximately 2,000 U.S. consumers participated.
- **Holiday spending intentions:** 35% expect to spend more on gifts and products than last year; 23% expect to spend less. The 12-percentage-point net balance is up from 7 points last year.
- **Inflation as a spending driver:** 54% of consumers planning to spend more cite inflation; consumers planning to spend less also commonly cite higher prices.
- **AI adoption:** 52% of holiday shoppers have used or plan to use AI tools, up from 45% last year. Adoption reaches 65% among consumers aged 16–34.
- **AI-enabled purchasing:** 3% are comfortable allowing AI to make purchases automatically, even within set limits.
- **Holiday e-commerce:** Forecast to grow 8–10% year over year.
- **General merchandise, household goods, and food retail:** Forecast to grow approximately 4–5%, with price increases expected to account for much of the growth.
- **Airline fares:** More than 20% higher than last year, while travel demand has held up.

@@@ Main Insights:
- **Higher prices are lifting spending totals:** Many consumers expect to spend more without buying more items, making nominal spending an imperfect measure of underlying demand.
- **Consumer conditions are uneven:** Some shoppers expect higher budgets, while others are cutting spending in response to the same broad price pressures.
- **AI is primarily a discovery and comparison tool:** Shoppers are using AI to find deals and compare prices, but remain reluctant to delegate purchasing decisions.
- **E-commerce growth may benefit from AI adoption and higher-income households:** Morgan Stanley expects online holiday spending to outpace broader retail categories.
- **Travel remains comparatively resilient:** Consumers continue to prioritize travel despite substantially higher airfares.
- **Freight volumes have not confirmed a material holiday shipping surge:** This supports the view that higher retail sales forecasts do not necessarily indicate a comparable increase in goods volumes.

@@@ Market Commentary:
- **Michelle Weaver, Morgan Stanley:** “This year holiday sales may rise even if shoppers leave with fewer bags.”
- **Michelle Weaver, Morgan Stanley:** “The real test of consumer strength is how much more people buy, not how much more they pay.”
- **Morgan Stanley’s outlook:** E-commerce spending is expected to grow 8–10%, while spending across general merchandise, household goods, and food retailers is expected to rise about 4–5%; higher prices are expected to drive much of the latter increase.

@@@ Industrial Sector Relevance:
- **Freight and logistics:** The absence of a meaningful pickup in shipping volumes suggests holiday sales growth may not translate into a proportional increase in freight demand.
- **Retail supply chains:** If higher prices rather than higher unit sales drive spending, retailers may see less volume-related pressure on distribution and fulfillment than headline sales growth implies.
- **Air transport:** Resilient travel demand despite fares rising more than 20% is a positive demand signal for airlines, though the transcript does not provide capacity, yield, or profit forecasts.
- **Demand assessment:** Industrial companies exposed to consumer goods and transportation should distinguish nominal spending growth from unit-volume growth when evaluating near-term demand.
