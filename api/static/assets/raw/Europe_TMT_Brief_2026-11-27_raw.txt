### 7. MACROECONOMIC UPDATE

@@@ Key Data Points:
- Morgan Stanley surveyed approximately 2,000 U.S. consumers.
- 35% expect to spend more on holiday gifts and products than last year; 23% expect to spend less. The net balance is 12 percentage points, up from 7 points last year.
- Among consumers planning to spend more, 54% cite inflation and higher prices as a reason.
- 52% of holiday shoppers have used or plan to use AI tools this season, up from 45% last year; adoption reaches 65% among consumers aged 16–34.
- 3% of holiday shoppers are comfortable allowing AI to make purchases automatically, even with a budget or guidelines.
- Morgan Stanley expects holiday e-commerce spending to grow 8–10% year over year.
- Holiday spending at general merchandise, household goods, and food retailers is expected to grow about 4–5%, with higher prices accounting for much of the increase.
- Airline demand has held up despite fares rising more than 20% year over year.
- Freight data have not shown a meaningful pickup in shipping volumes.

@@@ Main Insights:
- Higher prices are driving spending increases as well as budget cuts; planned spending growth does not necessarily indicate consumers will buy more items.
- AI is increasingly used for deal-finding and price comparisons, but shoppers remain reluctant to delegate purchasing decisions.
- E-commerce is expected to outperform broader retail spending, with adoption of AI tools and stronger spending intentions among higher-income households contributing to growth.
- Limited evidence of higher freight volumes supports the view that retail growth is being driven more by prices than by increased goods volumes.
- Travel remains a relative bright spot, with consumers continuing to prioritize it despite higher fares.

@@@ Market Commentary:
- Morgan Stanley’s survey shows a more positive spending outlook than last year, but inflation is influencing both consumers who plan to spend more and those who plan to spend less.
- The report’s central takeaway: “This year holiday sales may rise even if shoppers leave with fewer bags.”
- Morgan Stanley characterizes the key measure of consumer strength as how much more people buy, rather than how much more they pay.

@@@ TMT Sector Relevance:
- Expected e-commerce growth of 8–10% supports a relatively constructive outlook for digital commerce, though price-driven growth may not translate into comparable increases in units sold.
- Rising AI use for product discovery and price comparisons creates an opportunity for AI-enabled shopping experiences, but the 3% willingness to let AI complete purchases points to a current limit on fully automated checkout.
- The contrast between e-commerce growth and subdued freight volumes is a reminder to distinguish online sales growth from underlying merchandise volume when assessing digital retail demand.
