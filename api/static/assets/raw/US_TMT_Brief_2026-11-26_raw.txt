### 7. MACROECONOMIC UPDATE

@@@ Key Data Points:
- Morgan Stanley surveyed approximately 2,000 U.S. consumers.
- 35% expect to spend more on holiday gifts and products than last year; 23% expect to spend less, a 12-percentage-point net difference versus 7 points last year.
- Among consumers planning to spend more, 54% cite inflation and higher prices as a reason.
- 52% of holiday shoppers have used or plan to use AI tools this season, up from 45% last year; adoption rises to 65% among shoppers aged 16–34.
- Only 3% are comfortable allowing AI to make purchases automatically, even with budget limits or guidelines.
- Morgan Stanley expects holiday e-commerce spending to grow 8–10% year over year and spending at general merchandise, household goods, and food retailers to grow about 4–5%.
- Airline fares are up more than 20% from last year; freight data have not yet shown a meaningful pickup in shipping volumes.

@@@ Main Insights:
- Higher prices, rather than consumers buying more items, are expected to account for much of the increase in holiday spending.
- Inflation is influencing consumers in both directions: it is prompting some shoppers to spend more in nominal terms while pushing others to cut their budgets.
- AI-powered deal discovery and price comparison are gaining traction, though consumers remain reluctant to delegate purchasing decisions to AI.
- The holiday outlook is uneven: e-commerce and higher-income households provide support, while limited evidence of rising freight volumes points to restraint in goods demand.
- Travel demand is a relative bright spot, with consumers continuing to prioritize travel despite higher airfares.

@@@ Market Commentary:
- Michelle Weaver of Morgan Stanley said the key test of consumer strength is how much more people buy—not simply how much more they pay.
- Morgan Stanley expects holiday sales to rise even if shoppers purchase fewer items, as higher prices lift spending totals.
- The firm noted that AI-assisted savings and product discovery could encourage additional purchases, although shoppers generally want to retain control of checkout.

@@@ TMT Sector Relevance:
- E-commerce platforms and retailers with effective digital merchandising may benefit from projected 8–10% online holiday growth and increased use of AI for deal discovery and price comparison.
- The 52% AI-tool adoption rate signals growing consumer engagement with shopping assistants, but the 3% comfort level with automated purchases suggests near-term value is more likely in search, recommendations, and price comparison than autonomous checkout.
- Because much of projected retail growth reflects higher prices rather than increased unit sales, TMT companies exposed to retail advertising and commerce volumes should distinguish nominal spending growth from underlying demand.
