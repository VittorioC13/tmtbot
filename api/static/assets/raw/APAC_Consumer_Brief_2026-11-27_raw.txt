### 7. MACROECONOMIC UPDATE

@@@ Key Data Points:
- Survey sample: approximately 2,000 U.S. consumers
- Holiday spending intentions: 35% expect to spend more and 23% expect to spend less than last year; the 12-percentage-point net gap compares with 7 percentage points last year
- Inflation is cited by 54% of consumers planning to spend more, with higher prices—not necessarily more purchases—driving much of the increase
- AI use for holiday shopping: 52% have used or plan to use AI tools, up from 45% last year; adoption reaches 65% among people aged 16–34
- Only 3% of holiday shoppers are comfortable allowing AI to make purchases automatically
- Forecast holiday e-commerce spending growth: 8–10% year over year
- Forecast holiday spending growth at general merchandise, household goods, and food retailers: approximately 4–5%
- Airfares are up more than 20% year over year, while travel demand has held up

@@@ Main Insights:
- Higher prices are lifting holiday budgets for some shoppers, while inflation is also causing others to cut their budgets.
- Spending growth may reflect paying more for similar quantities of goods, rather than buying more items.
- AI shopping tools are increasingly used to find deals and compare prices; those savings and product discoveries could also encourage additional purchases.
- Consumers remain more comfortable using AI for recommendations than delegating purchasing decisions.
- Freight data have not yet shown a meaningful pickup in shipping volumes, consistent with limited evidence of materially higher goods volumes.
- Higher-income households and greater AI adoption are among the supports for the e-commerce outlook.

@@@ Market Commentary:
- “This year holiday sales may rise even if shoppers leave with fewer bags.”
- Morgan Stanley’s central test of consumer strength is “how much more people buy, not how much more they pay.”
- The outlook is uneven: e-commerce is forecast to grow faster than spending across general merchandise, household goods, and food retailers.
- Travel is a relative bright spot, with demand holding up despite sharply higher airfares.

@@@ Consumer & Retail Sector Relevance:
- Retail sales growth may overstate unit-demand strength if price increases account for much of the gains.
- Retailers should distinguish revenue growth from volume growth when assessing holiday performance, inventory needs, and promotional effectiveness.
- AI-driven deal discovery may intensify price comparison while also supporting incremental product discovery and purchases.
- Resilient travel demand suggests consumers continue to prioritize experiences, even as higher prices pressure holiday goods budgets.
