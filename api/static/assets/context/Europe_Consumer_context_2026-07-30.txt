Busy Bee: Mike Ashley’s Frasers Group is all over lately.

Frasers, which made a surprise takeover bid for Hugo Boss last month, said in a regulatory filing that it had amassed put options on 15 million shares of Burberry Group, giving it a 4.2 percent stake in the company.

Burberry has lately been showing some signs of a rebound, although investors have been slow to give the company credit.

First-quarter sales grew by 5 percent to 455 million pounds and were fueled by Gen Z and shoppers desire for trenchcoats, scarves and handbags. For the first time in three years, the company is growing across the women’s, men’s, accessories and children’s categories.

[TITLE]Manhattan’s Teeming Union Square Rides a Wave of New Retail:
[TEXT]
The retail numbers are looking pretty good for Manhattan’s Union Square.

Spending in the area has maintained a steady pace over the past 12 months, on par with the preceding year despite inflation and weak consumer confidence. But businesses — particularly in the food and beverage sector — continue to flock to the neighborhood. That’s according to data provided exclusively to WWD by the Union Square Partnership, which this week issues its 2026 Commercial Market Report and its second-quarter 2026 Biz + Broker Report.

“It’s a busy time for Union Square. From what the data says and from what we see on the ground every day, the district is alive and booming,” said Julie Stein, executive director of the Union Square Partnership, which covers an area from 12th to 20th Streets and First to Sixth Avenues and runs the Union Square Business Improvement District.

You May Also Like

Union Square welcomed 51 new storefront retailers in the last 12 months, including 38 food and beverage establishments. That compared with 43 openings in the preceding 12 months. Not to be left out of the surge were fashion and beauty retailers like The North Face, Reformation, PacSun, Ulta, Aritzia and Uniqlo, all of which opened within the past year.

Among the recent food and beverage entries were La Tazza D’Oro for fine Italian pastries and coffee; Seahorse, a stylish seafood brasserie with a raw bar and mermaid mural; the Bar Ambra and Beltane lounges; Narkara for Thai cuisine; the Angelina Italian bakery; Nespresso coffee and espresso, and the Time Out Market food hall.

Union Square’s storefronts are currently 90.1 percent occupied, compared with 88.5 percent a year ago, and 83.4 percent two years ago. There has been 59,000 square feet of ground floor retail space leased over the past 12 months, compared with 40,000 square feet in the preceding 12 months. Broadway from 17th to 18th streets is 100 percent leased and has been a vehicle-free, pedestrian plaza since 2024, under the city’s Broadway Vision plan.

Union Square generated an average $1.85 million in daily consumer spending in the last 12 months, with an average of $82.04 per customer per day, according to Datafy, a visitor analytics platform. That compares to $1.88 million in daily consumer spending for the preceding 12 months, and an average daily consumer spend of $82.18.

Spending in Union Square is “well distributed” across the area’s top categories. Dining and nightlife accounted for 23 percent; grocery and department stores accounted for 20 percent, and clothing and accessories made up 19 percent, reflecting the district’s diverse business mix.

“We are seeing a continued renaissance in the food and beverage space,” Stein said. “Restaurants have long been a part of the Union Square identity for the last 50 years since the Greenmarket has been anchoring the square. This month is the 50th anniversary of the Greenmarket — it’s core to Union Square’s identity.

“One of the other interesting trends we’re seeing in Union Square and in other neighborhoods is around experiential retail,” said Stein, citing Activate Games, a high-tech, interactive indoor gaming facility that recently opened, and the soon-to-open Bubble House, a K-pop experience. “You’re seeing people crave those unique in-person experiences.”

Stein also singled out new construction, citing six ground-up buildings, five under construction, one just completed, by Fifth and Sixth avenues. “You’re seeing a huge overhaul in the look and feel of that section of our district in particular because of the new private investment,” Stein said.

The Union Square Partnership is composed of two 501(c)3 organizations — a local development corporation (LDC) and a business improvement district (BID) operating through a management agreement. The BID provides its core services — a clean team, public safety officers, and landscaping — within the formal boundaries of the BID, which are 14th Street between First and Sixth avenues, and all the properties that front on Union Square Park.

Like other Manhattan neighborhoods such as the Upper East Side, Meatpacking, SoHo, Fifth Avenue and Flatiron, Union Square is a beneficiary of the city’s post-pandemic recovery in foot traffic, the return to office, and the retailers and brands looking to snatch up available real estate.

Business in and around Union Square also benefits from the neighborhood’s eclectic, energetic character, its range of restaurants and stores and its year-round crowds of students, office workers, residents, tourists and protesters. Major retail draws are Whole Foods, Paragon, Barnes & Noble, the Strand Bookstore, Best Buy, Target and Uniqlo, which opened this year. There’s also an array of big-box discounters including Burlington, Nordstrom Rack and DSW.

The area is anchored by Union Square Park, with its dog run, Greenmarket, Holiday Market, and Harvest culinary benefit which this year is scheduled for Nov. 10. Count New York University, Mount Sinai-Union Square, Zeckendorf Towers and other residential high rises as anchors as well. Top restaurants include the Union Square Cafe, Boucherie Union Square, ABC Kitchen, ABC Cocina, and Tarallucci e Vino.

In the first quarter, 14th Street reached 94 percent storefront occupancy between First and Sixth avenues thanks to some new mixed-use projects in development.

The corridor has also benefited by the 14th Street busway introduced six years ago. According to the Partnership, ridership has picked up, trip times are shorter, and there’s no sense that businesses have been negatively impacted despite some early concerns. The M14-SBS bus recorded over 5.1 million riders in the last 12 months with total daily riders on the corridor reaching 14,000. Additionally, the Sixth Avenue subway station on 14th Street was renovated for full accessibility and improved linkups to the F, L, and IRT subway lines.

The 14th Street corridor could see further enhancements in a few years, specifically more greenery, greater outdoor seating for restaurants or just hanging out, safer pedestrian crossings and programming, via a public-private partnership called the 14th Street Plan involving the Partnership, the Department of Transportation, the Meatpacking District and the Parks Department. In its early design stages, the plan intends to improve the streetscape around Union Square along 14th Street and create more of a cohesive look and feel to the corridor, Stein said. The plan is in the early phase of a two-year design process.

“The idea is to make 14th Street a pedestrian street of choice,” said Stein, although many people tend to avoid eastern sections that have dealt with crime, homelessness and illegal street vending.

“We’re also looking at modernizing Union Square Park as part of it,” Stein said. “It’s like what if Union Square became like New York City’s best Italian piazza, and what if 14th Street were its best Parisian boulevard? These are things that are achievable by us.”

[TITLE]Dior Revival Breaks Losing Streak at LVMH Fashion Division:
[TEXT]
PARIS — Dior is starting to feel the Jonathan Anderson effect.

The French fashion house saw double-digit sales growth with American and Japanese clients in the second quarter, helping parent company LVMH Moët Hennessy Louis Vuitton’s key fashion and leather goods division break a two-year losing streak.

The unit, which also includes brands such as Louis Vuitton, Loro Piana and Celine, posted organic sales growth of 1 percent in the three months to June 30, following seven consecutive quarters of declines.

This was in line with analysts’ estimates, amid mixed results from sector peers that indicate luxury brands are increasingly dependent on a small cohort of ultra-wealthy clients, while aspirational consumers remain squeezed by inflation and geopolitical turmoil.

“LVMH demonstrated its solidity and effective strategy. Our maisons — which remained focused on ensuring the utmost quality in our products, and several of which are pursuing their creative renewal — continued to inspire dreams and enhance their desirability,” Bernard Arnault, chairman and chief executive officer of LVMH, said in a statement.

“Accelerating growth in the second quarter arose in particular from the success of Jonathan Anderson’s first designs for Christian Dior, the remarkable performance of Louis Vuitton’s exceptional new stores in Beijing and Seoul, and Tiffany and Bulgari’s iconic lines,” he added.

Cécile Cabanis, chief financial officer of LVMH, said Dior performed better than the fashion division average in the first half, with momentum reflecting the arrival in stores of Anderson’s first products since taking over as creative director of women’s and men’s collections last year.

“When it comes to Dior, all clienteles were up in [the first half],” she told analysts and reporters on a webcast after the market close on Monday.

The brand has seen good uptake for women’s ready-to-wear and leather goods, ranging from new introductions like the Cigale handbag to updates of pillar collections like the Lady Dior, she noted.

“We’ve made a lot of progress,” Cabanis said. “What we are offering is getting success, and there will be more to come.”

While acknowledging Dior has hit speed bumps amid reported shortages of some key styles, Cabanis said she was confident in the general direction of business.

“Whenever you start with creative renewal, and you are in a transition, it’s a bit more complex for your supply chain,” she said. “The trend is positive and accelerating, so I think it’s what matters.”

Feeding the Pyramid

Growth at Vuitton was in line with the division average, with the brand leveraging the 130th anniversary of its signature Monogram motif to return its pillar Speedy and Alma handbag lines to growth, in addition to launching the Monogram Emblème collection, featuring jacquard instead of the traditional coated canvas.

Special-edition Louis Vuitton bags for the 130th anniversary of the Monogram. Courtesy of Louis Vuitton

The house is also seeing progress on women’s rtw, jewelry and perfume, while customers are flocking to its experiential flagships in Seoul and Beijing, opened late last year. The Louis Vuitton Hotel pop-up in London was a commercial success, Cabanis reported.

LVMH has been strategically offloading assets as it focuses on brand reboots under new creative directors at Dior, Celine, Fendi, Loewe and Givenchy. The group revealed in May it was selling Marc Jacobs to WHP Global, having divested its stakes in Stella McCartney and Off-White over the last two years.

Newness has become crucial in the battle to woo and retain VICs, with the top 0.1 percent now accounting for around 23 percent of global expenditure, according to a recent research note by Bernstein analyst Luca Solca and his team. They estimated that 70 million aspirational consumers have exited the market since 2023.

“Selling more to existing consumers will require sustained relevance and a higher innovation tempo. As the inflow of new middle-class consumers slows, brands must lean more heavily on their installed base of repeat clients and VICs, who already own the iconic essentials and will only part with additional money for genuinely new and compelling products,” it noted.

“This is pushing brands to accelerate innovation, whether through category expansion, product refresh or increased brand investment, to remain differentiated, interesting and culturally relevant,” the Bernstein report said.

Cabanis said LVMH continues to court new customers with products at all price ranges.

“Where wealth is created, consumer appetite for luxury — and for our products in particular — is strong,” she said. “It’s very important to continue to nourish the pyramid, so both the very exclusive clients and the aspirational, because we need to continue to recruit.”

Founded in 2014 by CEO Mike Xu, GrubMarket started as an e-commerce platform for fresh produce. It has since morphed into something much larger: a supply chain technology company that operates across all 50 US states and more than 70 countries.

Advertisement

GrubMarket’s thesis is straightforward. The American food supply chain is fragmented, analog, and inefficient. The company deploys what it describes as enterprise AI agents for inventory management, operations, and financial reporting.

The SEC-shaped elephant in the room

For context, an $8 million penalty against a company valued at $4.5 billion is roughly 0.18% of its valuation.

Why the IPO market matters right now

A confidential filing means the company has submitted its S-1 registration statement to the SEC without making it publicly available. This gives GrubMarket the ability to receive regulatory feedback, revise its financials, and choose the optimal moment to go public without tipping its hand to competitors or the market.

The SEC settlement adds a layer of discount risk. If the S-1, once public, reveals growth deceleration or margin compression from its acquisition-heavy strategy, the reception could be cooler.

The acquisition integration risk is real. Sixty acquisitions is an enormous number for any company that hasn’t yet been through the discipline of public market reporting. Each deal brings its own accounting quirks, cultural integration challenges, and potential surprises.
