Representatives of the Federation of Indian Chambers of Commerce and Industry (Ficci), the Associated Chambers of Commerce and Industry of India (Assocham), the Federation of Indian Micro and Small & Medium Enterprises (Fisme) and the Confederation of Indian Industry (CII) met Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman on Tuesday as part of the pre-Budget consultations.

Ficci has proposed a dedicated development finance institution (DFI) for manufacturing, estimating that more than ₹100 trillion in incremental credit would be needed over the next decade to raise manufacturing’s share in gross domestic product (GDP) to 25 per cent. It said borrowing costs in India were 400-500 basis points higher than in China and South Korea, making long-term, competitively priced finance difficult to access for capital-intensive projects.

The proposed DFI would provide long-tenor finance to mid-sized manufacturers in sectors such as batteries, semiconductors, green hydrogen, defence and advanced materials. Ficci has also sought a Strategic Manufacturing Capacity Development Programme to build domestic capacity in 36 priority products and segments across 10 foundation sectors, which together account for nearly 36 per cent of India’s non-petroleum imports.

Separately, Ficci has proposed a ₹12,000 crore production-linked incentive (PLI) scheme for advanced chemistry cell battery components, including cathode and anode materials, electrolytes and separators. It cited a 35-50 per cent cost disadvantage for domestic manufacturers compared with Chinese imports.

Assocham has sought a dedicated PLI scheme for robotics, automation equipment, sensors and precision components, along with stronger support for existing schemes covering specialty steel, textiles, white goods, batteries and solar modules. It has also called for the restoration of weighted tax deductions for research and development (R&D), noting that India’s R&D expenditure remains below 1 per cent of GDP.

To promote electric freight vehicles, Assocham has proposed a 3 per cent interest subvention for MSME fleet owners, loan tenures of seven to eight years, and financing and registration of electric vehicles without batteries. Freight transport accounts for 22-25 per cent of the country’s oil consumption, it said.

CII has called for targeted, time-bound tax incentives, including accelerated depreciation on new plant and machinery, higher weighted deductions for incremental R&D spending and corporate tax benefits for companies generating additional net employment. It has proposed verifying employment through Employees’ Provident Fund Organisation records. The industry body has also sought time-bound disposal of nearly five lakh pending first appeals and faster release of legitimate tax refunds.

For MSMEs, Fisme has proposed a National Enterprise Graduation Mission to help informal businesses enter the formal economy and enable existing formal enterprises to move from micro to small, small to medium and eventually become globally competitive businesses.

The proposal includes a five-year compliance window under which regulatory obligations would increase progressively with an enterprise’s size and risk. Fisme has also sought a three-year transition period for businesses crossing MSME classification thresholds to avoid a sudden increase in compliance requirements.

To improve access to credit, Fisme has proposed an Enterprise Guarantee Passport under the Credit Guarantee Fund Trust for Micro and Small Enterprises. The digital platform would allow MSMEs to submit business, banking, Goods and Services Tax and project details through a common window, enabling participating banks and non-banking financial companies to compete to finance eligible proposals.

Fisme has also called for ready-built, leasable factory spaces in industrial clusters to help expanding MSMEs avoid the high upfront costs of land, construction and approvals. It has proposed a platform to facilitate financing against confirmed export orders and receivables.

[TITLE]Telia, Nokia form AI gigafactory alliance:
[TEXT]
Telia is the latest European telco to throw its hat into the AI gigafactory ring by forming a consortium with Nokia and three others – British neocloud Nscale, Finnish AI cloud provider Verda and Estonian power systems specialist Skeleton Technologies – that will bid for a share of the €10bn funds that the European Commission (EC) is set to pump into seven major AI infrastructure deployments in the European Union.

The move comes as Europe’s major digital infrastructure players decide how, or if, they will get involved with the EC’s plan to help develop sovereign AI infrastructure that will provide the region’s governments and enterprises with a local or regional source for their AI needs.

The EC’s plan revolves around the ‘mobilisation’ of €30bn in investments – up to €10bn from the EC and national public funding sources, and €20bn from the private companies involved in the successful bids. The deadlines for bids to be submitted are fast approaching – 3 December for large-scale facilities and 4 February 2027 for medium-sized facilities – with the winning bids set to be announced in the first half of next year and construction set to commence soon after.

A number of telcos are involved in various bids: Telefónica is heading up a consortium that includes the Spanish government and the Santander banking group; Orange and Iliad Group (via its Scaleway cloud division) are part of the AION consortium; Deutsche Telekom is considering a joint bid with partners but is still wary of the economics associated with the EC’s funding; and Vodafone Group has been linked to a potential bid in partnership with German construction firm Hochtief.

Now Telia has stepped up with its partners to form what they are calling the AI Gigafactory Northern (AIGF Northern) consortium, which aims to develop “trusted, distributed AI infrastructure through a federated, multi-site AI gigafactory across the Nordic and Baltic region” that will combine “large-scale AI computing infrastructure with sovereign cloud and AI platform services, high-capacity connectivity and decarbonised energy”.

The consortium members will also be supported by specialist R&D partners, namely the VTT Technical Research Centre of Finland, which specialises in quantum computing, semiconductors, physical AI and photonics, and Sweden’s largest technical university, the KTH Royal Institute of Technology, with which Telia and the telco’s AI infrastructure partner, investment firm Brookfield, recently forged a sovereign AI partnership.

The AIGF Northern consortium plans to distribute capacity across borders to “reduce concentration risk and strengthen the resilience of Europe’s AI ecosystem”. AIGF Northern will connect facilities in: Oulu, Finland; the Tallinn region in Estonia; Valmiera, Latvia; and a to-be-determined location in Sweden.

“Distributing capacity across these locations will allow the consortium to draw on multiple sources of decarbonised energy, expand computing infrastructure where capacity is available and reduce reliance on any single site or supplier. This approach will strengthen operational resilience and support cross-border expansion as European demand for AI grows,” noted the consortium in this announcement.

Telia, together with its systems integration subsidiary Telia Cygate, will “contribute expertise in connectivity, data transmission, infrastructure operations and sovereign cloud solutions, supporting the development of a federated, multi-country AI ecosystem,” explained the operator.

Anders Olsson, head of Telia Sweden, stated: “Our customers – and our continent – have a rapidly increasing need for scalable and sovereign AI services. To meet this need, Europe’s best must step up. Telia’s highly relevant skills in secure and resilient connectivity and systems integration, combined with our position as a trusted partner for businesses and societies, make us a natural fit for this ambitious consortium. We look forward to working together to create even more value for customers while helping to secure long-term European competitiveness and resilience.”

Taavi Madiberk, CEO and co-founder of Skeleton Technologies, stated: “Europe is stepping up its AI investment, but building lasting AI capacity requires more than compute alone. It also requires the energy and power infrastructure to support that compute efficiently, reliably and at scale, using technologies developed and manufactured in Europe. Northern Europe brings together many of the right conditions: abundant decarbonised electricity, strong engineering capabilities and an advanced industrial base. Skeleton contributes the power systems that ensure AI infrastructure doesn’t waste energy, places less strain on the grid, and gets more compute power from every GPU. The Northern AI Gigafactory is an opportunity to combine these strengths into real European AI capacity, industrial value creation and greater technological sovereignty.”

Mikko Hautala, chief geopolitical and government relations officer at Nokia, added: “Europe’s AI ambitions depend on infrastructure it can trust, control and scale. AIGF Northern brings together the connectivity, computing, cloud and energy capabilities needed to turn that ambition into reality. By linking strengths across borders, we can build a more resilient foundation for European innovation, competitiveness and technological sovereignty.”

Data management and protection software company AvePoint found in its State of AI 2026 report that unauthorized autonomous agent actions were the third most common AI agent-related security breach, affecting 34.1% of companies that reported such incidents.

Those actions included executing transaction and modifying data without approval, though the findings were not isolated to corporate treasury payments.

"When agents negotiate and execute autonomously … governance shifts from monitoring human behavior to monitoring machine-to-machine dependencies," said AvePoint CEO Tianyi Jiang.

Jiang explained that interacting AI agents could amplify errors and trigger transactions before humans can intervene.

“The worst-case scenario is a cascading failure where one compromised or hallucinating agent feeds bad data to another,” he said, potentially “triggering a high-speed chain reaction of flawed financial transfers before a human even realizes it is happening.”

To address such concerns, Kinexys developed a framework that restricts AI agents to predefined transaction parameters, automatically rejecting requests that fall outside authorized limits.

"You are going to draw a box around the agent. The agent will never be able to do anything out of the box," Toh said.

For example, an AI agent authorized to transact up to $5 million would be blocked from executing transactions above that threshold, he said.

Hassan Ahmed, Coinbase's Singapore country director, said safe agentic payments will require clear guardrails, including spending limits, restricted account permissions and explicit authorization requirements.

Users should also be able to monitor activity and revoke access at any time, he said, given the potential for AI to create new opportunities for malicious actors.
