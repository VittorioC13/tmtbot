[TITLE]Streaming Coalition Urges Squires to Support Enforcement Against Abusive SEP Licensing Practices:
[TEXT]
“Once the standard is widely adopted, [these SEP holders] renege on that promise and demand excessive royalties on threat of an injunction.” – Coalition letter

A coalition of ten streaming, broadcasting, and audiovisual media organizations, including the Motion Picture Association (MPA) and the Streaming Innovation Alliance, sent a letter today to U.S. Patent and Trademark Office (USPTO) Director John Squires, urging him to “support strong public and private sector enforcement against abusive SEP licensing practices.” The signatories said that abuse of standard essential patents (SEPs) was undercutting the open process of setting standards for the video streaming technologies their members use.

In December 2025, the USPTO established an SEP Working Group that reports directly to Director Squires. The agency said the group would continue its practice of filing court statements supporting SEP injunctions, such as the statement of interest filed in Radian Memory Systems v. Samsung Electronics in June 2025.

The remaining signatories were the Association of Commercial Television and Video on Demand Services in Europe, the Commercial On-Demand and Broadcasting Association, Strima, the Asociación Española de Video on Demand, the European Video On Demand Coalition, Streaming for Australia, Streamingdiensten Nederland and the Television Association of Programmers Latin America. If left unaddressed, the letter said SEP abuse would harm consumers and competition, significantly affect investment in local creative economies, “misappropriate millions” intended for creative content and new offerings, and lower the quality of the viewing experience.

Standardized codec technologies such as AVC and HEVC allow competing streamers to provide dependable, high-quality video on devices from many manufacturers, the signatories explained. Since inclusion in a standard enhances a patent’s market power by requiring every potential competitor to license the technology, the coalition wrote, SEPs must be licensed on fair, reasonable, and non-discriminatory (FRAND) terms.

According to the coalition, a growing list of SEP holders, many of which it said invest primarily in lawyers and litigation, had abandoned that principle for video streaming standards. These SEP holders commit to FRAND licensing to convince standards bodies, such as the International Organization for Standardization and the International Telecommunications Union, to incorporate their patented technology into industry standards, and then “once the standard is widely adopted, they renege on that promise and demand excessive royalties on threat of an injunction,” the letter stated. The coalition said those injunctions result from lawsuits filed in a handful of jurisdictions that routinely grant them without prompt review of the asserted patents’ validity or before a final infringement determination.

The signatories described this conduct as a “bait-and-switch patent ‘holdup’” affecting streamers bound to a standard for years, noting that AVC and HEVC were published in 2003 and 2013, respectively. According to the letter, the practice imposes large costs by destabilizing streaming technology standards, raising consumer prices, potentially diminishing commercial offerings and chilling streaming innovation while the industry remains in lengthy litigation.

The coalition stated that “the threat isn’t theoretical,” pointing to advisory FRAND royalty guidelines recently issued by a European court, identified in the letter’s footnotes as the Munich Regional Court. “Without any supporting evidence,” the letter said, the court proposed an annual codec royalty for two leading subscription video on demand (SVOD) services at 16% of one-third of annual subscription revenues, increasing to 25% for premium services. The signatories cited a Statista figure projecting nearly $100 billion in global SVOD revenues for 2026 and said that applying only the lower rate across the industry would drain well over $5 billion from the entertainment sector.

The 7th Civil Chamber of the Munich Regional Court I published the guidelines on August 13, 2026, building on recent decisions including ASUS I, ASUS II, Renault and ZTE v. Samsung. The guidelines reflect only that chamber’s views, and other courts may reach different conclusions.

All signatories support the protection of intellectual property (IP) rights, the coalition stated, adding that “it is precisely because we take IP rights so seriously” that they object to holdup tactics. The signatories said those tactics far exceed the value of the underlying patents, raise competition concerns, and damage the reputation of IP rights. The letter also referenced an amicus curiae brief the MPA submitted to the U.S. District Court in Delaware, which it said explained the conduct in detail.

That brief was filed in Disney Enterprises, Inc. v. InterDigital, Inc., an antitrust lawsuit Disney filed in August 2025 alleging that InterDigital’s licensing practices for video compression patents violated Sections 1 and 2 of the Sherman Act.

According to the letter, these SEP holders’ conduct is “cultivating a new generation of opponents for the IP community,” including car makers, TV manufacturers, healthcare providers and farmers. The signatories said they need to facilitate a FRAND licensing framework that respects IP rights and allows the streaming ecosystem to benefit creators, innovators, consumers, and IP rights holders. The coalition urged Director Squires to support public and private sector enforcement against abusive SEP licensing practices.

STOCKHOLM, Nov. 3, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- EQT Infrastructure VI ("EQT Infrastructure") and SatPort Infrastructure ("SatPort") are pleased to announce that SatPort has completed the acquisition of the teleport infrastructure business from communications and IT services provider Speedcast, expanding its carrier-neutral platform for satellite ground infrastructure globally.

Demand for satellite services is growing, with global revenues expected to reach USD 145bn by 2035¹, supported by the increased deployment of LEO satellites. These constellations can provide low-cost, high throughput and secure connectivity to serve the needs of consumers, enterprises and governments, playing an increasingly important role in global telecommunications. Each constellation requires a global network of ground stations, or teleports, which host the antennas and other physical infrastructure, to enable the transmission of data between the Earth and orbiting satellites. As satellite networks expand, operators are increasingly looking for independent infrastructure partners to provide these capabilities at scale.

SatPort was formed by EQT Infrastructure to provide secure, resilient, and well-located essential ground infrastructure for satellite constellations. SatPort combines deep operational expertise with the long-term capital and capabilities to fund, develop, and scale critical infrastructure globally. The acquisition of Speedcast's ground infrastructure network will further enhance SatPort's offering for customers around the world. The business acquired from Speedcast comprises 12 teleports across 11 countries and five continents. More than 100 experienced professionals also join SatPort, and Speedcast will remain a customer under a new long-term infrastructure services agreement.

The acquisition builds on SatPort's global build-to-suit agreement with Telesat, signed in September 2026, to develop, build, and host landing stations for Telesat Lightspeed, a LEO constellation designed for enterprise and government customers. With the acquisition, SatPort is now a fully operational, carrier-neutral global platform for satellite operators, governments, and enterprises, with a network of operating teleports and capabilities to deploy new sites for operators worldwide.

Carl Sjölund, Partner at EQT Infrastructure, said: "The satellite industry is undergoing a fundamental transformation, with rapidly growing constellations creating an unprecedented need for ground infrastructure. Just as we have seen in towers, fiber and data centers, we believe the next phase of growth will be supported by independent, specialized infrastructure platforms that can serve multiple customers at scale. With SatPort, we are building a global leader in this emerging infrastructure category. The acquisition of Speedcast's teleport business is an important milestone in that journey, and we're excited to support Joe and his team in realizing the significant opportunity ahead."

Joe Spytek, Chief Executive Officer of SatPort, said: "With this acquisition, SatPort has built a platform with the global network and operational expertise to provide tailored ground infrastructure for operators in any orbit. With long-term backing from EQT Infrastructure, we're excited to continue growing the platform to serve the next generation of satellite operators as they scale worldwide, allowing our customers to focus on their constellations while we take care of the infrastructure on the ground."

With this transaction, EQT Infrastructure VI is expected to be 80-85 percent invested (including closed and/or signed investments, announced public offers, if applicable, and less any expected syndication).

[TITLE]EloElo raises $ 80mn Series C led by Hornbill Capital; eyes US expansion:
[TEXT]
Bengaluru-based EloElo Group, which operates the microdrama platform Story TV, is stepping up investments in original content, artificial intelligence and international expansion as it looks to tap growing demand for mobile-first entertainment.

The platform has seen daily time spent by users rise to 95 minutes from 65-70 minutes a few months ago. The company has over 120 million app installs across its platforms and more than 40 million monthly active users across Story TV, Master, its micro-learning platform, and Voxy AI.

The expansion plans coincide with an $80 million Series C funding round led by Hornbill Capital and NBCUniversal, taking the company’s total capital raised to $130 million. Existing investors, including Kalaari Capital, Play Ventures, Waterbridge Ventures and Griffin Gaming Partners, also participated.

Founded in Bengaluru in 2020 by former Flipkart executive Saurabh Pandey, EloElo has built a library of over 5,000 microdramas across six languages. It currently produces around 250 new dramas every month and plans to more than double its content slate over the next year, with Hindi, Tamil and Telugu as its key focus languages.

“We hope to more than double our slate in the next one year itself. The idea is not just to double the numbers, but to build genuinely good IP and really good stories,” Pandey told businessline.

Artificial intelligence is increasingly central to its production strategy. Pandey said 70-80% of the workflows involved in making microdramas are now AI-powered, helping bring production costs down to ₹15-17 lakh per drama. The company is also investing in Dolphin AI, its AI-powered creative suite, and plans to expand its data science, machine learning and generative AI engineering teams.

International expansion, beginning with the US, is another priority. The company wants to target both Indian diaspora audiences and native-language viewers in overseas markets.

“We believe there is no Netflix of microdramas yet, and no one brand in this space that dominates global markets. We believe Story TV has that potential,” Pandey said.

“The team’s execution, financial discipline, and AI capabilities position it well for its next phase of growth,” said Manoj Thakur, Founder and Managing Partner of Hornbill Capital.

The expansion comes as microdramas gain traction globally. Omdia estimates the industry will generate $14 billion by the end of 2026. In India, Redseer estimates 40 million people paid for a microdrama at least once in FY26.

“Under Saurabh’s leadership, EloElo has developed a differentiated,mobile-first approach with a clear focus on content and storytelling, positioning the company for long-term success,” said Deepak Arya, Executive Vice President, Corporate Strategy & Development at NBCUniversal.

[TITLE]Canva announces layoffs, job changes for about 22% of workforce:
[TEXT]
Canva Inc. announced job cuts and a sweeping overhaul that will impact more than one-fifth of the workforce, as artificial intelligence forces a structural reckoning at one of Australia’s highest-profile technology companies.

Some 1,271 people, or 22 per cent of Canva’s staff, will lose their jobs, change roles or relocate to central hubs, Chief Executive Officer Melanie Perkins said in a note to employees that was posted on the firm’s website Wednesday.

The sweeping changes at Canva, an online design and publishing platform, mirror the broader upheaval across the software industry. Investors have wiped billions of dollars from the value of these firms amid concerns new technology like Anthropic PBC’s Claude will cannibalize their business.

Artificial intelligence is allowing people to do more on their own without specialty programs, while companies are using AI to operate with fewer workers.

“New technology is transforming what people can create, how they create it, and what they expect from the products they use,” Perkins wrote in the note. “I’ve come to the difficult conclusion that small changes won’t be enough.”

Perkins, who co-founded Canva with Cliff Obrecht and Cameron Adams, didn’t specify how many staff would lose their jobs. But after the Sydney-based firm’s growth slowed for the first time in a decade, Perkins said Canva needs to become a simpler business and return to running small, empowered teams. There will be fewer niche specializations, she said.

She described the reorganization as “the hardest moment in our 13-year history.”

Sydney will become the main base for more of Canva’s product teams. Perkins said more people will work face-to-face, and most hybrid staff will spend two to three days in the office a week from January.

Canva has been preparing for a potential listing, and the overhaul leaves the company in a delicate financial balance. After hitting a $40 billion high in 2021, Canva’s valuation tumbled to roughly $26 billion a year later during a broad tech selloff. While Canva’s dollar worth subsequently rebounded, the effect of more recent tumult is not yet clear.

While the AI era has led to widespread job losses, investors are also questioning when, or even if, the vast associated investments will deliver a financial dividend.

Firmus Grid Ltd. last month scrapped a planned Australian initial public offering of up to $5.5 billion, turning a rising star of the AI data center boom into one of the biggest deal flops in recent memory. Nextdc Ltd. shares plummeted in October after the data center operator said it was considering force majeure options and cut earnings guidance.
