[TITLE]Ovaloop targets Nigeria's 15m retailers with end-to-end business management platform:
[TEXT]
By Juliet Umeh

Nigerian retail technology startup, Ovaloop Technologies, has unveiled an integrated retail operating system designed to help small and medium-scale enterprises, SMEs, digitise their operations, improve inventory management, curb fraud and generate credible business data that can unlock access to financing.

The company said the platform goes beyond payment collection by offering an end-to-end solution that enadbles retailers to manage inventory, sales, payments, accounting and supply chain activities from a single platform, eliminating the need for multiple disconnected applications.

Speaking at the unveiling, Co-founder and Chief Executive Officer of Ovaloop Technologies, Princewill Mba, said the solution was developed after years of studying how African businesses operate and understanding the challenges faced by retailers across the continent.

According to him, while many financial technology firms focus primarily on payment processing, Ovaloop is tackling the broader challenge of helping businesses manage their daily operations more efficiently.

He said: “Most Nigerian businesses are retailers, whether they know it or not. They buy and sell goods, yet many still manage inventory manually or rely on fragmented software for payments, accounting and stock management.

“We built Ovaloop as a retail operating system that manages business operations from end to end. It helps businesses generate reliable data that can be used to access funding because many SMEs struggle to secure loans simply because they cannot provide accurate financial records.”

Mba noted that the retail sector remains one of Africa’s largest economic drivers, adding that reliable business data would not only help retailers scale but also improve financial inclusion by making businesses more bankable.

Also speaking, Co-founder and Chief Technology Officer, Daniel Kilanko, said the platform was built specifically for African businesses instead of adapting foreign software to local realities.

He explained that the solution works both online and offline and can be accessed via web browsers as well as Android and iOS devices, allowing business owners to monitor their operations from anywhere.

Kilanko also unveiled Ovaloop Pay Protect, a payment verification feature designed to curb internal fraud by ensuring that every completed sales transaction is matched with a verified payment.

“No sales transaction can be completed unless a verified payment is linked to it. This eliminates situations where staff divert customer payments into personal accounts while issuing receipts to customers,” he said.

He added that the company plans to integrate e-commerce, logistics, supply chain management and supplier networks into the platform, creating a complete retail ecosystem for businesses.

Responding to questions on adoption among informal traders, Mba said the growing acceptance of digital payments across Nigeria shows that small businesses are increasingly ready for digital transformation.

He said Ovaloop would support adoption through user training, simplified interfaces and dedicated customer support, adding that the platform was developed using feedback from local merchants.

On policy, Mba called for stronger collaboration between technology firms, government agencies and industry associations to promote the digitalisation of retail businesses and improve tax compliance.

Meanwhile, Chairperson of Ovaloop Technologies, Mrs. Titilope Ejimagwa, described the platform as a timely intervention for entrepreneurs battling inventory losses and employee theft.

She said many business owners lose substantial revenue because trusted employees manipulate sales records and divert funds, expressing confidence that the integrated platform would improve accountability, strengthen business governance and support sustainable growth.

The company said its long-term ambition is to make Ovaloop the preferred operating system for retailers across Nigeria and Africa, enabling businesses to manage operations more efficiently, generate bankable financial records and scale with confidence.

[TITLE]At District, E-Commerce Goes Live:
[TEXT]
Eddie Koai co-founded District three years ago, an AI platform that can build an entire online marketplace for any brand. The goal is to help brands big and small build businesses their own way without having to code anything.

The platform ties into to a bigger idea about how marketplaces are closely linked with the communities that necessitated them. Communities have shared interests, and marketplaces, online or otherwise, are built around transactions over these shared interests.

Those close links, however, do not seem to be close enough for consumers today. They want entertainment and commerce rolled in one. They want to be able to live chat with the host making the sale. They want to feel closer, even if they’re paying for it.

Though District is capable of much more, many businesses are interested in a specific feature that the AI platform enables them to do: live selling.

“One of the most popular reasons people actually use District is because they want to add live shopping to their own website or their own platform,” Koai, District CEO, told Sourcing Journal. “Live selling is blowing up right now in the U.S.”

Live commerce in the U.S. is still nowhere close to the popularity and sales volume seen in China, which is projected to reach $1.14 trillion by 2026. It’s an extraordinary leap from 2019, when it was valued at $58.6 billion, according to a market intelligence report from the U.S. International Trade Administration.

Nevertheless, live commerce in the U.S. is growing with industry players like TikTok Shop and live shopping app Whatnot. Market research company Emarketer said the entire sector grew nearly 50 percent in 2025 with $14.64 billion.

“It’s taken some time to click in the U.S., but you’re seeing it all over the place now in the past couple of years,” Koai said. “It has become more of a social experience, where you’re actually building an authentic relationship between the buyer and the seller.”

District Co-Founders Khoi Tran, Eddie Koai, and Patrick Mandia chollette

Koai started District with two of his former colleagues at Snap, the company behind Snapchat. The AI platform charges its brands a monthly subscription, depending on the size of the business and the requirements. In a span of three years, the startup grew its subscriber base in the U.S. and Canada to over 1,000 brands. Koai said collectibles and fashion are among the most popular categories.

Without much fanfare, some of these brands have been quietly growing too, finding their community in their corner of the internet. One of them is NikNax, an online thrifting marketplace that now has over 5,000 sellers listing all sorts of resale items from mugs to trading cards, according to a report from CNBC Make It. It was founded by Jocelyn Elizabeth in 2023, whose 310,000 YouTube subscribers might know better as “Crazy Lamp Lady.”

Just last May, District secured $14.7M in seed funding co-led by Andreessen Horowitz and Kindred Ventures—a vote of confidence that speaks to the potential of live commerce as it breaks the barriers between buyer and seller.

“Historically, platforms have tried to drive a wedge between the buyer and the seller because that’s how platforms demonstrate their value,” said Koai. “We’re flipping that on its head. Our goal is to give businesses a way to actually interact directly with their customers in a much more intimate and authentic way.”

[TITLE]Italian unions agree workers' rights deal with Amazon:
[TEXT]
MILAN, July 22 : Italian unions have signed an agreement with Amazon.com covering leave, employee rights and video surveillance, the unions and the U.S. e-commerce giant said on Wednesday.
