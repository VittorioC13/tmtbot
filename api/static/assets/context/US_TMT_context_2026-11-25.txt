[TITLE]Amazon announces $7.5 billion deal for Israeli AI technology:
[TEXT]
Amazon announced Monday that it has signed an agreement to acquire proprietary technology developed by Israeli artificial intelligence startup Decart and bring the company's three co-founders and its team of researchers and engineers into Amazon.

The transaction is valued at $7.5 billion, according to Israeli financial newspaper Calcalist. Amazon did not disclose the financial terms of the agreement in its official announcement.

The deal covers the technology behind Decart's Optimization Stack (DOS), which is designed to improve the efficiency and performance of AI computing systems.

Decart's three co-founders, Dean Leitersdorf, Moshe Shalev, and Orian Leitersdorf, will join Amazon alongside the company's AI researchers and engineers.

The Israeli startup was founded in 2023 and has developed artificial intelligence technologies, including Lucy, a model that allows users to edit live video in real time, and Oasis, which generates interactive virtual environments.

It was reported in October that Amazon was negotiating a potential acquisition of Decart for approximately $7 billion.

According to Calcalist, Decart previously held acquisition discussions with Nvidia and Anthropic. The latter advanced negotiations for a transaction valued at approximately $6 billion but ultimately withdrew from the proposed deal.

Amazon said it began collaborating with Decart in 2025, with the Israeli company's expertise in optimizing software and hardware performance contributing to efforts to improve the efficiency of Amazon's custom AI chips.

The technology is expected to support Amazon's development of its Trainium processors and improve AI inference, enabling customers to run artificial intelligence models more efficiently and at lower costs.

"Every leap forward in AI starts with getting more out of every chip," said Peter DeSantis, Amazon's senior vice president of foundational AI models, custom silicon, and quantum computing.

"More efficient compute means better training, better reinforcement learning, faster inference, and better models for customers at a lower cost. That's the pillar Trainium and our AI efforts are built on, and from our earliest work with Decart, we knew they were the kind of technologically fearless team who would help us push it even further."

Decart co-founder and CEO Dean Leitersdorf welcomed the agreement.

"From day one, our mission has been to redefine the boundaries of what AI can do and to make it dramatically more efficient along the way," Leitersdorf said.

"Joining Amazon gives our team the scale, the customers, and the infrastructure to pursue that mission faster than we could on our own. We're looking forward to exploring what we can build together."

In May, Decart raised $300 million in a funding round that valued the company at approximately $4 billion. The round included participation from Nvidia, and the startup has raised more than $450 million since its establishment.

According to Calcalist, the agreement with Amazon includes a combination of cash and stock, with payments spread over several installments. The founders have reportedly committed to remaining with Amazon for five years.

Amazon said completion of the transaction remains subject to customary closing conditions, including regulatory approval.

[TITLE]Irish-founded software start-up Hoplite raises $5.5m in funding:
[TEXT]
Irish-founded software start-up Hoplite has raised $5.5 million (€5 million) to develop its platform that provides an easier way for developers to run artificial intelligence coding agents in the cloud.

The company, which was founded by former University of Limerick (UL) students Ryan Morrissey and Bence Redmond earlier this year, gives companies of all sizes the ability to use AI agents to build and test software. Agents can work around the clock in the cloud, but setting it up can be time consuming and need specific staff resources dedicated to managing it.

Hoplite helps to eliminate some of that complexity, allowing developers to connect their code and use their existing set-up to run agents as needed.

“The models are good enough. What’s missing is the infrastructure around them – the set-up, the machine,” said Morrissey, co-founder and chief executive of Hoplite. “The most interesting software in the world is increasingly built by very small teams and their agents. We now give them the same leverage large companies have. We built the version that just works.”

The funding round was backed by Y Combinator, Stripe co-founders John and Patrick Collison, Fin co-founder Des Traynor, Relational Capital, Upside Ventures and Theo Browne of T3 Chat. The money raised will be used to grow Hoplite’s engineering team and bring the product to more users. Hoplite’s agents have already processed 824 billion tokens from 18,000 users.

“AI is completely changing how software development is happening, and Ryan and Bence are at the forefront of that change,” said John Collison.

Hoplite is based in San Francisco and Dublin. Morrisey and Bence met while studying the Stripe-backed immersive software engineering course at UL, but left the course to take part in Y Combinator’s summer 2026 programme.

[TITLE]Cyient Semiconductors gets $10 mn strategic investment from GlobalFoundries:
[TEXT]
Hyderabad-headquartered Cyient Semiconductors on Thursday said it has secured a $10 million strategic equity investment from GlobalFoundries (GF), at a pre-money equity valuation of $490 million.

The funds will be used to expand Cyient Semiconductors’ custom application-specific integrated circuit (ASIC) capabilities as well as silicon development, the company said.

“Having a leading semiconductor ecosystem partner as a strategic investor validates our technology and business model, accelerates our access to advanced manufacturing capabilities, and strengthens our ability to deliver custom silicon and power products globally,” Suman Narayan, the chief executive officer of Cyient Semiconductors said.

Earlier this year in May, the company had announced another strategic financing transaction of $10 million with funds managed by EAAA India Alternatives, in addition to raising $20 million in structured debt.

The company had then said that the funds raised in May would be used to advance its research and development roadmap in custom power semiconductors, building in-house semiconductor validation and testing infrastructure in India to strengthen development and qualification capabilities and to support working capital requirements as Cyient Semiconductors scales larger, longer-cycle global customer programs.

Speaking of the investment made by GlobalFoundries, Cyient said that the investment was a “strong validation” of the company’s vision and the opportunity ahead for semiconductor innovation from India.

“The investment comes at a pivotal time for India’s semiconductor industry as government and industry-led initiatives continue to strengthen the country’s position within the global semiconductor value chain,” the company said.

The semiconductor company, which is part of the Cyient group, is a maker of custom ASIC solutions. It has design centres in India, Belgium, and the US.

Globe, NCS partner for new digital growth opportunities

MANILA, Philippines — One of the country’s major telcos said it is opening new avenues for growth in the country’s expanding digital economy through its strategic partnership with NCS.

Globe Telecom Inc. said the deal positions itself to benefit from rising enterprise demand for artificial intelligence, cloud computing, cybersecurity and advanced digital solutions.

The partnership comes at a time when Philippine businesses and government institutions are accelerating their digital transformation, creating opportunities for technology providers capable of delivering secure, integrated and scalable enterprise solutions.

Across Southeast Asia, demand for IT modernization, automation and AI-enabled services continues to expand as organizations seek to improve productivity, streamline operations and strengthen their competitive capabilities. The Philippines is emerging as an important market for these services as digital adoption moves beyond basic connectivity and online transactions.

Globe’s joint venture with NCS, a member of the Singtel Group, brings together regional technology expertise and established domestic market experience through the integration of Globe’s technology subsidiary Yondu into NCS Philippines.

The combination gives the venture access to a wider portfolio of enterprise solutions and the capabilities to support large-scale digital projects for corporations and government agencies. Its services cover enterprise IT infrastructure, software development, cloud migration, systems integration and cybersecurity, alongside emerging technologies such as AI, predictive analytics and robotic process automation.

A key opportunity lies in helping Philippine organizations move from initial AI adoption to actual business deployment. Digital In Asia has reported that 92% of organizations in the country have adopted some form of AI, although 65% remain in the pilot stage, indicating substantial room for further implementation and commercialization.

Through its NEXT Digital Solutions portfolio, NCS Philippines can help address this gap by enabling businesses to integrate AI, automation and advanced analytics into their daily operations. Its Core IT Services portfolio, meanwhile, provides the underlying systems, infrastructure and cybersecurity required to support these digital applications.

The venture also opens opportunities in specialized enterprise applications, including private 5G networks, edge computing and smart-city technologies, where businesses and public institutions increasingly require customized solutions rather than conventional connectivity services alone.

NCS brings considerable regional resources to the partnership, including approximately 14,000 technology professionals and capabilities spanning 56 technology disciplines. Its regional scale, combined with Yondu’s more than two decades of Philippine market experience, provides a foundation for competing in the country’s growing enterprise technology market.

Under the joint venture structure, NCS holds a 51% majority stake while Globe retains 49% ownership. This enables Globe to participate in the venture’s potential growth and future earnings while benefiting from NCS’s established technology capabilities and regional network.

The arrangement also reflects a capital-efficient strategy for Globe, allowing it to expand its exposure to higher-value ICT services without independently funding the full development and regional scaling of an extensive enterprise technology operation.

For Globe, the opportunity extends beyond providing connectivity. By participating in the delivery of enterprise digital solutions, the company can broaden its role in the technology value chain and gain exposure to business segments that complement its established telecommunications operations.

The expansion is particularly significant as the Philippines strengthens its digital infrastructure and advances policies supporting electronic commerce and digital government services. These developments are expected to sustain demand for secure IT systems, cloud-based platforms and integrated technology solutions.

The NCS Philippines platform gives Globe a strategic avenue to participate in these developments while leveraging a regional technology partner with established expertise and resources. Its ability to translate market demand into actual contracts, recurring revenues and sustainable profitability will be important in determining the venture’s long-term contribution.

The partnership, with an indicated enterprise baseline valuation of approximately P1.87 billion, therefore represents more than a business expansion. It provides Globe with an additional platform for growth in an industry where digital transformation is becoming increasingly central to corporate and government operations. (Contributed story)
