[TITLE]Crypto scammers used SEC’s website to lure investors into alleged $130 million fraud:
[TEXT]
The SEC has removed the misleading filings and sued both firms, but recovering the more than $100 million in alleged investor losses remains unresolved.

More than 223,000 blockchain addresses sent funds to the scheme as fabricated trading profits and false regulatory claims fueled investor confidence.

Two crypto firms allegedly exploited the SEC's own filing system to promote a $130 million scheme promising 60% monthly returns.

The US Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) has removed regulatory filings allegedly used to legitimize a $130 million crypto investment fraud.

In an Oct. 29 announcement, the regulator said BG Wealth Sharing and DSJ Exchange submitted false disclosures to its public databases and subsequently presented those records to prospective investors as evidence of government authorization.

The scheme allegedly raised more than $130 million from tens of thousands of investors across the United States and abroad, resulting in losses exceeding $100 million.

According to the SEC, the companies operated between at least October 2025 and June 2026, promoting a cryptocurrency investment program that promised monthly returns of at least 60% and claimed investors could double their money within 40 days without risk.

BG Wealth portrayed itself as the world's largest hedge fund and an SEC-licensed investment adviser, while DSJ Exchange claimed to run a secure cryptocurrency trading platform that executed profitable trades.

However, investigators alleged that the promised trading activity was fictitious and the companies diverted most investor deposits rather than investing them as advertised.

How the firms exploited the SEC's own filing system

The alleged fraud relied heavily on the credibility of the SEC's public disclosure infrastructure, which allows investors to research financial businesses and securities offerings.

According to the SEC's civil complaint, BG Wealth submitted a Form ADV claiming exempt reporting adviser status, while DSJ Exchange filed a Form D announcing a purported $100 million securities offering.

The filings became publicly accessible through the SEC's Investment Adviser Public Disclosure system and EDGAR database, allowing the promoters to direct prospective investors to government-hosted records as supposed proof of legitimacy.

The companies allegedly reinforced those claims with fabricated registration certificates bearing the SEC's official seal and identifiers associated with their filings.

The deception extended beyond regulatory credentials.

Investigators found that the defendants received more than $130 million from over 223,000 blockchain addresses between March and early June 2026.

The SEC alleges that no more than $25 million was returned to addresses likely belonging to investors through Ponzi-like payments, while the remaining funds were largely misappropriated.

The operation also employed social media campaigns, encrypted messaging platforms, weekly video conferences, and recruitment incentives to attract new participants.

When investors tried to withdraw their funds, the companies allegedly imposed fees of up to 20% and delayed withdrawals before eventually blocking access altogether.

The defendants subsequently demanded additional tax payments and encouraged investors to move their assets to another platform, HQI Exchange, without restoring withdrawal access.

The SEC has now removed BG Wealth's Form ADV and DSJ Exchange's Form D from its public websites.

[TITLE]Weekly news roundup: Taiwan debates talent distribution; SK Hynix looks beyond HBM:

Weekly news roundup: Taiwan debates talent distribution; SK Hynix looks beyond HBM

This week’s coverage highlights intensifying competition and capacity constraints across the global semiconductor industry. Taiwan faces debate over whether public R&D funding should prioritize universities and talent development. Micron workers receive unusually large bonuses amid unresolved labor negotiations. India is expanding state-level semiconductor incentives, while KLA reportedly considers local manufacturing. Memory shortages may persist through 2030, and SK Hynix is investing beyond memory. Server CPU demand is projected to surge, with AMD potentially surpassing Intel in revenue.

Ex-TSMC CTO says Taiwan's research institutes get too much funding and train too little talent

Former TSMC CTO Chenming Hu argues Taiwan directs too much public R&D funding to national research institutes, which train insufficient talent and compete with industry. No institute has publicly responded, although supporters cite collaboration and personnel movement between institutions and companies. Separately, NIAR and MSScorps are applying ALD-based analysis to advanced materials, including High-NA EUV and metal oxide photoresists for possible A16, A14 and A12 processes, highlighting research-industry cooperation.

Micron says 700-plus Taiwan fab workers earned five years' salary in a single year

Micron said more than 700 Taiwan production workers earned at least 60 months of salary in fiscal 2026 total pay, combining performance, mass-production and appreciation bonuses. A new quarterly profit bonus begins in fiscal 2027. The Taoyuan union overwhelmingly rejected Micron’s offer after a warning strike, while Taichung reached a separate agreement. No production disruption has been reported, but negotiations matter because both fabs support Micron’s high-bandwidth memory output.

KLA reportedly weighs India manufacturing entry as Semicon 2.0 opens to equipment makers

KLA is reportedly evaluating manufacturing in India and considering one or two applications under the Semicon 2.0 incentive scheme, although the company did not respond to queries. The move would build on its planned Chennai R&D campus, backed by up to US$400 million and potentially 4,000 jobs. Analysts expect sub-assemblies and precision components before full tool assembly, given India’s limited advanced-tool customer base and supply chain, with OSATs likely early customers.

Samsung, SK Hynix say memory crunch could drag on for years despite spending surge

Samsung expects memory supply to remain tight through 2028, while SK Hynix sees constraints extending to 2030 despite substantial investment. Factory construction, cleanrooms, equipment delays, rising costs and increasingly complex HBM products will limit near-term bit growth. SK Hynix has contracted more than half of 2027–2028 capacity with 14 customers and considers production through 2029 effectively sold out. Samsung and SK Hynix say higher prices are already pressuring smartphones and PCs.

SK Hynix's next bet goes beyond memory chips

SK Hynix launched SK Hynix Ventures to invest across AI computing, data centers, system software and optical interconnects, extending its strategy beyond supplying HBM. The fund’s size and initial investments remain undisclosed, while its earlier portfolio includes Avicena, Eliyan, Etched and Fabric8Labs. The company is also pursuing AI Co., customized memory and high-bandwidth flash. Its next priorities and organizational responsibilities remain uncertain pending restructuring and management changes.

Server CPU shipments to exceed 50 million units in 2027; AMD's server CPU revenue will surpass Intel

DIGITIMES projects global server CPU shipments will reach 52.6 million units in 2027, up 45.7%, driven by Agentic AI, AI infrastructure, cloud providers’ in-house processors and replacement demand. Arm shipments are expected to grow faster than x86, although x86 remains substantial. Intel is projected to lead shipments with 39% share, while AMD reaches 30.4%. AMD server CPU revenue may surpass Intel, reaching US$31.2 billion versus US$28.7 billion.

Tamil Nadu adds independent chip subsidy route as Indian states vie for investment

Tamil Nadu introduced a Semiconductor Ecosystem Scheme allowing eligible projects to receive state support even without Semicon 2.0 selection. The program offers fixed-capital subsidies of 15% to 25% and turnover-linked subsidies of up to 2% annually for five years, covering materials, testing, equipment and components. Analysts see opportunities in specialty semiconductors and suppliers, but warn that competing states could encourage subsidy shopping unless incentives include performance targets and clawbacks.

[TITLE]Bank of Japan says inflation hit 2% target after 13 years:
[TEXT]
The Bank of Japan said today that inflation had hit its 2% target, more than 13 years after it adopted the goal in a bid to pull the economy out of deflation, but held off hiking interest rates for now.

Successive governments struggled for decades to pull the country out of a spiral of deflation - sustained falls in prices that are harmful for economic growth - and the 2% goal was set in 2013 as the Bank of Japan launched massive monetary easing measures.

While core consumer inflation intermittently topped the level in the early 2020s, today marked the first time that the central bank confirmed the long-term price trend had been achieved.

[TITLE]CCI clears Xavier Niel's 5.1 bn euro Vodafone stake acquisition, 3 deals:
[TEXT]
The Competition Commission of India (CCI) on Friday approved four major corporate transactions, headlined by French telecom billionaire Xavier Niel's 5.1 billion euro acquisition of a 16.2 per cent stake in UK-based telecommunications major Vodafone Group PLC.

The Vodafone transaction is being executed through Vega SAS, a French holding company and subsidiary of Maya SAS, which serves as Niel's family investment vehicle.

Under an agreement first announced in July, Vega SAS is purchasing the 16.2 per cent stake from Emirates Telecommunications Group Co PJSC. Upon completion, Vega SAS will emerge as the single largest shareholder in Vodafone Group.

Although Vega SAS does not have direct business operations in India, the transaction required mandatory review by the Indian competition regulator because of Vodafone's substantial domestic presence through its joint venture, Vodafone Idea Ltd.

Under Indian competition laws, global acquisitions crossing specified financial or ownership thresholds require CCI clearance to ensure they do not adversely affect competition in domestic markets.

The investment comes as Vodafone Group undergoes a major corporate restructuring across its European and African operations. The company has recently divested its Spanish and Italian businesses and completed a major network merger in the UK.

Alongside the global telecom transaction, the CCI approved three domestic deals spanning financial technology, renewable energy and power infrastructure.

In the fintech sector, the regulator approved the acquisition of a stake in Navi Ltd by MIH Payments Holdings BV.

The acquirer is an indirect wholly owned subsidiary of global investment group Prosus NV and functions as an investment holding vehicle with portfolio companies operating in India.

Navi Ltd is the holding company of the Navi Group, which offers consumer-facing financial products and services through its subsidiaries via a unified mobile application.

In the energy sector, the CCI approved Aditya Birla Renewables' acquisition of Solenergi Power from Shell Overseas Investment BV.

Solenergi is a Mauritius-registered holding company with strategic interests in several solar and wind power generation entities across India.

Additionally, the CCI approved Adani Energy Solutions' acquisition of 100 per cent of IntelliSmart Infrastructure from the National Investment and Infrastructure Fund (NIIF) and Energy Efficiency Services Ltd.

Both companies specialise in advanced electricity metering infrastructure, while IntelliSmart also deploys smart gas meters.

The regulator said detailed formal orders for all four transactions would be issued separately.
