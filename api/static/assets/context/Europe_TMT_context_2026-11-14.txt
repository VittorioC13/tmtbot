[TITLE]Chinese e-commerce giant JD.com completes €1.3 billion takeover of German retailer CECONOMY:
[TEXT]
The Chinese e-commerce company acquired 59.8% of CECONOMY's share capital.

Key takeaways

JD.com completed its takeover offer for 59.8% of CECONOMY for total consideration of EUR 1,335 million.

CECONOMY results are consolidated into JD.com’s statements from November 12, 2026.

With Convergenta’s 25.3% stake, the combined shareholding in CECONOMY reaches 85.2%.

JD.com said on Nov. 12, 2026 it completed the JD.com deal for CECONOMY AG, acquiring 59.8% of the share capital and voting rights for total consideration of EUR 1,335 million. CECONOMY becomes a subsidiary of JD.com, which consolidates its financial results into JD.com’s statements from November 12, 2026.

According to the release, JD.com’s partner Convergenta retained a 25.3% stake, bringing the combined shareholding in CECONOMY to 85.2%. JD.com made the offer through JINGDONG HOLDING GERMANY GMBH, an indirect wholly-owned subsidiary of JD.com.

JD.com deal: the details

Consideration (cash / stock / assumed debt) EUR 4.60 in cash per share; total consideration of EUR 1,335 million What is acquired or divested 59.8% of the share capital and voting rights of CECONOMY AG (XETRA: CEC) Ownership change (share count, stake %) JD.com 59.8%; Convergenta retains 25.3%; combined 85.2% Closing conditions and timing Completed Nov. 12, 2026 after merger control, foreign investment and EU foreign subsidies clearances

“JD.com and CECONOMY share a common vision to build Europe’s leading next-generation consumer electronics platform. We can now start to implement our vision and bring it to life,” said Sandy Xu, CEO of JD.com.

“Today marks the beginning of a new chapter for CECONOMY. Our partnership with JD.com has become reality and we are ready to move into implementation with full momentum,” said Remko Rijnders, CEO of CECONOMY.

Background to the JD.com deal

JD.com describes itself as a leading supply chain-based technology and service provider listed on NASDAQ as JD and on HKEX as 9618 and 89618. The company said it has opened its technology and infrastructure to partners, brands and other sectors through its Retail as a Service offering. CECONOMY operates more than 1,000 consumer electronics stores in 11 markets under the MediaMarkt, Saturn and MediaWorld brands, alongside online retail. It employs more than 50,000 people and in 2025 had more than 53 million loyalty members and 2.2 billion annual customer contacts.

What this means

According to the release, CECONOMY will remain a stand-alone business in Europe with a local independent technology stack. The companies plan no changes to the workforce, employee agreements and sites. JD.com committed to investing in CECONOMY’s consumer brands and staying true to their heritage, while CECONOMY combines its local expertise with JD.com’s technology and logistics capabilities.

Analysis

Completion gives JD.com a large physical retail presence in Europe, since CECONOMY runs more than 1,000 stores in 11 markets under the MediaMarkt, Saturn and MediaWorld brands. Consolidation from November 12, 2026 means CECONOMY’s results will likely start to appear in JD.com’s reported figures, which may change how holders of JD read the company’s business mix.

[TITLE]SpaceX announces over $74 billion investment in Texas, Arizona supercomputing campuses:
[TEXT]
SpaceX has announced plans to invest more than $74 billion across two artificial intelligence supercomputing campuses in Texas and Arizona.

In a statement issued on Thursday, SpaceX outlined an investment of over $24 billion for a supercomputing facility in Texas's Rio Grande Valley and roughly $50 billion for a separate campus in Cochise County, Arizona.

According to the company, the two projects will rely on distinct power infrastructures.

The Starbase facility in South Texas will generate its own electricity without drawing from the public grid, whereas the Arizona campus will collaborate with local electric cooperatives and could scale up to 6 gigawatts at full buildout.

The Texas development covers approximately 444 acres between Boca Chica Boulevard and the Port of Brownsville, near Brownsville’s landfill.

A Starbase public hearing notice identifies Space Exploration Technologies Corp. as the owner and applicant for the gas-fired power plant and supercomputing facility.

The roughly $24 billion estimate covers initial phases while allowing for future expansion.

SpaceX noted that the Starbase project has already generated over 3,000 construction jobs and is expected to sustain more than 500 permanent positions, alongside up to $220 million in private capital to expand Brownsville's water and wastewater infrastructure.

The Starbase City Commission is scheduled to consider a property tax abatement and economic development agreement for the project on December 9.

In Arizona, the proposed $50 billion campus — which remains subject to county regulatory approvals — is projected to create roughly 5,500 construction jobs and 1,000 permanent roles, utilising dry cooling technology with no planned industrial groundwater consumption.

Cisco (NASDAQ: CSCO) today reported first quarter results for the period ended October 24, 2026. Cisco reported first quarter revenue of $18.5 billion, net income on a generally accepted accounting principles (GAAP) basis of $4.6 billion or $1.14 per share, and non-GAAP net income of $5.6 billion or $1.40 per share.

"In Q1, we delivered the strongest revenue and non-GAAP earnings per share growth rates for Cisco in over 15 years," said Chuck Robbins, chair and CEO of Cisco. "Organizations are turning to Cisco for our trusted, market-defining technology and our expertise to help them scale, secure and optimize their environments for the agentic AI era. We are in the very early stages of a multi-year networking supercycle, and we're confident in our ability to continue capturing the multi-billion-dollar opportunity it represents for Cisco."

"Q1 FY27 was another record quarter for Cisco, with revenue, non-GAAP operating margin, and non-GAAP EPS all exceeding the high end of our guidance ranges. The strength of our Q1 performance gives us confidence to raise our revenue and non-GAAP EPS guidance for FY27," said Mark Patterson, CFO of Cisco. "As our business continues to scale, we are unlocking significant operating leverage which is enabling us to grow earnings much faster than revenue. Looking ahead, we remain focused on delivering durable growth and driving continued capital returns."

Financial Summary

All comparative percentages are on a year-over-year basis unless otherwise noted.

Q1 FY 2027 Highlights

Revenue -- Total revenue was $18.5 billion, up 24%, with product revenue up 32% and services revenue up 1%.

Revenue by geographic segment was: Americas up 28%, EMEA up 18%, and APJC up 21%. Product revenue performance reflected growth in Networking up 43%, Security up 7%, Collaboration up 8%, and Observability up 2%.

Gross Margin -- On a GAAP basis, total gross margin, product gross margin, and services gross margin were 63.7%, 61.8%, and 70.9%, respectively, as compared with 65.5%, 64.5%, and 68.4%, respectively, in the first quarter of fiscal 2026.

Total gross margins by geographic segment were: 64.1% for the Americas, 68.9% for EMEA and 66.5% for APJC.

On a non-GAAP basis, total gross margin, product gross margin, and services gross margin were 65.6%, 63.7%, and 72.9%, respectively, as compared with 68.1%, 67.2%, and 70.7%, respectively, in the first quarter of fiscal 2026.

Operating Expenses -- On a GAAP basis, operating expenses were $6.2 billion, down 3% year over year, and were 33.4% of revenue. Non-GAAP operating expenses were $5.1 billion, up 2%, and were 27.8% of revenue.

Operating Income -- GAAP operating income was $5.6 billion, up 66%, with GAAP operating margin of 30.2%. Non-GAAP operating income was $7.0 billion, up 37%, with non-GAAP operating margin at 37.8%.

Provision for Income Taxes -- The GAAP tax provision rate was 17.2%. The non-GAAP tax provision rate was 18.5%.

Net Income and EPS -- On a GAAP basis, net income was $4.6 billion, an increase of 59%, and EPS was $1.14, an increase of 58%. On a non-GAAP basis, net income was $5.6 billion, an increase of 39%, and EPS was $1.40, an increase of 40%.

Cash Flow from Operating Activities -- $4.4 billion for the first quarter of fiscal 2027, an increase of 36% compared with $3.2 billion for the first quarter of fiscal 2026.

Balance Sheet and Other Financial Highlights

Cash and Cash Equivalents and Investments -- $15.8 billion at the end of the first quarter of fiscal 2027, compared with $15.9 billion at the end of fiscal 2026.

Remaining Performance Obligations (RPO) -- $46.2 billion, up 8% in total. Product RPO was up 7% and services RPO was up 9%.

Deferred Revenue -- $30.1 billion, up 7% in total, with deferred product revenue up 6% and deferred services revenue up 9%.

Capital Allocation -- In the first quarter of fiscal 2027, we returned $3.7 billion to stockholders through share buybacks and dividends. We declared and paid a cash dividend of $0.42 per common share, or $1.7 billion, and repurchased approximately 18 million shares of common stock under our stock repurchase program at an average price of $112.38 per share for an aggregate purchase price of $2.0 billion. The remaining authorized amount for stock repurchases under the program is $6.1 billion with no termination date.

In the first quarter of fiscal 2027, we closed the acquisition of WideField Security Inc., a privately held security company.

[TEXT]
Policies that Shaped Modern India By Madan Sabnavis Published by Rupa 208 pages ₹695

This book chronicles pivotal policies that have shaped India since Independence, covering fiscal and monetary policy, foreign direct investment, competition law, the Green and White Revolutions, forex, telecommunications, nationalisation, capital market reforms, and liberalisation. However, policies concerning social sectors such as education, health care, vaccination, Swachh Bharat, unemployment, the precarious nature of state finances, and the prospects of emerging sectors such as aerospace and defence are either absent or given insufficient attention relative to their impact on national progress. Consequently, it is difficult to agree with the author’s assertion that “policies covered in the book are the ones that have had a long-lasting impact on the economy”.

The book largely presents a faithful, at times laudatory, narrative of past and present policies. The critical analysis expected for a work of this genre is notably lacking. Readers would anticipate more in-depth analytical insights and suggestions for addressing the gaps and shortcomings identified through such analyses. Among the well-presented points is the observation regarding the innocuousness of the 14th Finance Commission’s increase in devolution to states to 42 per cent in 2015, from the previous 32 per cent. Funds were devolved, but related programmes were also transferred, rendering the increase largely optical. This effect was exacerbated by a substantial increase in central government collections through non-divisible cesses, which rose from ₹6,212 crore in 2006-07 to ₹3.87 trillion in 2024-25.

The discussion on goods and services tax (GST) should have been more comprehensive, given its significance as one of the most substantial changes in the past decade. There is an undue focus on a single or fewer rates, despite the European Union employing numerous nuanced rates even for single products, such as automobiles and hotels, with constituent host governments routinely adjusting rates by 0.5-1 per cent annually through national budgets. The author’s contention that GST has failed to reduce inflation or promote growth appears contentious.

The foundations of India’s financial systems were laid by the Chakravarty Committee in the mid-1980s and the Narasimham Committee-I in the 1990s. Many of their recommendations regarding systems, macro-prudential measures, governance mechanisms, limits on the cash reserve and statutory liquidity ratios, key interest spreads, and the reach of priority-sector lending continue to shape current practice, even as some recommendations await full implementation. The Monetary Policy Committee receives less attention than it deserves, considering its permanent status and the potential suboptimality of its claimed key rate (4 per cent), which reportedly imposes “losses” on the system.

Industrial policies are covered from the draconian measures of the Monopolies and Restricted Trade Practices Act era to modern-day production-linked incentive (PLI) schemes. While PLI is discussed in laudatory terms, the author overlooks its significant skew — the restricted number of targeted sectors and the even more uneven distribution of benefits.

The wave of nationalisation eventually giving way to disinvestment, privatisation, and asset monetisation represents an important aspect of reforms in the financial and industrial sectors. While these receive due attention, the dramatic events surrounding liberalisation in the early 1990s and the impact of foreign direct investment could have been explored more extensively.

India has managed the technological and market transition in capital markets exceptionally well, with its trading and settlement systems and the introduction of new products in futures and options. The rapid evolution of mutual funds as a conduit for savings into capital markets has been remarkable. The success in futures and options could have been replicated in agriculture, enabling farmers to plan their cropping decisions based on market-specific futures prices rather than relying on all-India minimum support prices (MSPs). However, repeated interference in futures trading across several commodities has hindered its replication in commodities trading. This also demands solutions for utilising the extensive infrastructure built for MSP procurement and the public distribution system.

Beyond the rather truncated coverage of policies, some arguments presented may not resonate with all readers. Chapter 13 discusses areas where policies did not work as intended but confines itself to just two: demonetisation and the farm laws. The foiled attempts to alter land acquisition laws and the slow implementation of the labour Codes also merited significant attention. Subsidies for water, electricity, and MSPs without quality differentiation, along with direct benefit transfers for livelihood protection at a single rate across all states, should have received much more scrutiny.

It is difficult to agree with the author’s contention that equilibrium would be difficult without speculators, given that buyers would want the lowest prices and sellers the highest. The laudatory claim that the rural employment guarantee programme has set a benchmark for enabling workers to know what to ask for strains credulity in a country like India.

This book serves as a quick and convenient reference for anyone wishing to refresh their memory of India’s key policies. With its non-controversial narration and lack of overt ideological bias, its depth may appeal to the mass market. However, analysts, commentators, and even civil services aspirants would need to supplement their understanding with other works on this subject, several of which have been reviewed in this paper.

[TITLE]SpaceX announces over $74 billion investment in Texas, Arizona supercomputing campuses:
[TEXT]
SpaceX has announced plans to invest more than $74 billion across two artificial intelligence supercomputing campuses in Texas and Arizona.

In a statement issued on Thursday, SpaceX outlined an investment of over $24 billion for a supercomputing facility in Texas's Rio Grande Valley and roughly $50 billion for a separate campus in Cochise County, Arizona.

According to the company, the two projects will rely on distinct power infrastructures.

The Starbase facility in South Texas will generate its own electricity without drawing from the public grid, whereas the Arizona campus will collaborate with local electric cooperatives and could scale up to 6 gigawatts at full buildout.

The Texas development covers approximately 444 acres between Boca Chica Boulevard and the Port of Brownsville, near Brownsville’s landfill.

A Starbase public hearing notice identifies Space Exploration Technologies Corp. as the owner and applicant for the gas-fired power plant and supercomputing facility.

The roughly $24 billion estimate covers initial phases while allowing for future expansion.

SpaceX noted that the Starbase project has already generated over 3,000 construction jobs and is expected to sustain more than 500 permanent positions, alongside up to $220 million in private capital to expand Brownsville's water and wastewater infrastructure.

The Starbase City Commission is scheduled to consider a property tax abatement and economic development agreement for the project on December 9.

In Arizona, the proposed $50 billion campus — which remains subject to county regulatory approvals — is projected to create roughly 5,500 construction jobs and 1,000 permanent roles, utilising dry cooling technology with no planned industrial groundwater consumption.

"As our business continues to scale, we are unlocking significant operating leverage which is enabling us to grow earnings much faster than revenue. Looking ahead, we remain focused on delivering durable growth and driving continued capital returns."
