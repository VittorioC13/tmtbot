[TITLE]AI Will Not Make You Rich:
[TEXT]
Click here to subscribe to print for your office or home.

Fortunes are made by entrepreneurs and investors when revolutionary technologies enable waves of innovative, investable companies. Think of the railroad, the Bessemer process, electric power, the internal combustion engine, or the microprocessor—each of which, like a stray spark in a fireworks factory, set off decades of follow-on innovations, permeated every part of society, and catapulted a new set of inventors and investors into power, influence, and wealth.

Yet some technological innovations, though societally transformative, generate little in the way of new wealth; instead, they reinforce the status quo. Fifteen years before the microprocessor, another revolutionary idea, shipping containerization, arrived at a less propitious time, when technological advancement was a Red Queen’s race, and inventors and investors were left no better off for non-stop running.

Anyone who invests in the new new thing must answer two questions: First, how much value will this innovation create? And second, who will capture it? Information and communication technology (ICT) was a revolution whose value was captured by startups and led to thousands of newly rich founders, employees, and investors. In contrast, shipping containerization was a revolution whose value was spread so thin that in the end, it made only a single founder temporarily rich and only a single investor a little bit richer.

Is generative AI more like the former or the latter? Will it be the basis of many future industrial fortunes, or a net loser for the investment community as a whole, with a few zero-sum winners here and there?

There are ways to make money investing in the fruits of AI, but they will depend on assuming the latter—that it is once again a less propitious time for inventors and investors, that AI model builders and application companies will eventually compete each other into an oligopoly, and that the gains from AI will accrue not to its builders but to customers. A lot of the money pouring into AI is therefore being invested in the wrong places, and aside from a couple of lucky early investors, those who make money will be the ones with the foresight to get out early.

The microprocessor was revolutionary, but the people who invented it at Intel in 1971 did not see it that way—they just wanted to avoid designing desktop calculator chipsets from scratch every time. But outsiders realized they could use the microprocessor to build their own personal computers, and enthusiasts did. Thousands of tinkerers found configurations and uses that Intel never dreamed of. This distributed and permissionless invention kicked off a “great surge of development,” as the economist Carlota Perez calls it, triggered by technology but driven by economic and societal forces.[1]

There was no real demand for personal computers in the early 1970s; they were expensive toys. But the experimenters laid the technical groundwork and built a community. Then, around 1975, a step-change in the cost of microprocessors made the personal computer market viable. The Intel 8080 had an initial list price of $360 ($2,300 in today’s dollars). MITS could barely turn a profit on its Altair at a bulk price of $75 each ($490 today). But when MOS Technologies started selling its 6502 for $25 ($150 today), Steve Wozniak could afford to build a prototype Apple. The 6502 and the similarly priced Zilog Z80 forced Intel’s prices down. The nascent PC community started spawning entrepreneurs and a score of companies appeared, each with a slightly different product.

You couldn’t have known in the mid-1970s that the PC (and PC-like products, such as ATMs, POS terminals, smartphones, etc.) would revolutionize everything. While Steve Jobs was telling investors that every household would someday have a personal computer (a wild underestimate, as it turned out), others questioned the need for personal computers at all. As late as 1979, Apple’s ads didn’t tell you what a personal computer could do—it asked what you did with it.[2] The established computer manufacturers (IBM, HP, DEC) had no interest in a product their customers weren’t asking for. Nobody “needed” a computer, and so PCs weren’t bought—they were sold. Flashy startups like Apple and Sinclair used hype to get noticed, while companies with footholds in consumer electronics like Atari, Commodore, and Tandy/RadioShack used strong retail connections to put their PCs in front of potential customers.

[TITLE]Kalshi Adds 36 More Stock Perpetual Futures, From TSMC and ASML to Goldman Sachs:
[TEXT]
Kalshi has added 36 more single-stock perpetual futures to its U.S. exchange, and this batch reaches deep into the chip supply chain. Taiwan Semiconductor, ASML, SK hynix, Arm, KLA and Lam Research are all on the list.

In a certification letter filed with the Commodity Futures Trading Commission dated Nov. 17, KalshiEX LLC said it intends to start listing the contracts on Nov. 18, on a continuous basis. The products are perpetual security futures, meaning they never expire.

The filing also lays out how fast the product line has grown. With this batch, Kalshi’s single-stock perpetual lineup reaches 148 contracts in just over two weeks.

Three Batches in 16 Days

According to the letter, the CFTC approved Kalshi’s first 58 perpetual security futures products on Nov. 2, after the exchange submitted them for review on Sept. 18. We covered that decision when the CFTC cleared stock perps earlier this month.

ADVERTISEMENT

Kalshi then self-certified 54 more on Nov. 4. The Nov. 17 filing adds 36, using the faster self-certification route under CFTC Regulations 40.2(a) and 41.23(a). Under that route, an exchange certifies that a product complies with the law rather than waiting for agency approval.

Single-stock perpetual security futures products approved or certified for KalshiEX by date. Running total calculated by DCForecasts. Data: KalshiEX LLC certification letter to the CFTC, Nov. 17, 2026. Retrieved Nov. 18, 2026.

The letter says the new contracts have the same terms as the earlier ones in every respect except the underlying stock. Kalshi’s New Product Committee determined that each stock meets the listing standards in Chapter 14 of its rulebook, which has been filed with the Securities and Exchange Commission.

That dual oversight is part of the design. Kalshi is notice-registered with the SEC as a national securities exchange for security futures, so both regulators have a hand in these products.

Which Stocks Made the List

The schedule includes 33 common stocks and three American depositary receipts. The ADRs are Arm Holdings, SK hynix and Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing.

Semiconductors and chip equipment stand out. Besides the three ADRs, the batch covers ASML, Analog Devices, KLA, Lam Research, Amphenol, Arista Networks and Dell.

ADVERTISEMENT

Wall Street names are well represented too. Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley, Citigroup, Wells Fargo, BlackRock, Charles Schwab and American Express are all included.

The rest span health care (Abbott, Danaher, Thermo Fisher, Intuitive Surgical, Gilead), industrials (GE Aerospace, GE Vernova, Deere, RTX, Union Pacific) and other sectors, including Accenture, CrowdStrike, Shopify, Linde, NextEra Energy, Philip Morris, TJX, T-Mobile US and Welltower.

By our count, 24 of the underlying shares have their primary listing on the NYSE and 12 on Nasdaq.

How the Contracts Work

Each contract covers 100 shares of its underlying stock, with a minimum tick of $0.005 and a tick value of $0.50. Traders do not need to buy a full contract. The minimum order is one five-thousandth of a contract, which equals 0.02 of a share.

In practice, the smallest position is worth about 2% of one share’s price. That is the same minimum unit Kalshi used for the 54 contracts it certified on Nov. 4.

Instead of an expiry date, the contracts use a once-daily funding payment to stay close to the stock price. Kalshi measures the gap between the contract and an underlying price index minute by minute during the regular U.S. stock session. If the contract trades at a premium, longs pay shorts. If it trades at a discount, shorts pay longs.

Trading runs from 6 p.m. ET on Sunday to 5 p.m. ET on Friday, with a one-hour daily maintenance break from 5 p.m. to 6 p.m. ET. That means the contracts can trade overnight on weekdays, when the stock market itself is closed. Settlement is in cash, and no shares change hands.

A Built-In Leverage Cap

The most important number in the filing may be 15.50%. That is the minimum margin Kalshi requires on each position, measured against its current market value. The exchange can set it higher.

Federal security futures rules already require at least 15% customer margin. Kalshi’s floor sits slightly above that, which caps leverage at roughly 6.45 times. Leverage is not user-selectable, according to the filing.

Maximum leverage implied by minimum margin (1 divided by the margin ratio), compared with the up-to-20x figure for offshore HIP-3 stock perps cited in the filing. Calculated by DCForecasts. Data: KalshiEX LLC certification letter to the CFTC, Nov. 17, 2026. Retrieved Nov. 18, 2026.

Kalshi contrasts this with venues outside U.S. rules. Its filing notes that independent builders on Hyperliquid deploy single-stock perpetuals under the HIP-3 standard, introduced in November 2025, with leverage of up to 20 times.

Citing a comment letter from the Hyperliquid Policy Center, the filing says HIP-3 markets recorded more than $480 billion in notional volume in their first ten months, with about $4 billion in open interest.

Crypto’s Product Goes Mainstream

Perpetual futures were invented in crypto, and for years Bitcoin traders were their main users. Kalshi’s filing even cites academic work on hedging with leverage on Bitcoin perpetuals.

Now the same structure is being applied to chipmakers and Wall Street banks inside a regulated, centrally cleared market. We see that as a strong endorsement of a design that the Bitcoin market tested at scale first.

It also connects back to Bitcoin holders. As we reported, Kalshi Prime now lets institutions margin Kalshi perpetual futures with Bitcoin held at Anchorage. Every new contract on the exchange widens the menu that Bitcoin-backed collateral can support.

The race is not limited to Kalshi. The CFTC recently cleared Backpack’s blockchain exchange for U.S. perpetual futures, and rival Polymarket US has certified new oil and gas contracts.

What to Watch

The filing notes that any stock not on the schedule must be submitted to the CFTC before listing. Kalshi has filed three batches in 16 days, so more additions would not be a surprise.

The bigger test is volume. Offshore crypto-native venues already have a head start in stock perps. Kalshi is betting that tighter margin, central clearing and oversight from both the CFTC and SEC will draw traders who want the nearly round-the-clock perpetual format inside U.S. rules.

If it works, the trading tool that Bitcoin popularized will have become a standard part of the U.S. stock market.

Chase Bank, Bank of America, and Apple services also faced brief disruptions in recent days. And on Tuesday, some internal systems at the U.S. National Security Agency (NSA) went down.

Given the outages occurred on such a massive scale and in quick succession, questions have been raised as to whether they were a result of nefarious activities. But there is currently no evidence that the recent incidents were due to cyberattacks or that they were linked.

The Microsoft outage was the result of a combination of factors, including a “latent bug” from July that caused servers to become “overwhelmed and unresponsive,” Charles Lamanna, Microsoft’s executive vice president of Copilot, agents, and platform, said in a post on X. Lamanna said a “surge in load” and decreased server capacity had also contributed to the outage.

The FAA is still assessing what led to the ground stop, which lasted less than five hours, but a preliminary investigation indicated that the technical problem was caused by the installation of a router at a radio facility in Arkansas that may have flooded its communications network. A radar site in Amarillo, Texas, is also being looked at as part of the inquiry.

The short-lived Chase Bank outage on Monday, which led to issues with processing transactions and seemingly had a ripple effect that caused problems for customers at other banks, was the result of a “software bug in on-premise hardware systems,” the bank said in a post on X, adding that “there is no evidence this is cyber-related.” Apple said the outage it experienced on Tuesday, which lasted hours and disrupted the Apple Store and other services, was the result of an “internal update” and not due to a cyberattack.

The NSA disruption, which reportedly caused the agency’s internal communications network for highly classified information, NSANet, to go offline, had a similarly prosaic cause. “The National Security Agency experienced temporary degradation during scheduled routine maintenance,” the agency said in a statement. “While some internal systems were affected, mission-critical capabilities remain fully operational and our ability to defend national security continues. NSA’s infrastructure is designed with multiple layers of redundancy to maintain operations during planned maintenance or unexpected events.”

The Trump administration has acknowledged the unease the outages have caused among the public while emphasizing that there are currently no indications that any were tied to a cyberattack or coordinated by outside actors.

“I understand the timing of these incidents can raise questions, and it should. The American people should ask us these questions,” Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton told the House Homeland Security Committee on Wednesday. “But based on what we know today, after a great deal of digital diligence, these are separate service outages associated with maintenance and technical changes. They are not a coordinated or state-sponsored attack.”

Secretary of State Marco Rubio on Tuesday recognized that people are “fearful” and “uncertain,” but he was unequivocal: “We are not under systemic attack,” he said. “These events—one has nothing to do with the other. They are not interrelated.”
