[TITLE]Marvell Technology reportedly in talks to buy Lumentum in all-stock AI deal:
[TEXT]
The chipmaker could gain control of laser and optics technology that is sold out for years, but Breakingviews warns the price may outrun the payoff

Marvell Technology is reportedly in advanced talks to acquire Lumentum Holdings, the laser and optical components maker whose products help shuttle data around AI data centers. Bloomberg reported the discussions on Wednesday, October 28, 2026. It said an all-stock deal could be announced as soon as the week of November 3.

What’s on the table

Lumentum’s market capitalization is estimated at roughly $83 to $85 billion. Marvell itself is valued somewhere between $196 and $221 billion, based on figures at the time of the report.

The strategic logic is fairly clean. Marvell makes digital signal processors, the chips that clean up and interpret signals moving through high-speed networks. Lumentum makes the lasers and optics that turn those signals into light and send them across fiber.

A sold-out supplier with a famous shareholder

Lumentum is not a company struggling to find customers. Earlier in October, CEO Michael Hurlston said key optical products are sold out until early 2029. He also pointed to a significant supply shortfall for certain products expected by 2027.

Lumentum also has a notable backer. Nvidia owned a $2 billion stake in Lumentum as of March 2026 and is a customer of the company.

Investors split the difference, badly for Marvell

The initial market reaction followed a familiar script. Lumentum shares climbed by 9% to 14% after the news. Marvell shares fell by 8% to 14% in after-hours trading.

[TITLE]Apple reportedly agreed $1B annual deal with Google for Gemini technology access:
[TEXT]
The iPhone maker is leaning harder on Google's custom chips to build its AI models, extending a partnership that started in 2024

Apple is scaling up pre-training of its foundation models on Google’s Tensor Processing Units. That puts some of the most expensive work in Apple’s AI strategy on chips designed by one of its biggest rivals.

What Apple is actually doing

Pre-training is the heavy-lifting phase of building a large language model. That phase eats compute, which makes the choice of hardware a strategic decision rather than a procurement detail.

Apple’s choice is Google’s TPUs. These are custom chips Google built specifically for machine learning workloads, rather than general-purpose graphics processors adapted for AI.

Apple’s first AFM server model was trained on a cluster of 8,192 TPU v4 chips.

The on-device model, the smaller one designed to run locally on Apple hardware, was built on a 2,048-chip slice of TPU v5p chips.

The performance gains were not trivial. The move gave Apple more than 2x the floating point operations per second, or FLOPS, along with triple the high bandwidth memory.

The chips could do far more math per second and hold far more data close at hand while doing it. The result was training time for large language models cut by up to 2.8 times.

How Apple ended up here

The story starts in July 2024. That’s when Apple revealed it had trained its initial Apple Foundation Models, known as AFM, on Google Cloud TPUs instead of the NVIDIA GPUs most of the industry was using.

Availability was part of the reasoning. The shift came amid NVIDIA H100 supply issues, combined with what the research describes as the superior performance and cost-effectiveness of Google’s TPUs for Apple’s needs.

In January 2026, Apple entered a multiyear agreement with Google for access to Gemini technology, a deal reportedly worth $1 billion annually.

Then, in June 2026, Apple announced its third-generation AFM models. That release marked custom development work done with Google.

Not everything runs on TPUs, though. One of the third-generation models, AFM 3 Cloud Pro, operates on NVIDIA Blackwell GPUs inside Google Cloud.

Apple has also been consistent on one point throughout. The company maintains that no private user data was used to train its foundation models, and it says it honors publisher opt-out preferences.

What this means

The most immediate takeaway is that Google has become a load-bearing pillar of Apple’s AI effort. Between TPU-based training, Google Cloud infrastructure and the Gemini licensing deal, Apple’s AI roadmap now runs through Mountain View in several places at once.

AFM 3 Cloud Pro running on Blackwell GPUs shows Apple is still buying into NVIDIA’s ecosystem, just through Google Cloud rather than as a direct anchor customer for its training runs.

[TITLE]Goldman Sachs eyes Tmap Mobility acquisition by year-end:
[TEXT]
Goldman Sachs is seeking to acquire a controlling stake in Tmap Mobility, operator of Korea’s leading navigation app, from SK Square by the end of this year, seeing an investment opportunity in its lower valuation and vast trove of mobility data as autonomous driving advances, industry sources said Friday.

For SK Square, SK Group’s investment arm and the largest shareholder of SK hynix, the sale is part of a broader strategy to focus on artificial intelligence (AI) and semiconductors.

The global investment bank is looking to invest between 600 billion won ($450 million) and 800 billion won to secure a 60 to 70 percent stake in Tmap Mobility. It is in talks to buy shares from SK Square, the largest shareholder with a 63.23 percent stake, as well as other financial investors, including Affirma Capital and EastBridge Partners, which each hold 9.83 percent, and KB Kookmin Bank, which owns 6.39 percent.

Tmap Mobility traces its roots to a navigation service launched by SK Telecom in 2002. The telecom operator spun off its mobility business into a separate company in December 2020, and Tmap Mobility became a subsidiary of SK Square the following year. Its navigation app now has more than 15 million monthly active users, while the company’s offerings extend beyond smartphone navigation to AI-powered mobility solutions.

Its navigation system is also integrated into vehicles from most major imported automakers in Korea, including Mercedes-Benz, BMW, Volvo and Tesla.

Industry officials said Goldman Sachs appears to be betting on Tmap's extensive driving and location data accumulated over more than two decades. The data could become valuable as the mobility industry embraces AI and moves closer to autonomous driving.

The data generated by the mobility platform's user base could help develop new AI-powered services and autonomous driving technologies.

The U.S. investment bank appears to see value in Tmap's future growth prospects.

Tmap Mobility recorded revenue of 283.5 billion won and an operating loss of 14.1 billion won last year, but posted its first annual net profit of 25 billion won. It also recorded its first monthly operating profit in June.

These developments come amid Goldman Sachs' growing interest in Asia's mobility sector.

In 2023, it invested 10 billion yen in GO, Japan's leading taxi-hailing platform. Earlier this year, Goldman also joined a VIG Partners-led consortium that explored acquiring a stake in Kakao Mobility, the operator of Korea's largest ride-hailing platform. The discussions, however, did not lead to a deal.

"Goldman Sachs' interest in Asia's mobility market has grown rapidly, drawing on its expertise in IT and AI. An acquisition could give Tmap Mobility greater opportunities to develop new businesses and expand into overseas markets," an investment banking industry official said.

In addition, Tmap Mobility’s valuation has declined from previous levels amid delays to its initial public offering and growing competition, making it a more attractive investment target for Goldman Sachs.

Goldman Sachs declined to comment.

SK Square, meanwhile, is expected to retain around a 10 percent stake in Tmap Mobility after the deal, remaining its second-largest shareholder and continuing to collaborate on AI and data services.

"We are reviewing various options for Tmap Mobility's future growth and will provide details once they are finalized," an SK Square official said.

The potential deal is also drawing attention from the investment banking industry, as it would mark Goldman Sachs' return to Korea's buyout market after nearly a decade.

The bank's last major buyout in Korea was in 2016, when it acquired cosmetics maker Carver Korea alongside Bain Capital and kitchenware company Happycall with EastBridge Partners. It has since focused largely on minority investments, as it reorganized its private equity business and renewed its push into the Asia-Pacific market.

[TITLE]Where to invest: PL Wealth bets on Midcap, flexicap, banks; cautions on IT:
[TEXT]
Investors looking to allocate money to mutual funds should consider flexi-cap, multi-cap, mid-cap, large-and-mid-cap and banking and financial services funds, according to PL Wealth’s September 2026 Market Outlook. The wealth manager also favours infrastructure and defence-themed funds, while maintaining a cautious stance on IT sector funds amid concerns over artificial intelligence-led disruption and slower revenue growth.

On the debt side, PL Wealth prefers liquid, overnight, money market, low-duration and short-duration funds, arguing that investors should prioritise interest income over making aggressive bets on falling interest rates.

The recommendations come against a backdrop of strong domestic economic growth but rising inflation risks. Elevated crude oil prices, a deficient monsoon and the possibility of a strengthening El Niño could put pressure on food prices and corporate margins. PL Wealth has flagged the possibility of the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) raising policy rates by 25-50 basis points in the third or fourth quarter of FY27.

Despite these near-term risks, the firm's long-term outlook on Indian equities remains positive, supported by domestic investment, earnings growth and relatively attractive market valuations.

Which mutual fund categories does PL Wealth favour?

In its fund category positioning guide, PL Wealth has an overweight stance on five equity fund categories:

Flexi-cap and multi-cap funds: The firm sees these as a way to participate in opportunities across market capitalisations, with an emphasis on banks, capital goods and metals.

Large-and-mid-cap funds: These offer exposure to both established large companies and mid-sized businesses with growth potential.

Mid-cap funds: PL Wealth points to stronger earnings growth and the performance of the Nifty Midcap 150 as reasons for its preference.

Banking and financial services funds: The outlook is supported by strong bank credit growth, liquidity conditions and expectations of earnings growth in the financial sector.

Infrastructure and defence thematic funds: The firm sees opportunities from defence indigenisation, infrastructure spending, power transmission, renewables and data centres.

The wealth manager has a neutral stance on large-cap funds, recommending selective active exposure rather than passive index strategies. It is underweight on IT and technology sector funds, citing artificial intelligence-related disruption, margin concerns and slower revenue growth.

The report's preferences are category-level views and do not amount to recommendations to buy specific mutual fund schemes.

Nifty target 27,123: Is there room for further gains?

PL Research has set a 12-month base-case target of 27,123 for the Nifty, implying an upside of around 12% from the index level of 24,208 used in its August 26, 2026 analysis.

The target is based on a valuation multiple of 17.6 times estimated FY28 earnings per share of ₹1,537.

The report also outlines a bull-case target of 30,137 and a bear-case target of 24,971, reflecting the range of possible market outcomes.

At 24,208, the Nifty was trading at 17.3 times its one-year forward earnings, an 11.7% discount to its 15-year average price-to-earnings multiple of 19.6 times. It was also trading at a 14.8% discount to its 10-year average multiple of 20.3 times.

PL Wealth believes this valuation gap provides some support to the medium- and long-term outlook. However, it recommends staggered investments rather than aggressive lump-sum exposure in the near term.

The difference in performance between market segments is another factor informing its strategy. As of August 31, 2026, the Nifty Midcap 150 had gained 13.4% over one year, while the Nifty 50 had declined 1.4%.

Banks, defence and capital goods among preferred sectors

PL Wealth's sector preferences reflect its expectation that domestic investment and infrastructure spending will remain important drivers of corporate earnings.

The firm's model portfolio is overweight on banks, capital goods and defence, diversified financials, metals, healthcare, telecom and ports.

Within banking and financial services, it expects credit growth and liquidity conditions to support business activity. Non-food bank credit growth stood at 19.1% year-on-year as of July 31, 2026.

Capital goods and defence are supported by India's investment cycle, including spending on infrastructure, power, renewables, data centres, semiconductors and defence manufacturing.

The report is more cautious on IT services, consumer companies and automobiles. It flags artificial intelligence-related disruption and slower revenue growth in IT, while higher input costs and moderation in growth could affect consumer and auto companies.

Debt mutual funds: Why PL Wealth prefers short duration

For fixed-income investors, the central recommendation is to focus on accrual rather than duration.

Accrual strategies aim to earn returns through interest income from debt instruments. Duration strategies, in contrast, seek to benefit from changes in bond prices when interest rates move.

PL Wealth prefers the short end of the yield curve, particularly investments with maturities of three months to three years.

The report cites yields of around 7-7.5% on certificates of deposit and 7.3-7.8% on commercial papers. It also favours liquid, overnight, money market, ultra-short-duration and low-duration funds for investors looking to park money or maintain liquidity.

Within debt mutual funds, the positioning guide gives an overweight stance to:

Overnight, liquid and money market funds

Low-duration and short-duration funds

Banking and PSU debt funds, restricted to high-quality AAA-rated or sovereign instruments

The report maintains a neutral stance on corporate bond and medium-duration funds, favouring selective exposure to AA+ and higher-rated instruments.

It is also neutral on gilt and long-duration funds. PL Wealth believes that taking an aggressive long-duration position may be premature because of the possibility of RBI rate hikes in the second half of FY27. It suggests dynamic bond funds for investors seeking long-duration exposure, as these funds can actively adjust portfolio duration.

Why El Niño and crude oil could change the market outlook

Inflation is one of the biggest risks identified in the report.

India's cumulative monsoon deficit stood at 13% as of August 26, 2026, with 16-17 meteorological subdivisions reporting deficient rainfall. Kharif sowing was 2% below normal, with rice and oilseeds among the crops most affected.

PL Research cited a 70% probability of a full-season drought according to Skymet, with a projected rainfall deficit of 15%. A strengthening El Niño could push up prices of agricultural commodities such as sugar, palm oil, coffee, cocoa and soybean.

Crude oil adds to the inflation concern. Brent crude averaged $90-93 per barrel in August 2026. According to the report, every $10-per-barrel increase in crude prices could add around 30 basis points to headline inflation.

The firm also warns that companies carrying 45-60 days of imported raw material inventory purchased at elevated prices could face margin pressure in the second and third quarters of FY27.

Against this backdrop, PL Research has flagged a 25-50 basis-point rate hike risk in the second half of FY27, depending on the movement in crude prices and monsoon conditions.

Gold and silver: What does the report expect?

PL Wealth expects gold to remain range-bound in the near term, with a projected range of $3,900-$4,400 per ounce. Central bank buying is expected to support prices, although changes in US monetary policy and geopolitical developments could increase volatility.

Silver was trading around $58-59 per ounce at the end of July. The report identifies demand from green technology industries and a physical market deficit as supportive factors. However, it says a sustained rally would require stronger inflows into silver exchange-traded funds.

What should investors take away?
