[TITLE]Marvell Technology reportedly in talks to buy Lumentum in all-stock AI deal:
[TEXT]
The chipmaker could gain control of laser and optics technology that is sold out for years, but Breakingviews warns the price may outrun the payoff

Marvell Technology is reportedly in advanced talks to acquire Lumentum Holdings, the laser and optical components maker whose products help shuttle data around AI data centers. Bloomberg reported the discussions on Wednesday, October 28, 2026. It said an all-stock deal could be announced as soon as the week of November 3.

What’s on the table

Lumentum’s market capitalization is estimated at roughly $83 to $85 billion. Marvell itself is valued somewhere between $196 and $221 billion, based on figures at the time of the report.

The strategic logic is fairly clean. Marvell makes digital signal processors, the chips that clean up and interpret signals moving through high-speed networks. Lumentum makes the lasers and optics that turn those signals into light and send them across fiber.

A sold-out supplier with a famous shareholder

Lumentum is not a company struggling to find customers. Earlier in October, CEO Michael Hurlston said key optical products are sold out until early 2029. He also pointed to a significant supply shortfall for certain products expected by 2027.

Lumentum also has a notable backer. Nvidia owned a $2 billion stake in Lumentum as of March 2026 and is a customer of the company.

Investors split the difference, badly for Marvell

The initial market reaction followed a familiar script. Lumentum shares climbed by 9% to 14% after the news. Marvell shares fell by 8% to 14% in after-hours trading.

Reuters Breakingviews made that case explicitly. The commentary argued that Marvell may have found an expensive shortcut in the race to move AI data at light speed. It said the potential acquisition may cost too much and deliver inadequate returns.

Marvell’s shopping habit

The bear case is what Breakingviews flagged. When a target is already priced for years of sold-out demand, much of that good news is baked into the valuation. The acquirer pays for future growth upfront and must then outperform those expectations just to break even on the investment.

[TITLE]Apple reportedly agreed $1B annual deal with Google for Gemini technology access:
[TEXT]
The iPhone maker is leaning harder on Google's custom chips to build its AI models, extending a partnership that started in 2024

Apple is scaling up pre-training of its foundation models on Google’s Tensor Processing Units. That puts some of the most expensive work in Apple’s AI strategy on chips designed by one of its biggest rivals.

What Apple is actually doing

Pre-training is the heavy-lifting phase of building a large language model. That phase eats compute, which makes the choice of hardware a strategic decision rather than a procurement detail.

Apple’s choice is Google’s TPUs. These are custom chips Google built specifically for machine learning workloads, rather than general-purpose graphics processors adapted for AI.

Apple’s first AFM server model was trained on a cluster of 8,192 TPU v4 chips.

The on-device model, the smaller one designed to run locally on Apple hardware, was built on a 2,048-chip slice of TPU v5p chips.

The performance gains were not trivial. The move gave Apple more than 2x the floating point operations per second, or FLOPS, along with triple the high bandwidth memory.

The chips could do far more math per second and hold far more data close at hand while doing it. The result was training time for large language models cut by up to 2.8 times.

How Apple ended up here

The story starts in July 2024. That’s when Apple revealed it had trained its initial Apple Foundation Models, known as AFM, on Google Cloud TPUs instead of the NVIDIA GPUs most of the industry was using.

Availability was part of the reasoning. The shift came amid NVIDIA H100 supply issues, combined with what the research describes as the superior performance and cost-effectiveness of Google’s TPUs for Apple’s needs.

In January 2026, Apple entered a multiyear agreement with Google for access to Gemini technology, a deal reportedly worth $1 billion annually.

Then, in June 2026, Apple announced its third-generation AFM models. That release marked custom development work done with Google.

Not everything runs on TPUs, though. One of the third-generation models, AFM 3 Cloud Pro, operates on NVIDIA Blackwell GPUs inside Google Cloud.

Apple has also been consistent on one point throughout. The company maintains that no private user data was used to train its foundation models, and it says it honors publisher opt-out preferences.

What this means

The most immediate takeaway is that Google has become a load-bearing pillar of Apple’s AI effort. Between TPU-based training, Google Cloud infrastructure and the Gemini licensing deal, Apple’s AI roadmap now runs through Mountain View in several places at once.

AFM 3 Cloud Pro running on Blackwell GPUs shows Apple is still buying into NVIDIA’s ecosystem, just through Google Cloud rather than as a direct anchor customer for its training runs.
