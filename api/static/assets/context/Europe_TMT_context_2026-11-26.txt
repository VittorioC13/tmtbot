[TEXT]
Thomas Dohmke, the former chief executive officer (CEO) of open-source platform GitHub, said there will be more open-source models going forward because of economic pressure and the models themselves becoming better. "They may not be catching up to frontier models, but they are reaching a quality level that makes them a viable substitute for many tasks. So, we're going to see that kind of shift, and then we're going to have software factories—where you can build certain things just by telling a factory of coding and review agents,” he told Business Standard in an interaction. According to Dohmke, open-source models lag in capital investment, pre-training, and data labelling because they lack the team strength and large clusters of graphics processing units (GPUs) needed for training, forcing them to rely on synthetic data. However, their advantages lie in lower costs, customisation for specific use cases, and better support for languages where frontier models fall short.

The debate over frontier and open-source models has been on the rise. Model orchestration has become an important topic in the artificial intelligence (AI) world as enterprises have found out that random use of tokens hardly improves efficiency or productivity. It, on the other hand, only burns the annual AI budget quicker than anticipated. That is leading them to use a variety of models—large, small, or open-source—according to the activity, which is often more effective and helps rein in costs.

Dohmke, who led GitHub for four years, stepped down last year to start his own startup Entire, which is a developer platform focused on agent-to-human collaboration. The company raised $60 million in seed funding earlier this year.

Unlike GitHub, which is designed for human-to-human collaboration, Entire is built for a world where millions of lines of code are written by agents and reviewed by humans in the loop.

“What we have seen in the last six months is that more and more code are coming from agents. Not only the code, but all the summaries, comments, and descriptions are also coming from agents,” Dohmke said.

According to him, code generation is now the responsibility of AI agents rather than human engineers, shifting the software engineer's role to orchestrating these specialised agents. This orchestration framework relies on two core components: a decentralised infrastructure layer that maintains global data residency for geopolitical and scaling needs, and a semantic layer that stores session logs and agent traces alongside the repository code.

That is why Entire launched repositories in the US, Europe, and Australia before putting India on its map. “Now we're excited to be here to also talk with many individual developers to be able to create the repository from the start on Entire in India,” he said.

For that, Entire has tied up with sovereign model company Sarvam. This will make Entire India’s first agent-native Git forge with data residency, storing code locally, while Sarvam serves inference for agent runs.

“With Entire and Sarvam, an engineering team can host its code in India and run its agents on models we train and serve here. For enterprises that need their code to stay in India, that opens up agent-assisted development even for their most sensitive repositories,” Sarvam cofounder Vivek Raghavan said in a statement.

AI Factories Need an Intelligent Data Layer

AI infrastructure is rapidly expanding beyond hyperscalers as NeoCloud providers, Sovereign AI initiatives, research institutions, and enterprises build dedicated AI Factories to support a growing wave of AI applications and agents.

AI Factories use GPUs to transform data into tokens that power AI models and agents. While GPUs are often the focal point of these investments, they create value only when continuously supplied with the right data and context at the required performance. Although storage typically represents a modest percentage of overall AI infrastructure spending, its impact on AI Factory efficiency is disproportionate. As AI Factories scale, efficient access to data becomes as important as compute. This requires a persistent data layer that can ingest, organize, move, protect, and serve data while maintaining security, resilience, and cost efficiency.

AI Factories are evolving beyond model training and experimentation toward large-scale inference and integration into business and consumer applications. This shift increases the importance of delivering timely access to enterprise data and maintaining context across interactions. Newer technologies such as retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), Key-Value (KV) cache offload, and frameworks such as llm-d help AI agents reduce token costs, preserve context, and operate more efficiently over extended periods. Industry initiatives such as the NVIDIA AI Data Platform recognize this evolution by combining accelerated computing with intelligent data services designed to make enterprise data more accessible and usable for AI applications and agentic workflows.

As AI adoption grows, the data layer is becoming a strategic component of the AI Factory, directly influencing utilization, efficiency, and business outcomes.

AI Factory Data Layer Requirements

AI Factories require a data layer that delivers:

High Performance: Consistent, sustained throughput and low latency across ingestion, training, inference, and analytics.

Cost-Efficient Scale: Performance, capacity, context memory, and archive capabilities without creating data silos.

Independent Scaling: Capacity and performance that can scale with AI infrastructure growth.

Multi-Tenancy: Secure isolation and resource governance across shared infrastructure.

Data Sovereignty and Resilience: Control over data location, access, protection, and recovery for regulated and mission-critical environments.

Operational Simplicity: Automation, observability, and lifecycle management at scale.

These requirements increasingly translate into a tiered data architecture, with different data tiers supporting the path from persistent data to accelerated compute.

The AI Factory Data Tiers

Figure: AI Factory data tiers interactions.

Modern AI Factories are increasingly adopting this tiered data architecture that aligns storage resources with the performance, persistence, and data locality requirements of AI workloads.

Tier 0 Data Acceleration leverages local NVMe storage within GPU-based compute servers to accelerate access to hot datasets, active checkpoints, and temporary training data.

Tier 1 High-Performance Active Data serves as the shared data foundation for AI training, fine-tuning, inference, and High-Performance Computing (HPC) workloads. This tier stores active models, datasets, checkpoints, and other frequently accessed AI data that must be shared across clusters, users, and applications.

Tier 2 Persistent AI Data Lake provides long-term storage for enterprise data, curated datasets, model repositories, and AI-generated content. This tier acts as the system of record for AI initiatives and supplies data to higher-performance tiers as required.

Tier 3 Cyber-Resilient Protection delivers backup, recovery, archive, and compliance services to protect data assets throughout the AI lifecycle.

Context Memory Mapping (CMX / KV Cache)

A key emerging concept in AI infrastructure is Context Memory, which enables the preservation and reuse of inference state, reducing the need to repeatedly regenerate context during AI inference. Unlike the storage tiers above, Context Memory is not a standalone storage tier. Instead, it is a logical service that can leverage resources across both Tier 0 and Tier 1.

NVIDIA's Context Memory Exchange (CMX) architecture allows inference context to extend beyond expensive GPU memory into lower-cost storage tiers while remaining accessible to inference frameworks. IBM Storage Scale KV Cache Offload can leverage high-performance storage resources to persist and reuse inference context, helping improve GPU utilization and reduce inference costs. In the above picture, Context Memory can span both:

Tier 0 Data Acceleration for ultra-low-latency access to active inference contexts and hot KV cache data.

Tier 1 High-Performance Active Data for larger-scale context persistence, sharing, and reuse across inference clusters.

This approach positions Context Memory as a cross-tier AI data service rather than a separate storage tier, aligning with emerging industry architectures while leveraging existing storage investments.

IBM Storage Scale based AI Factory data layer implementation supports implementation of all the above tiers and enables building of cost efficient, optimally performing and scalable data platform.

Key Differentiators of IBM Storage for AI Factories

Traditional storage architectures were designed to manage data. Modern AI infrastructure requires a data layer that can also help accelerate AI workloads and simplify operations across increasingly complex, large and distributed environments.

IBM's approach to the AI data layer is built around two core principles: accelerating AI workloads, and operating AI Factories at enterprise scale.

Accelerating AI Workloads

AI infrastructure performance is ultimately measured by how effectively organizations can transform data into AI outcomes. As accelerated computing environments continue to grow in scale and complexity, the ability to efficiently move, access, and process relevant data becomes critical to maximizing infrastructure utilization. IBM Storage incorporates technologies designed to reduce bottlenecks between storage and accelerated computing environments while delivering AI-ready data.

GPU Direct Storage (GDS)

Direct GPU-to-storage data movement helps reduce latency and minimize unnecessary CPU and memory overhead, enabling faster access to the required datasets. IBM Storage not only supports GPU Direct Storage (GDS) with Portable Operating System Interface (POSIX) protocol but also with S3 protocol to support applications that depend on file or S3 object storage. GDS can be leveraged for all types of AI workloads from training, inferencing and analytics.

Context Memory and KV Cache Offload

As inference and agentic AI workloads scale, context memory is emerging as an important part of the AI Factory data architecture, complementing accelerator memory and high-performance active data. Key-Value (KV) cache is one important example of this emerging data class. IBM Storage Scale extends KV cache beyond GPU memory to high-performance shared storage, accelerating long-context AI inference while enabling cache reuse across nodes. Validated testing demonstrated up to 56× faster Time-to-First-Token and 22× higher inference throughput, while delivering performance comparable to KV cache in local GPU memory (Ref). Data Acceleration Tier

As AI Factories scale to thousands of accelerators, Tier 1 storage must simultaneously support model training, checkpointing, inference pipelines, metadata operations, and shared access to massive datasets. IBM Storage Scale Data Acceleration Tier enables frequently accessed datasets to be staged within this Tier 0 layer while preserving a single global namespace across the environment. This allows organizations to improve storage efficiency, reduce data access latency, and maximize GPU utilization. IBM Storage Scale supports S3 over Remote Direct Memory Access (RDMA), enabling object-storage access with near-NVMe latencies while reducing CPU and network-stack overhead. Content Aware Storage

IBM Content-Aware Storage (CAS) turns enterprise storage into an active participant in RAG and agentic AI workflows by keeping vectorized context synchronized with changes in source data. By bringing content discovery, enrichment, and retrieval closer to where data resides, CAS helps applications access relevant and up-to-date enterprise context while reducing unnecessary data movement. IBM Fusion Data Catalog complements CAS with metadata-driven discovery to help AI workflows identify the data they need.

IBM Storage Scale is proven in production environments operating at exascale under a single namespace, demonstrating the scale and operational maturity required for mission-critical AI, HPC, analytics, and scientific computing workloads. As accelerators and AI models continue to evolve, the durable competitive advantage will be the ability to securely deliver the right data to the right compute, at the right performance and cost.
