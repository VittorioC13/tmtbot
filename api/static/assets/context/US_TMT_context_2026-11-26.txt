While Amazon has declined to disclose the deal's financial terms or comment on its value, Bloomberg News reported that the deal is worth around $7.5 billion.

Founded in 2023, Decart has been developing software to improve the efficiency of AI workloads across different hardware platforms, including chips from Nvidia, Amazon and Google. The company has been working with Amazon since 2025 to improve the performance of its Trainium processors.

The acquisition is significant because one of the biggest obstacles to Amazon's ambition of becoming a serious AI-chip supplier has not necessarily been the hardware itself, but the software ecosystem surrounding it.

What is the problem Amazon wishes to solve? The advantage that Nvidia has in artificial intelligence extends well beyond the performance of its graphics processing units (GPUs).

Its CUDA software platform has become deeply embedded in the workflows of AI researchers, developers and enterprises.

With its years of investment in software libraries, development tools, and optimized applications, Nvidia has become the default hardware choice for a large portion of the AI industry.

Even if an alternative processor offers attractive price-performance characteristics, customers will have to consider the engineering effort required to migrate their existing workloads, adapt their software, and optimize their models for a new architecture.

This is where Amazon's Trainium chips come in. They are designed to address this challenge by offering customers an alternative for training and running AI models through AWS.

But Amazon needs to demonstrate that the performance and cost benefits are substantial enough to justify switching, and this is exactly where Decart will be a strategic tool for the company.

Founded in 2023, the startup offers a software engine called Decart Optimization Stack (DOS), which helps companies run AI systems faster and switch more easily between a range of AI chips, including those from Nvidia, Amazon and Google.

For Amazon, this technology could make Trainium more competitive without requiring the company to depend entirely on increasingly expensive investments in additional computing capacity.

Trainium is already attracting large customers Amazon has spent years developing its custom silicon chips through Annapurna Labs, which it had acquired in 2015. Anthropic has been using AWS infrastructure to train and serve its Claude models. According to AWS, nearly one million Trainium2 chips are being used for Anthropic's workloads associated with Project Rainier. Decart itself, Karakuri, and Poolside are some of the other Trainium customers.

Amazon is not replacing Nvidia completely Despite this push towards its own custom chips, Amazon is not replacing Nvidia completely, and only in August this year, AWS and Nvidia announced an expanded partnership that includes plans to deploy two million additional Nvidia GPUs across AWS infrastructure.

Amazon wants to offer customers a broader choice of computing platforms while developing its own chips as a competitive alternative.

This is also where Decart's technology becomes particularly interesting.

Since its optimisation stack was designed to work across different chip architectures, it could help Amazon develop software capabilities that are not limited to a single hardware platform.

Amazon announced Monday that it has signed an agreement to acquire proprietary technology developed by Israeli artificial intelligence startup Decart and bring the company's three co-founders and its team of researchers and engineers into Amazon.

The transaction is valued at $7.5 billion, according to Israeli financial newspaper Calcalist. Amazon did not disclose the financial terms of the agreement in its official announcement.

The deal covers the technology behind Decart's Optimization Stack (DOS), which is designed to improve the efficiency and performance of AI computing systems.

Decart's three co-founders, Dean Leitersdorf, Moshe Shalev, and Orian Leitersdorf, will join Amazon alongside the company's AI researchers and engineers.

The Israeli startup was founded in 2023 and has developed artificial intelligence technologies, including Lucy, a model that allows users to edit live video in real time, and Oasis, which generates interactive virtual environments.

It was reported in October that Amazon was negotiating a potential acquisition of Decart for approximately $7 billion.

According to Calcalist, Decart previously held acquisition discussions with Nvidia and Anthropic. The latter advanced negotiations for a transaction valued at approximately $6 billion but ultimately withdrew from the proposed deal.

Amazon said it began collaborating with Decart in 2025, with the Israeli company's expertise in optimizing software and hardware performance contributing to efforts to improve the efficiency of Amazon's custom AI chips.

The technology is expected to support Amazon's development of its Trainium processors and improve AI inference, enabling customers to run artificial intelligence models more efficiently and at lower costs.

"More efficient compute means better training, better reinforcement learning, faster inference, and better models for customers at a lower cost. That's the pillar Trainium and our AI efforts are built on, and from our earliest work with Decart, we knew they were the kind of technologically fearless team who would help us push it even further."

"From day one, our mission has been to redefine the boundaries of what AI can do and to make it dramatically more efficient along the way," Leitersdorf said.

"Joining Amazon gives our team the scale, the customers, and the infrastructure to pursue that mission faster than we could on our own. We're looking forward to exploring what we can build together."

In May, Decart raised $300 million in a funding round that valued the company at approximately $4 billion. The round included participation from Nvidia, and the startup has raised more than $450 million since its establishment.

According to Calcalist, the agreement with Amazon includes a combination of cash and stock, with payments spread over several installments. The founders have reportedly committed to remaining with Amazon for five years.
