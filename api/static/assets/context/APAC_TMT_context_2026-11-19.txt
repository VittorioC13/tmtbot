Mumbai: Has Indian IT turned the corner?After surging more than 17% in October, the Nifty IT index appears to have lost momentum, declining nearly 2% so far in November, although the Sensex and Nifty harnessed new-found enthusiasm from foreign funds to climb more than 10% in the run-up to Diwali and its immediate aftermath. Legacy IT stocks have stagnated despite a precipitous plunge in AI-heavy bets across Asia, putting a question mark over whether earnings concerns at outsourcing firms would continue to keep prices - and valuation multiples - in restraint despite obvious opportunities arising from a sectoral churn.Since the beginning of October, South Korea's Kospi and Taiwan's stock index have fallen around 20% and 13%, respectively. Japan's Nikkei has declined 10%, while the Nasdaq Composite has lost 6%. The Shanghai Composite and Hang Seng have fallen 4.4% and 2.4%, respectively, reflecting the correction in global AI-related stocks.The September-quarter earnings of Indian IT companies, meanwhile, point to an uneven recovery, with mid-tier players outperforming larger peers while underlying demand conditions remain largely unchanged. Choice Institutional Equities said companies under its coverage reported average sequential revenue growth of around 5.4% for tier-2 players, compared with nearly 1.1% for tier-1 companies.Coforge led the mid-tier pack with 10.9% growth, followed by Persistent Systems at 7.8%, while Tech Mahindra and HCLTech led among larger companies.However, healthy deal bookings have yet to translate into commensurate revenue growth. Phased transitions, staggered project ramp-ups and longer client decision-making cycles continue to delay revenue conversion.Shorter-cycle AI engagements, typically lasting one to two quarters, are also limiting the visibility traditionally associated with large, multi-year deals, Choice said.A key concern for the sector is the changing economics of technology services as clients increasingly use AI to improve productivity and reduce costs. While AI is creating new business opportunities, these gains are yet to fully offset pricing pressure in traditional services.Kotak Institutional Equities estimates gross deflation of around 7% and net deflation of 3.5% for companies as clients seek a greater share of productivity savings through contract renewals.Sandeep Gogia, MD and co-head, Investment Banking, Equirus Capital, said pressure on enterprise clients to improve returns on AI-related investments, alongside concerns around safety, security and token-cost optimisation, could create fresh opportunities for IT services providers. "Integration of systems of records and data systems with systems of intelligence and AI models is not plug and play," Gogia said, pointing to opportunities in data engineering, application modernisation, cloud migration and cybersecurity as enterprises move towards large-scale AI implementation.On profitability, Choice said operational efficiency and cost discipline had partly offset wage and delivery-related pressures, although margin performance remained mixed. Kotak expects margins at large IT companies to remain largely stable, with rupee depreciation providing some support. However, pricing pressure and AI-related investments could weigh on profitability.The earnings outlook also remains uneven. Kotak expects Infosys to lower its FY27 revenue growth guidance to 1.5-2.5%.

[TITLE]Asian stocks sink as scandal-hit Nvidia prepares for earnings:
[TEXT]
Tech firms dragged Asian stock markets down again Tuesday as the fallout from the Nvidia crisis continued to rattle trading floors, with US moves to tighten restrictions on Chinese access to chips adding to uncertainty.

The selloff comes as investors brace for the release of earnings from the Wall Street titan, which comes days after boss Jensen Huang resigned over revelations of extramarital affairs, including with a woman suspected of being a Chinese spy.

Also in focus is the initial public offering of Claude-creator Anthropic, which is being seen as a test of confidence in the AI trade, which has been whipsawed in recent months by concerns over companies’ eye-watering AI investments and their profitability.

Markets have been battered since the crisis broke at Nvidia, which is at the centre of a buildout in artificial intelligence that has helped propel global markets to record highs and boosted economic growth around the world.

The tech-heavy Nasdaq led steep losses Monday on Wall Street as traders continued to assess the latest developments.

And Asia again followed suit, with Tokyo, Seoul and Taipei — home to the region’s biggest AI-linked firms — again taking the brunt of the selling, with all three down at least one percent.

There were also big losses in Hong Kong, Shanghai, Sydney, Singapore and Wellington.

Nvidia’s earnings will be closely watched after the market closes later Tuesday, with investors looking for signs of whether the AI boom can withstand the widening scandal.

Matt Weller at Forex.com said: “Data centre demand and guidance will be central.

“Investors will look for evidence that hyperscalers and other customers are sustaining their spending on AI infrastructure, and whether fourth-quarter guidance points to further growth.”

Donald Trump on Monday backed calls from some of Silicon Valley’s most powerful voices pushing for the swift return of Huang, with the US president downplaying national security concerns over the possible spy links.

However, Nvidia’s board is not considering reinstating Huang and is instead focused on an investigation into his conduct, CNBC reported Monday, citing a person familiar with the matter.

Adding to the downbeat mood was news that the US Commerce Department moved on Monday to close a loophole that allowed Chinese companies to access Nvidia’s powerful AI chips remotely.

A new rule will force data centre operators in Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia and Brazil to get a licence from the United States allowing them to ship high-end AI chips from Nvidia and other American firms to clients in those countries.

“The proposed rules are aimed at limiting the remote use or diversion of cutting-edge US chips and extend the regulatory overhang facing the semiconductor industry,” said National Australia Bank’s Rodrigo Catril.

“The news also raises questions about the outlook for global AI infrastructure investment, particularly in jurisdictions that have become important hubs for data centre development.”

Anthropic is expected to raise around $65 billion from its IPO, which could value the five-year-old start-up at $1.4 trillion, making it one of the world’s most valuable companies.

The firm is expected to begin trading in New York on Thursday, and observers said plenty of attention will be paid to its performance in light of the Nvidia crisis as well as concerns about funding and profitability.

[TITLE]Stocks tumble on Wall Street as Nvidia leads another sell-off in technology companies:
[TEXT]
Trader Robert Charmak, left, works on the floor of the New York Stock Exchange in New York, Monday, Nov. 9, 2026. (AP Photo/Yuki Iwamura)

HONG KONG – More drops for stocks in the artificial intelligence industry dragged the U.S. market lower on Monday, led by Nvidia.

The weakness for tech stocks helped knock the S&P 500 index 1.9% lower and pushed the Nasdaq composite index to a 2.5% loss. The Dow also fell sharply as losses broadened into other sectors. AI-focused companies were by far the biggest weights on the market and have been behind much of the recent turbulence on Wall Street.

Recommended Videos

Nvidia sank 4.1% and was again the heaviest weight on Wall Street following its crisis of leadership. The company has lost $735 billion in market value since the scandal erupted about CEO Jensen Huang’s infidelities and his ties to an alleged Chinese spy.

Nvidia is one of the most influential companies in the world not only because its chips are powering much of the world’s move into AI technology but also because its cash has helped companies across the industry to grow.

Now, following Huang’s resignation as Nvidia’s CEO last week, both the chip company and the broad industry are facing a more uncertain future without the leather-jacketed man who became one of AI’s biggest celebrities.

It’s the latest challenge for the AI industry, which had already been shaken by criticism that stock prices in the industry shot too high and were in a bubble. The sweeping victories for Democrats in this month’s U.S. midterm elections also raised worries about possible restrictions on further construction of AI data centers.

“The market was already worried about this risk before what happened at Nvidia,” Zhiwei Zhang, president and chief economist at Pinpoint Asset Management, said in a commentary. “Now the risk has become even more serious.”

The sector is also now facing more scrutiny from the U.S. government. The Trump administration on Monday issued new rules to restrict remote access to advanced U.S. computing chips and servers.

Outside of Nvidia, other chip companies also fell. They had climbed in the earlier days of Nvidia’s fall on hopes that they could gain a competitive advantage. But Broadcom dropped 3.9% and notched its second straight loss on concerns about the broader industry. Advanced Micro Devices fell 5.6%.

CoreWeave, which buys Nvidia chips and then rents out access to them to customers over the cloud, sank 6.1%. GE Vernova, which is helping to power AI data centers, fell 4.9%.

The next big milestone for the AI industry may arrive Tuesday after trading in the U.S. stock market ends for the day. That’s when Nvidia will report its latest quarterly results, and interim CEO Colette Kress is set to speak to Wall Street. Investors have become accustomed to Nvidia not only reporting stronger results than analysts expected for each quarter but also giving a forecast for upcoming revenue that’s well above expectations.

Elsewhere on Wall Street, L3Harris Technologies fell 3.9% after officials in President Donald Trump’s administration suggested the contractor for the Federal Aviation Administration may be at fault for a nationwide ground stop Friday. L3Harris said Saturday that it’s supporting the FAA in its investigation.

Summit Therapeutics sank 16.5% after the biopharmaceutical company said the U.S. Food and Drug Administration indicated it cannot approve its application for a therapy to treat certain patients with lung cancer.

Tyson Foods rose 8.7% after reporting a stronger profit for the latest quarter than analysts expected. The company behind the Jimmy Dean and Hillshire Farm brands, though, also reported weaker revenue than expected after selling less beef than it did a year earlier.

Overall, the S&P 500 shed 145.08 points to close at 7,416.59, marking the third straight loss for the benchmark index. The Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 739.38 points, or 1.4%, to 51,608.17. The Nasdaq composite fell 626.52 points to 24,509.76.

The recent swings for AI stocks have overshadowed what's been another strong earnings reporting season for U.S. companies. The big businesses in the S&P 500 index are on track to deliver earnings per share for the summer nearly 32% higher than a year earlier, according to FactSet.

It's the third straight quarter of growth above 25% for the index, which is important because corporate profits are one of the two big levers that set stock prices over the long term, along with interest rates.

In the bond market, Treasury yields eased back following last week's jump due to a worse-than-expected report on U.S. inflation.

The yield on the 10-year Treasury fell to 5.13% from 5.15% late Friday, though it remains well above a low of 4.80% reached earlier this month. Higher yields make borrowing money more expensive for everyone, and they’ve already hurt the housing industry through expensive mortgage rates.

Yields fell as oil prices sank, which took away some of the upward pressure on inflation. The price for a barrel of Brent crude oil fell 3.5% to $92.44. It’s been yo-yoing amid uncertainty about what will happen in the war with Iran.

In stock markets abroad, indexes were mixed in Europe.
