[TITLE]Anduril in talks to raise funding at $100B valuation, signaling defense tech’s gravitational pull on venture capital:
[TEXT]
Palmer Luckey's defense AI firm could nearly double its valuation just two months after closing a $5 billion round

Anduril Industries is reportedly in early discussions to raise a new funding round at a valuation of approximately $100 billion, according to Reuters. If the deal materializes, it would mark one of the fastest valuation jumps in private company history, nearly doubling the $61 billion figure the company achieved just two months ago.

For context, $100 billion is roughly where AMD trades publicly. Anduril builds AI-powered autonomous defense systems. It was founded in 2017.

## The valuation staircase

In June 2025, the company raised $2.5 billion at a $30.5 billion valuation, with Founders Fund contributing its largest-ever single investment of $1 billion. Then in May 2026, Anduril closed a $5 billion Series H led by Thrive Capital and a16z, pushing the post-money valuation to $61 billion. That round alone more than doubled the previous price tag.

Now, barely eight weeks later, the company is apparently back at the table looking for more capital at a figure that would represent roughly a 64% premium over the Series H. The company has raised over $6.8 billion in total funding across its various rounds.

Advertisement

The size, timing, and lead investors for this potential new round remain unclear.

## Why this matters beyond defense circles

Anduril reported approximately $2.2 billion in revenue for 2025. At a $100 billion valuation, that implies investors are willing to pay roughly 45 times trailing revenue for a private defense contractor. For comparison, Lockheed Martin and Raytheon, the publicly traded defense giants, typically trade at low single-digit revenue multiples.

Palmer Luckey, who previously founded Oculus VR before selling it to Facebook, built Anduril around a thesis that the US military’s procurement system was broken. Traditional defense contractors spend years and billions on cost-plus contracts. Anduril’s model focuses on building products first, then selling them to the government, more closely resembling a Silicon Valley playbook than a Beltway one.

The firm specializes in AI-driven autonomous systems designed for military applications, aligning its product roadmap with the Pentagon’s stated priorities around modernization and autonomous capabilities.

## What this means for investors watching from the crypto and tech sidelines

Anduril has no crypto tokens, no blockchain integration, and no digital asset strategy that anyone has publicly discussed.

The venture capital dollars flowing into Anduril are the same dollars that could be flowing into crypto infrastructure, DeFi protocols, or Web3 startups. When a single private defense company can absorb $5 billion in a single round and then immediately start shopping for more, it reveals where institutional capital’s priorities currently sit.

If the same names that backed crypto’s biggest rounds, firms like a16z and Founders Fund, continue to double down on defense AI, it could signal a durable reallocation of venture capital that reshapes funding dynamics across technology sectors for years to come.
