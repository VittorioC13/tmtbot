What is the problem Amazon wishes to solve? The advantage that Nvidia has in artificial intelligence extends well beyond the performance of its graphics processing units (GPUs).

The BEA lifted Q3 2026 GDP growth to a 4.3% annual rate from 3.4%, with the revision led by software investment and corporate profits up $206.0 billion.

The Bureau of Economic Analysis revised third-quarter 2026 real GDP growth up 0.9 percentage point to a 4.3 percent annual rate in its second estimate released November 25, and the biggest single lift came from software.

The figure, up from October’s advance estimate of 3.4 percent and above the 3.5 percent Reuters consensus, nearly doubles the second quarter’s 2.2 percent pace. Corporate profits from current production rose $206.0 billion in the quarter, against a $384.0 billion increase in Q2, and current-dollar GDP growth was revised to 6.2 percent from 5.6 percent.

Software led the revision

The BEA said the upgrade “was revised up 0.9 percentage point from the advance estimate, primarily reflecting upward revisions to investment and consumer spending.” The release states that “the revision to investment was led by an upward revision to intellectual property products, notably software, based primarily on revisions to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics’ Producer Price Index for system and application software publishing, excluding maintenance, tech support and games.”

Investment added 3.7 percentage points to the quarter’s growth, consumer spending 2.8 points. Household spending was revised up to 4.2 percent growth from 3.8 percent, and real final sales to private domestic purchasers, which strips out government, inventories and trade, rose 5.8 percent, revised up 0.9 percentage point.

The software investment lift was partly offset by a downward revision to services, led by health care on new Census Quarterly Services Survey data.

Prices cooled alongside the growth

The price index for gross domestic purchases rose 2.7 percent, revised down 0.3 percentage point, a sharp slowdown from 5.6 percent in Q2. The PCE price index rose 2.1 percent and core PCE rose 2.5 percent, both unchanged from the advance estimate. Real gross domestic income rose 4.4 percent, against a revised 2.3 percent in Q2.

The growth was broad: investment, consumer spending and government spending all contributed, partly offset by a decrease in exports, with imports the largest drag at minus 2.6 points.

The BEA’s third estimate of Q3 GDP, with industry-level detail, is due December 23, 2026.

[TITLE]Inside the indium phosphide squeeze and the optical boom — InP remains tightly constrained as the demand from AI puts the entire supply chain under pressure:
[TEXT]
Indium phosphide (InP) is a compound material used in specialized chip-making. Unlike silicon, which is the fundamental building block for mainstream logic and memory chips, InP — made from indium and phosphorus — is mainly used to manufacture highly specialized photonic and high-frequency electronic chips, largely due to its outstanding optical properties. Its immensely expensive and complex manufacturing process also makes it impractical for the large volumes and die sizes needed for logic and memory chips. With the industry predicting a shortfall of this crucial material right as the shift to optical interconnects in data centers begins, we explore the current state of the supply chain.

InP chips have powered high-speed lasers, modulators, photodetectors, and various other optical components used across the telecommunications industry and defense for decades. While the InP supply chain was relatively small and highly specialized, it was generally stable and sufficient to meet global demands.

Then came the AI boom. As AI clusters hit the physical limits of moving enormous amounts of data over copper, the industry began the switch to optical interconnects. First came ever-faster optical transceivers. Now the industry is transitioning to silicon photonics, near-packaged optics, and co-packaged optics, bringing optical links progressively closer to the processors themselves. InP is central to much of this transition, building the critical active optical components needed to turn electrical data into light and back again.

Individual AI data centers are already being built around hundreds of thousands of accelerators, with upcoming gigawatt-scale buildouts expected to house millions. Connecting those processors translates into demand for millions upon millions of indispensable optical components, all relying on the same relatively tiny InP supply chain.

Japan's Sumitomo Electric and U.S.-headquartered AXT together account for almost 80% of global InP substrate manufacturing, with JX Advanced Metals adding roughly another 10%. AXT, however, manufactures most of its InP substrates in China, where exports now require government licenses, further exacerbating the situation. It gets worse: China accounts for around 70% of global refined indium output, which is recovered as a byproduct of processing zinc. Export controls mean that restrictions apply to both the material itself and the finished wafer.

The resulting squeeze is already sending ripples across the industry, with several companies predicting shortfalls. Michael Hurlston, CEO of optical-component manufacturer Lumentum, said in July that the company's InP fabs were shipping more than 30% below customer demand, despite rapid capacity expansion. Coherent also described InP capacity as the “primary constraint” on its transceiver business.

A simple solution would be to expand the supply chain. However, growing large, low-defect InP crystals is difficult, while qualifying new suppliers requires time that doesn't align with the industry's demand. Furthermore, expanding substrate and laser-fabrication capacity can take years. Meanwhile, to secure optical components capacity for its accelerators, Nvidia has invested $2 billion each in Lumentum and Coherent, attaching multibillion-dollar purchase commitments and future capacity rights to both deals. Both companies, along with several others in the supply chain, are frantically pursuing solutions.

What is indium phosphide?

Indium phosphide belongs to the III-V family of compound semiconductors, named after the periodic-table groups of their constituent elements; in InP’s case, indium and phosphorus. Its better-known relatives include gallium arsenide and gallium nitride.

[TITLE]The geopolitics of open-access AI models: Technology, influence and the lobbying battle in Washington:
[TEXT]
'Open Weights and American AI Leadership',

Technology companies find common ground

Washington turns openness into a strategic question

China sees an opportunity in wider access

Europe and India seek technological autonomy

The influence question

The growing push for open-weight artificial intelligence is drawing technology companies and governments into a contest over access, innovation and control. What appears to be a technical debate over how AI models are released is increasingly shaping commercial interests, national security and the balance of technological power.The next contest in artificial intelligence may not be decided solely by who develops the most powerful model. Increasingly, the question is who controls access to it. As technology companies make their models' underlying weights available to developers, governments face a difficult choice: how can wider access encourage innovation and strengthen national competitiveness without exposing sensitive capabilities to misuse?The stakes extend well beyond software development. Open-weight AI is becoming part of a larger argument over industrial policy, corporate influence and geopolitical advantage. A coalition of technology companies, in an open letter namedis pressing Washington to resist premature restrictions, maintaining that wider distribution can stimulate competition, lower barriers to innovation and extend American technological influence. Yet the companies backing this position have different commercial interests, while governments remain divided over the security implications.A distinction is essential. Open-weight models make their trained parameters available for download, enabling developers to run, modify and fine-tune them, subject to licensing conditions. Open-source AI is a broader concept. Under the Open Source Initiative's definition, it requires access to the components and information needed to study, modify and redistribute a system.Publishing model weights, by itself, does not make a model fully open-source.For businesses, the attraction is straightforward. Organisations can deploy open-weight models on their own infrastructure, customise them for specific applications and retain greater control over sensitive information. Universities and smaller developers, meanwhile, gain opportunities to experiment without bearing the full cost of training a model from scratch. Governments can adapt the technology for local languages and public services.But access to a model is not the same as control over the technology behind it. Advanced systems still depend on computing infrastructure, semiconductors, electricity, data and specialised expertise. Those resources remain concentrated among a relatively small group of companies and countries.The corporate campaign acquired greater visibility on July 24, 2026, with the publication of Open Weights and American AI Leadership. Backed by technology companies and industry organisations, the letter brought together players across the AI supply chain, including Nvidia, Microsoft, Meta, Google, Amazon, OpenAI, IBM, AMD and Hugging Face.The signatories make a direct case for wider access. "Open weight models, AI models that anyone can download, inspect, modify, and run on their own infrastructure, are an important part of that foundation," the letter states. It presents open weights as a way to expand participation in the AI economy, encourage competition and give organisations greater control over their data and technology choices.The letter also acknowledges the risks. "Once released, the weights are beyond the original developer's control, and modified versions are difficult to trace or reverse," it says. This is a significant qualification: unlike an API-based service, a model distributed for independent use cannot simply be switched off by its original developer.Its signatories also argue that policymakers should not confuse legitimate model-development techniques with the unauthorised extraction of proprietary technology. On distillation, the letter describes it as "a widely used technique for model improvement, evaluation, and validation". It argues that unlawful extraction should be addressed through targeted legal and commercial frameworks rather than broad restrictions on the technique itself.The coalition's significance lies partly in the different interests it brings together. Nvidia supplies the computing infrastructure on which AI models run. Microsoft has substantial interests in cloud services and enterprise deployment, while Meta has used its Llama models to encourage a wider developer ecosystem. Platforms such as Hugging Face, in turn, benefit from greater participation in model development and distribution.For Nvidia, wider AI adoption can translate into demand for GPUs, servers and networking equipment. Microsoft's commercial interests extend across cloud computing and enterprise services.
