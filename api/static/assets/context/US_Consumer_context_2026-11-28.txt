[TITLE]Miami Investor Buys Fort Lauderdale Retail Center for $35M:
[TEXT]
Core Investment Management has paid $35.4 million for a strip mall in Fort Lauderdale, following a four-year repositioning.

The 73,756-square-foot asset called Gateway Shopping District features 11 buildings between 1914 and 1980 East Sunrise Boulevard. The strip mall is sandwiched between 18th Northeast Avenue and 20th Northeast Avenue, near the 799,851-square-foot Galleria Mall, which is slated to be redeveloped into a mixed-use development.

Gateway Shopping District is just under 90 percent leased, according to a representative for Native Realty, which brokered the sale, though Adam Greenberg, a managing partner at Core, put the figure closer to the low 80s. Tenants include Peppi’s Pizza, Stretch Zone, Barolo and Pulp & Press. The sale excluded the corner building at 1990 East Sunrise Boulevard.

The Miami-based buyer took out a $20 million loan from International Finance Bank.

First developed as a quarry and later as a jungle zoo in 1940, the property was gradually turned into a shopping center starting in the 1950s. In recent years, the center had fallen into disrepair, and in 2022, Native CEO Jaime Sturgis was hired by the separate owners of the retail buildings to rehab and lease up the asset.

“Our job was to listen, build trust and find a path that worked for everyone,” Sturgis said in a statement. “Seeing this property transform from a neglected shopping center into one of the most dynamic retail destinations in Fort Lauderdale, and then closing a sale of this size, is incredibly rewarding.”

Core, which also counts Michael Lapointe as a managing partner, specializes in retail, specifically strip malls and grocery-anchored properties, across Florida. Last year, the investor purchased a Publix-anchored shopping center in Plantation, Fla., for $53 million.

The new Ermenegildo Zegna Group footwear and leather goods plant in Sala Baganza, Italy

[TEXT]
Guinness Nigeria Plc has called for improved manufacturing efficiency, increased local sourcing and more efficient regional trade corridors to strengthen Nigeria’s industrial competitiveness and support long-term economic growth.

The company’s Managing Director, Girish Sharma, made the call at the CNBC Africa Invest in Nigeria Conference held on November 23, 2026, at Eko Hotel, Lagos, according to a statement on Tuesday.

Sharma said manufacturers needed to improve operational efficiency, optimise production and manage costs to remain competitive, particularly as households became more conscious of their spending.

He also highlighted the importance of consumer-led innovation, developing products at accessible price points and working with the formal banking sector to improve access to capital and manage financing costs.

The panel also featured Dr Ayodele Musibau Abioye, Chief Executive Officer of BUA Foods Plc; Ijeoma Taylaur, Executive Director and Chief Operating Officer of the Nigeria Sovereign Investment Authority; and Lynda Saint Nwafor, Chief Enterprise Business Officer at MTN Nigeria.

Drawing on Guinness Nigeria’s experience, Sharma said the company had adapted its global brewing formulations and recipes to local requirements, including the use of locally sourced grains and other agricultural inputs.

He said the approach demonstrated how manufacturers could strengthen domestic supply chains, support local agriculture and create opportunities across the wider value chain.

However, he noted that deeper backward integration would require improved infrastructure, particularly reliable electricity and efficient road networks, to enable small and medium-sized enterprises to participate in industrial supply chains.

On the investment environment, Sharma said long-term capital commitments depended on policy predictability and confidence in the operating environment.

He said, “The number one thing investors want is policy predictability over a long horizon, because every investment decision comes down to a payback calculation.”

He added that consistency in government policies and practical support for businesses would help investors plan, commit capital, and deliver sustainable returns.

Sharma also identified opportunities presented by the African Continental Free Trade Area (AfCFTA), noting that Nigeria was well positioned to strengthen its role as a regional manufacturing and trade hub.

He, however, said this would depend on the effective implementation of existing trade agreements and improved movement of goods across borders.

According to him, addressing practical barriers to cross-border trade and strengthening regional trade corridors would help businesses take advantage of wider markets under AfCFTA.

The importance of a stable investment environment also featured in the contributions of other speakers at the conference.

In her keynote address, the Minister of Industry, Trade and Investment, Dr Jumoke Oduwole, reaffirmed the Federal Government’s commitment to policy consistency and creating an environment that would give businesses and investors confidence to make long-term investment decisions.

She said the ultimate measure of economic growth should be its impact on Nigerians through stronger businesses, better jobs and rising incomes.

Similarly, Olivier Buyoya, IFC Division Director for Nigeria and Central Africa, highlighted the importance of investor confidence in the rules governing the investment environment.

He said, “Investors want to know three things: one, that they can get their money in; two, that they can get their money out; and three, that the rules hold for the length of their investment.”

[TITLE]British SMEs hail government's EU food deal:
[TEXT]
Small and medium food and drink firms across Britain have backed the Government’s historic UK-EU food and drink deal – claiming it will safeguard British jobs and boost trade.

As the Government introduces the landmark Cutting Costs Bill (formally the European Partnership Bill) in Parliament today, SMEs around the UK say the deals he agreed in Brussels last week will cut the red-tape and holding back business.

Last week, supermarket giants, such as Tesco, Morrisons and Marks & Spencer backed the food and drink deal to cut costs, put fresher food on shelves and relieve pressure on prices. But it’s smaller, family-run businesses that have been particularly impacted by complex border checks and delays - and often lack resources to absorb the extra burden.

Businesses across the UK have had to fill in more than a million expensive certificates, just to sell food and drink to our closest neighbours. That’s time spent navigating border checks instead of finding customers – and money spent on paperwork instead of going into wages.

The deal agreed at the UK-EU summit last week will slash back that red tape and lower barriers to selling into Europe.

Sheila Eggleston, chief executive of Eggs-Port, a cattle export firm from Northumberland, said:

Before the SPS checks came into force, we were exporting around 600 pedigree breeding cattle a year to customers across Europe, supporting growing demand for high-quality British breeds such as Aberdeen Angus and Hereford. When the new requirements were introduced, our order book fell from around £1 million to £200,000 almost overnight, with the practicalities and costs of moving live animals making that trade unviable or impossible. The impact has gone beyond our business to the British breeders who supply these animals. We welcome the opportunity to restart exports of cattle, embryos and semen to Europe. We already have £70,000 to £100,000 of orders waiting from customers in France and Spain.

Dairy farmer Tom Rhodes, director of Lancashire cheese producer, Carron Lodge, said:

This agreement is a huge opportunity to get more of our fantastic British cheese onto tables across Europe. Removing these barriers will not only help businesses like Carron Lodge grow, but also create opportunities for the next generation of young farmers, protect rural jobs and give British agriculture the future we deserve.

Meat exporters, like Warrendale Wagyu in East Yorkshire, said last week’s UK-EU agreement, will put British beef back on the map.

Tom Richardson, Warrendale Wagyu managing director, said:

The UK produces some of the finest beef in the world, and ensuring our farmers, processors and exporters can access international markets efficiently is essential to supporting long-term investment, growth and the future of British agriculture.

Environment Secretary Dame Angela Eagle said:

Family run cheese makers and British beef farmers deserve to sell their goods on the biggest stage and not be blocked by excruciating admin. We’ve struck a deal that will kick the door back open to trading in Europe. Whether you are a business of two or 2,000, this is a new era for British business and will give customers a better deal and more choice.

A survey of 600 British small and medium-sized food and drink manufacturers found that 55% of exporters to the EU saw a drop in profits following the introduction of new trading requirements in January 2021.

Around 79% of SMEs thought that removing red tape could unblock exports with the UK’s largest trading partner.

David Knibbs, chief executive of North Yorkshire Tofu producers Tofoo and Co, said:

This food and drink deal with the EU will greatly enhance our ability to export where we have significant opportunities in EU markets like France, Holland, Belgium & Germany.

Will Alex, sales director at Edinburgh based alcohol-free beer firm BRULO, said:

Taking certification, paperwork and border checks out of UK-EU food and drink trade means a brand like ours can spend its time winning customers instead of filling in forms. That’s a real help to SMEs like BRULO, and we welcome the deal. Less red tape makes trade easier, and our products more desirable across Europe, which in turn helps drive growth.

The food and drink sector in Great Britain supports 3.7 million jobs, and Europe is the largest food and drink partner, with 58% of exports.

Last week’s deal, struck by the Prime Minister in Brussels is expected to strengthen food supply chains, reduce costs for businesses and help put downward pressure on food price inflation. It could add up to £5.1bn to the UK economy.

Shop smarter, not just cheaper

This year's sale season is also seeing artificial intelligence play a bigger role in helping consumers make purchasing decisions. A product advertised with a 40% discount may still cost more than the same item on another platform once additional offers are taken into account. ChatGPT can help compare prices across different e-commerce websites and official brand stores while factoring in bank discounts, coupon codes, cashback offers, exchange bonuses and delivery charges.Instead of looking at headline discounts, shoppers can use the AI tool to calculate the effective final price and identify which retailer is offering the best overall value.This can be particularly useful for expensive purchases such as smartphones, laptops and home appliances where even a small difference in pricing can translate into significant savings.Popular products often sell out within hours of a sale going live. However, overall ratings often fail to reveal recurring issues that only emerge after extended use. ChatGPT can help shoppers analyse publicly available customer reviews from different platforms and identify common complaints, durability concerns and recurring problems. By focusing on long-term user experiences rather than promotional claims, shoppers can gain a better understanding of whether a product is truly worth buying.This approach can be especially useful when evaluating electronics, home appliances and other products where reliability matters more than first impressions.Black Friday sales often tempt consumers to spend more than they originally planned. This allows buyers to decide whether the upgrade offers genuine value or simply looks tempting because of the sale price.One of the biggest risks during Black Friday is overspending. Individual purchases may appear inexpensive, but multiple small transactions across several platforms can quickly exceed a budget.
