[TEXT]
I've been bullish on this company for some time, but today's announcement has given me something to think about.

Neuren Pharmaceuticals Ltd (ASX: NEU) shares are having a difficult day on Friday.

The biotechnology company's shares returned from a trading halt this morning and are currently down 11% to around $19, having traded as low as $17.30 at one stage.

As someone who has been bullish on Neuren for some time, would I take advantage of this sell-off?

Image source: Getty Images

Why are Neuren shares crashing?

Investors have been selling Neuren shares following an update on the development of NNZ-2591 as a treatment for Pitt Hopkins syndrome.

Despite proposing two alternative clinical trial designs, Neuren has been unable to reach an agreement with the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) on a Phase 3 study that it believes is feasible.

The issue is that the number of participants required for the FDA's preferred study design is considered too large by Neuren given the rarity of the condition.

Management remains committed to advancing the program and is hopeful that the FDA's new framework for reviewing rare disease treatments could help establish a way forward.

While today's news doesn't mean the end of the Pitt Hopkins syndrome program, it does create greater uncertainty around when, or indeed if, Neuren could bring the treatment to market.

Reasons to remain positive

Importantly, today's announcement does not change the status of Neuren's ongoing Phase 3 trial of NNZ-2591 for Phelan-McDermid syndrome.

Management confirmed that patient enrolment is progressing well and expects to provide guidance on the timing of trial results in the new year.

There is also the company's existing commercial success with DAYBUE, a treatment for Rett syndrome.

Earlier this month, Neuren upgraded its FY26 income guidance from the treatment to between US$91 million and US$93 million, including royalty and milestone payments.

With DAYBUE generating a growing stream of royalty income, Neuren has an established source of funding for its clinical development programs.

That puts it in a financial position that many biotechnology companies would envy.

Would I buy the dip?

My main reservation about buying Neuren shares today is the amount of future earnings growth still reflected in their valuation.

Consensus forecasts point to earnings per share (EPS) of 29 cents in FY26, rising to 57 cents in FY27 and 85 cents in FY28.

Even after today's sell-off, its shares are trading on a price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio of 65 times this year's expected earnings.

The valuation looks much more reasonable at around 22 times forecast FY28 earnings, but I would be careful about placing too much reliance on that figure.

It is possible that analysts could downgrade their earnings estimates to reflect today's news, so these earnings multiples could change once their models are updated.

Foolish takeaway

I remain optimistic about Neuren's future, especially given the performance of DAYBUE and the potential of NNZ-2591 across multiple indications.

However, I think there is enough uncertainty following today's announcement to warrant some patience.

I would prefer to see how analysts adjust their earnings forecasts and valuations before committing money to Neuren shares.

This study demonstrates that thymol can suppress fibroblast proliferation and activation by regulating the E2F8/NF‑κB/TGF‑β1/Smad signaling axis, and thymol exerts selective partial transcriptional reprogramming thereby alleviating inflammation and pulmonary fibrosis in mice. These findings provide a foundational basis for further exploration of thymol as a candidate antifibrotic agent in IPF therapy.

Thymol at concentrations up to 160 μM exhibited no significant cytotoxicity toward normal human lung fibroblasts. Thymol below 160 μM significantly inhibited TGF-β1-induced proliferation of fibroblasts (P < 0.05). Compared to the control group, the TGF-β1-induced group exhibited increased mRNA and protein levels of α-SMA, Collagen I, and Fibronectin, as well as enhanced phosphorylation of p65, Smad2, and Smad3, and elevated E2F8 mRNA expression (P < 0.05). Transcriptomic data revealed that thymol only partially reversed TGF‑β1‑triggered transcriptional alterations, preferentially suppressing myofibroblast‑contractile gene programs rather than broadly abrogating all extracellular‑matrix‑related transcripts. These parameters were significantly reduced in the thymol-treated groups (P < 0.05).

The primary therapeutic target in IPF is the aberrant activation of fibroblasts and the subsequent overproduction of collagen [ 6 ]. Multiple signaling pathways and their downstream effectors have been implicated in the persistent activation of fibroblast populations in IPF. Among pro-fibrotic mediators, transforming growth factor-β1 (TGF-β1) plays a pivotal role [ 8 ], activating the canonical TGF-β/Smad signaling cascade via TGF-β receptors, and contributing critically to fibroblast activation and ECM deposition. Due to the irreversible nature of pulmonary fibrosis, the median survival following diagnosis is only 3–5 years. The method of euthanasia for animals is to anesthetize them first and then dislocate their cervical vertebrae. The NF-κB/TGF-β1/Smad signaling pathway plays a critical role in the activation of fibroblasts. Therefore, we investigated whether thymol could regulate this pathway in activated human lung fibroblasts. Specifically, we examined the effect of thymol on the activation status of the NF-κB/TGF-β1/Smad pathway in HFL1 cells.

ELISA analysis of bronchoalveolar lavage fluid (BALF) showed that TGF-β1 levels were significantly elevated in the BLM group compared to the control group, whereas thymol treatment significantly reduced TGF-β1 expression ( Fig 4C ). Hematological analysis demonstrated that the numbers of white blood cells, neutrophils, and lymphocytes were markedly increased in the BLM group. In contrast, thymol treatment led to a decrease in these cell counts, with white blood cells and lymphocytes showing statistically significant reductions ( Fig 4D – 4F ).

To further evaluate the therapeutic effects of thymol, key parameters including survival rate, body weight, and inflammation levels were assessed. Survival analysis showed a tendency toward increased mortality in the BLM group, while thymol treatment exhibited a tendency to reduce mortality, but the difference was not statistically significant ( Fig 4A ). Body weight measurements indicated that BLM-treated mice experienced significant weight loss, while no notable differences were observed among the other groups ( Fig 4B ).

These findings indicate that thymol effectively mitigates both inflammatory responses and fibrotic remodeling in BLM-induced pulmonary fibrosis in mice. Although the absolute reduction in fibrosis scores is modest, the improvement in alveolar structure and collagen deposition is morphologically evident, and the efficacy is comparable to that of nintedanib.

4. Discussion

Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) is a devastating interstitial lung disease with a poor prognosis. Although antifibrotic agents such as pirfenidone and nintedanib have been approved for clinical use and have demonstrated the ability to delay disease progression to some extent [1], their efficacy in reversing established fibrotic lesions remains limited [14]. For patients in the advanced stages of the disease, lung transplantation continues to be regarded as the only curative option [15], underscoring the urgent need for novel antifibrotic therapeutic strategies.

In recent years, several emerging signaling pathways have been implicated in the pathogenesis and progression of IPF, including TGF-β/Smad, STAT3, and PI3K, which are critically involved in aberrant fibroblast activation and excessive extracellular matrix (ECM) deposition [16,17]. Among these, TGF-β1 is widely recognized as a central mediator of fibrosis. It activates Smad2/3 signaling and promotes their nuclear translocation, thereby regulating the expression of fibrosis-related genes such as those involved in epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT) and collagen synthesis [18]. Animal studies have also demonstrated that Smad3 deficiency significantly attenuates bleomycin (BLM)-induced pulmonary fibrosis in mice [19,20].

Beyond synthetic small-molecule inhibitors, natural product-derived active monomers have garnered increasing interest in recent years for IPF intervention due to their multi-target regulatory potential and relatively low toxicity profiles [21]. Several studies have reported that natural compounds, such as paeoniflorin from Paeonia suffruticosa, gallic acid (a polyphenol), and flavonoids like baicalein, can effectively mitigate fibrotic pathology by inhibiting activation of the TGF-β1/Smad pathway [22–24]. Similarly, active constituents such as myricetin, rutin, and scutellarein have been shown to suppress excessive ECM deposition and the expression of pro-inflammatory mediators [25,26].

In the present study, thymol treatment significantly suppressed the expression of α-SMA and fibronectin in TGF-β1-induced human lung fibroblasts and attenuated the activation of the NF-κB/TGF-β1/Smad signaling pathway. The antifibrotic and anti-inflammatory effects of thymol were further validated in a BLM-induced mouse model. These findings are consistent with previous studies demonstrating the regulatory effects of polyphenolic and flavonoid compounds on fibrotic signaling pathways [27]. The expanded transcriptomic analysis provides a broader context for these targeted findings. TGF-β1 altered 2,349 genes under the prespecified differential-expression thresholds, whereas thymol altered 971 genes relative to TGF-β1, and 306 TGF-β1-responsive genes showed significant opposite-direction changes after thymol treatment. Importantly, the reversed genes included several key contractile/myofibroblast markers, such as ACTA2, MYH11, TAGLN, CNN1, and MYLK. However, the transcriptional response was incomplete: COL1A1 and COL3A1 remained relatively elevated, and several matrix-remodeling genes showed only partial or heterogeneous normalization. These transcriptomic findings are consistent with the experimental observation that thymol reduced α-SMA and fibronectin but did not significantly affect Collagen I, and they support a more precise interpretation in which thymol selectively attenuates fibroblast activation rather than uniformly suppressing all components of extracellular matrix production. GO enrichment analysis further showed that TGF-β1-driven genes were associated with muscle contraction, muscle structure development, tissue/organ morphogenesis, and vascular development, whereas genes reduced by thymol were enriched in MAPK regulation, muscle structure development, organ morphogenesis, and cell motility/migration-related processes. This provides transcriptome-level evidence that the effect of thymol extends beyond individual marker genes and involves coordinated remodeling of fibroblast activation programs. At the same time, thymol-upregulated genes were enriched in mitotic and chromosome-segregation processes, indicating that the treatment response is broader and more complex than a simple reversal of the TGF-β1 state. Therefore, the RNA-seq data should be interpreted as evidence of selective transcriptional reprogramming rather than complete normalization of the fibrotic transcriptome.

This observation reveals an association between E2F8 and the antifibrotic effects of thymol, suggesting that E2F8 may serve as a potential upstream regulator in this process, providing a new perspective on its multi‑target mechanisms [28]. Although thymol’s anti‑inflammatory and antifibrotic properties and the TGF‑β/Smad pathway have been reported, this study is the first to reveal the E2F8–NF‑κB–TGF‑β1/Smad axis as an integrated target network. However, the role of the E2F family in pulmonary fibrosis remains insufficiently characterized, and the function of E2F8 was inferred solely from expression data. To firmly establish the causal link between thymol and the E2F8/NF‑κB/TGF‑β1/Smad axis, future studies will perform rescue experiments using TGF‑β1 inhibitors, NF‑κB inhibitors, and E2F8 knockdown/overexpression in vitro.

Previous studies have established that the NF-κB pathway is persistently activated in chronic inflammation and ECM accumulation, and it interacts synergistically with the TGF-β1/Smad axis to amplify fibrotic responses [29]. For example, astragalus polysaccharides have been reported to inhibit pro-inflammatory cytokine release by blocking the TLR4/NF-κB pathway, thereby delaying fibrosis progression [30]. Similarly, vinpocetine alleviates lung tissue injury and fibrosis by downregulating the NLRP3/NF-κB pathway, illustrating the potential of multi-target therapeutic strategies [31]. In this study, the observed reduction in phosphorylated NF-κB levels following thymol treatment aligns with these findings, further supporting the compound’s capacity to modulate multiple signaling pathways. Notably, the reduction in histological fibrosis scores is moderate, which is common in early‑stage intervention studies of BLM‑induced pulmonary fibrosis; combining quantitative collagen detection will further enhance the reliability of the conclusion.

In addition, the current study mainly used semi-quantitative histological scoring to evaluate fibrosis, which is subjective. However, the antifibrotic effect of thymol was consistently supported by multiple lines of evidence in vitro and in vivo, including down-regulated expression of fibrosis-related genes and proteins (α-SMA, fibronectin), reduced TGF-β1 levels in BALF, and suppressed activation of the NF-κB/TGF-β1/Smad signaling pathway. To further strengthen the objectivity of collagen quantification, a quantitative hydroxyproline assay to measure total lung collagen content will be performed in subsequent studies to provide more solid and reliable evidence for thymol’s antifibrotic effect.

It is important to note, however, that although natural monomers are generally considered to have low toxicity and a reduced risk of resistance [32], this study did not include systematic long-term toxicological or pharmacokinetic evaluations. The effective in vitro concentrations of thymol (80 μM and 160 μM) are relatively high, which may limit direct clinical translation; future studies should explore structural modifications or formulation optimization (e.g., liposomes, inhalable preparations) to improve local bioavailability and reduce effective concentrations. The comparability between the in vitro concentrations and the in vivo doses (60/120 mg/kg) also requires further clarification through pharmacokinetic studies. Moreover, the causal relationship between E2F8 and downstream signaling events has not yet been functionally validated, which is a critical area for future investigation.

In conclusion, this study provides evidence that thymol attenuates fibroblast activation and pulmonary fibrosis through coordinated modulation of inflammatory, contractile, extracellular-matrix, and TGF-β/NF-κB-associated programs. The expanded transcriptomic analysis indicates that this effect represents a selective and partial transcriptional reprogramming rather than complete reversal of the TGF-β1-induced state. E2F8 remains an interesting candidate for further mechanistic investigation, but the current data do not establish it as a causal upstream mediator. Further studies focusing on direct E2F8 perturbation, quantitative collagen assessment, long-term safety, pharmacokinetics, and dose translation are required to define the therapeutic and mechanistic relevance of thymol in IPF more rigorously.
