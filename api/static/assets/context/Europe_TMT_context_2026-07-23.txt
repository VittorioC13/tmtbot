[TITLE]Sila raises $300M to fast-track gigascale anode production and shore up US battery supply chains:
[TEXT]
TL;DR Sila raised $300M to expand its Moses Lake silicon anode plant, pitching US-made battery materials as an alternative to Chinese supply chains.

Sila, the battery materials company formerly known as Sila Nanotechnologies, has raised $300 million in a private equity round led by Atreides Management and Sutter Hill Ventures to expand its silicon anode manufacturing plant in Moses Lake, Washington. 8VC, Bessemer Venture Partners, Matrix Partners, and T Rowe Price also participated, alongside existing investors including In-Q-Tel, the venture arm of the US intelligence community. The round brings total funding to well above $1 billion for a company that has shipped its battery material in a single consumer product, the WHOOP fitness band.

Silicon anode technology replaces the graphite in conventional lithium-ion batteries with silicon, which can store roughly 20 percent more energy by weight. Sila’s product, Titan Silicon, was the first commercial silicon anode material to reach consumers when it launched inside the WHOOP wearable in 2021. Mercedes-Benz has named the electric EQG as the first vehicle to use the material, but no automotive batteries containing Titan Silicon have shipped yet.

The Moses Lake facility sits on 160 acres and opened in the fall of 2025, running Phase 1 production at a capacity of 2 gigawatt-hours. The $300 million funds Phase 2 expansion, which Sila says will scale the site toward a target of 250 gigawatt-hours over five years, a figure that would make it one of the largest battery material plants ever built. That target remains aspirational, as even major automakers building US battery plants have struggled to scale production at the speeds their initial plans projected.

The supply chain pitch is central to the raise. China controls more than 90 percent of global anode material processing and more than 80 percent of battery cell production, according to figures the company cites from the Department of Energy and International Energy Agency. Sila positions its Moses Lake plant as a domestic alternative, producing silicon anode material from silane gas supplied by REC Silicon under a long-term agreement rather than relying on Chinese graphite.

Gene Berdichevsky, Sila’s co-founder and CEO, was among Tesla’s earliest employees and founded the company in 2011 to develop silicon anode chemistry as an alternative to the graphite anodes that dominate lithium-ion batteries. The company raised $375 million in June 2024 from investors including Coatue and Canada Pension Plan Investment Board. In-Q-Tel’s presence on the cap table signals that US defense and intelligence agencies view domestic battery material supply as a national security priority.

The round arrives as next-generation battery technologies attract growing investment from both automakers and financial sponsors, with billions flowing into solid-state, silicon anode, and sodium-ion chemistries. Whether Sila can scale from a wearable-sized product to automotive volumes at a pace that justifies its fundraising trajectory is the question this round is designed to answer. No silicon anode company has yet crossed the gap between a 2 gigawatt-hour pilot and the kind of production capacity that would make it a genuine alternative to Chinese supply chains.

[TEXT]
Swedish electric and autonomous trucking company Einride has agreed to acquire EV charging startup Flipturn in an all-stock deal worth $38 million.

The acquisition — Einride’s first as a publicly traded company — will expand its EV-charging ecosystem as it works to scale its electric trucking business. The deal is expected to close this month. Flipturn, which employs 17 people, will continue operating with the same team, a company spokesperson confirmed.

Flipturn, founded in 2022, raised $15.5 million in venture capital to build an EV-charging software platform that can communicate with any EV charger over the Open Charge Point Protocol, the industry standard for communication between EV chargers and management systems. The software monitors the health of charging infrastructure; pulls in data such as vehicle battery levels, driving history, and routes to help companies better manage charging; and integrates with on-site solar and energy-storage systems.

Einride CEO Roozbeh Charli said the “acquisition is a decisive step in our U.S. scaling strategy.” The deal will also make Einride a more vertically integrated company, allowing it to offer customers not just electric trucks but also the charging software needed to run them more efficiently and reliably.

That’s an attractive, and potentially influential, pitch to future customers that want to add electric big rigs to their fleets but may be apprehensive to do so.

Einride is perhaps best known for its unusual-looking cabless autonomous podlike trucks. But its biggest revenue driver is its fleet of 200 heavy-duty electric trucks, which it operates for companies such as Heineken, PepsiCo, and Carlsberg Sweden in Europe, North America, and the United Arab Emirates.

The company, which merged with a blank-check company and debuted on Nasdaq in June, recently landed Amazon as a customer. Amazon isn’t buying Einride’s trucks; instead, Einride owns and manages the trucks —through its own Saga AI software — which can be used by drivers in Amazon’s Relay freight network. Relay, which launched in 2017, is an app that truck drivers use to book hauling gigs with Amazon.

[TITLE]Africa: President Xi Jinping’s vision for inclusive AI governance:
[TEXT]
Artificial intelligence is rapidly transforming economies, governance, security, healthcare, education, and international relations. Beyond its technological capabilities, AI has become a strategic asset that will significantly influence global economic competitiveness, geopolitical power, and sustainable development throughout the twenty-first century. Consequently, the conversation is no longer limited to how AI should be developed. Still, it increasingly focuses on who governs it, who benefits from it, and how its opportunities can be shared equitably.

Against this backdrop, President Xi Jinping’s four observations on AI development and governance during his keynote address at the opening ceremony of the 2026 World AI Conference and High-level Meetings on Global AI Governance present an important vision for a more inclusive global AI ecosystem. His emphasis on openness, international cooperation, responsible innovation, and broader participation by developing countries challenges the notion that AI should remain concentrated within a small number of technologically advanced economies. Instead, China advocates an AI future in which the Global South actively participates in innovation, governance, and capacity building.

For Africa, this vision arrives at a critical moment. The continent possesses abundant human capital, rapidly growing digital economies, expanding technology ecosystems, and increasing political commitment to digital transformation. However, significant challenges, including inadequate digital infrastructure, shortages of AI specialists, limited research funding, and unequal access to advanced technologies, continue to constrain Africa’s AI ambitions. President Xi’s proposals, therefore, offer both an opportunity and a strategic direction for narrowing these gaps through international cooperation.

Therefore, this article examines President Xi Jinping’s vision through the AFRICA Framework, an analytical model I developed to evaluate its relevance for Nigeria, Africa, and the wider Global South. The framework identifies six interconnected pillars: Access, Fairness, Responsibility, Innovation, Cooperation, and Advancement, which collectively provide a roadmap for inclusive AI development and governance.

A – Access: expanding opportunities for inclusive AI development. Access represents the foundation of every successful AI ecosystem. Without access to quality education, computing infrastructure, research facilities, datasets, cloud technologies, and technical expertise, developing countries cannot participate meaningfully in the AI revolution.

President Xi Jinping’s emphasis on openness directly addresses this challenge. China’s commitment to provide 5,000 AI training and seminar opportunities for developing countries, including support for the African Union, demonstrates that capacity building should accompany technological advancement. Such initiatives strengthen human capital while reducing barriers that have historically limited developing countries’ participation in emerging technologies.

For Nigeria, expanded access means strengthening universities, supporting AI research, improving broadband connectivity, expanding digital literacy, and creating opportunities for young innovators. Access transforms AI from an imported technology into a domestic development tool capable of improving healthcare, agriculture, education, financial services, public administration, and national security.

F – Fairness: Ensuring equitable global AI governance.

Access alone is insufficient if developing countries remain excluded from international decision-making. Fairness requires that all nations participate in shaping the policies, standards, ethical principles, and governance mechanisms that will regulate AI.

President Xi’s proposal for inclusive global AI governance recognises that a few countries should not monopolise technological leadership. As AI increasingly influences global development, Africa deserves a stronger voice in determining the international rules governing data, ethics, cybersecurity, digital sovereignty, and responsible innovation.

For Nigeria and Africa, fairness means moving from being technology adopters to becoming policy contributors. Through the African Union and other multilateral institutions, African countries can collectively advocate for governance frameworks that reflect their developmental priorities while promoting equitable participation within the international AI ecosystem.

R – Responsibility: Promoting ethical and secure artificial intelligence

The transformative potential of AI also introduces significant ethical and security challenges. Algorithmic bias, misinformation, cyber threats, privacy concerns, deepfakes, and autonomous systems demonstrate that technological advancement must be accompanied by responsible governance.

President Xi’s emphasis on responsible AI reflects the principle that innovation should always serve humanity. AI must remain transparent, accountable, secure, and subject to appropriate human oversight.

For Africa, responsible AI requires strengthening cybersecurity institutions, protecting personal data, promoting ethical standards, developing appropriate regulatory frameworks, and ensuring that AI supports rather than undermines democratic governance, human rights, and sustainable development. Responsible governance ultimately strengthens public confidence while encouraging long-term innovation.

I – Innovation: Building indigenous technological capacity.

Innovation represents the engine of sustainable AI development. Countries that generate knowledge, develop technologies, and commercialise research achieve greater economic competitiveness and technological sovereignty.

President Xi’s support for open-source collaboration and scientific cooperation creates opportunities for developing countries to strengthen their own innovation ecosystems. Open innovation lowers barriers to research, encourages collaboration among universities and industry, and enables local adaptation of AI technologies.

Africa already possesses enormous innovative potential. Nigerian startups have demonstrated AI applications in financial technology, healthcare, agriculture, logistics, cybersecurity, and education. However, sustained progress requires greater investment in research and development, innovation financing, university-industry collaboration, intellectual property protection, and AI research centres. International cooperation should therefore prioritise technology transfer and joint research capable of strengthening indigenous technological capability.

C – Cooperation: Strengthening China–Africa partnership.

No nation can fully realise AI’s potential in isolation. Artificial intelligence requires cooperation among governments, universities, research institutions, private industry, and international organisations.

President Xi’s vision places cooperation at the centre of global AI governance. China’s expanding partnerships with Africa in digital infrastructure, education, telecommunications, research, and capacity building provide a strong foundation for deeper collaboration in AI.

For Nigeria, cooperation should focus on mutually beneficial partnerships that promote technology transfer, joint innovation laboratories, research collaboration, AI education, startup development, and institutional strengthening. Such cooperation should empower African countries to become equal partners in technological development rather than remaining dependent consumers of imported technologies.

Equally important is South–South cooperation. By strengthening collaboration among developing countries, China supports a more balanced international technological order in which Africa contributes actively to global innovation and governance.

A – Advancement: Positioning Africa for global AI leadership.

The ultimate objective of the AFRICA Framework is Advancement. Access, Fairness, Responsibility, Innovation, and Cooperation collectively create the conditions necessary for Africa to emerge as an influential participant in the global AI economy.

President Xi’s vision encourages developing countries to move beyond technology adoption toward technology creation, governance leadership, and scientific excellence. Africa possesses unique demographic advantages, entrepreneurial talent, expanding digital markets, and increasing political commitment to innovation. These strengths position the continent to become a major contributor to global AI development.

For Nigeria, advancement requires sustained investment in STEM education, AI research, digital infrastructure, cybersecurity, innovation financing, cloud computing, data centres, and supportive regulatory institutions. By combining domestic reforms with strategic international partnerships, Nigeria can become one of Africa’s leading AI economies while contributing meaningfully to global AI governance.

President Xi Jinping’s vision represents more than a technological strategy; it proposes a broader philosophy of inclusive global development. Rather than framing AI as an arena of geopolitical competition alone, his proposals encourage international cooperation, shared innovation, and wider participation by developing countries. This perspective is particularly relevant for Africa, where AI has enormous potential to accelerate economic diversification, strengthen public institutions, improve healthcare, expand educational opportunities, enhance food security, and support sustainable industrialisation.

China’s commitment to capacity building, open collaboration, and support for the African Union demonstrates that its proposals extend beyond diplomatic rhetoric into practical cooperation. While successful implementation will depend upon sustained commitment from all partners, these initiatives offer African countries meaningful opportunities to strengthen their domestic AI ecosystems.

Nevertheless, Africa’s success cannot depend solely upon external assistance. Governments must complement international partnerships with domestic investments in education, infrastructure, research, innovation, cybersecurity, and institutional capacity. International cooperation should accelerate Africa’s development while preserving technological sovereignty and supporting indigenous innovation.

President Xi Jinping’s four observations on artificial intelligence provide a compelling vision for a more inclusive, cooperative, and equitable system of global AI governance. Through the AFRICA Framework: Access, Fairness, Responsibility, Innovation, Cooperation, and Advancement, this article has demonstrated that China’s proposals offer strategic opportunities for Nigeria, Africa, and the wider Global South.

The future of AI should not be defined by technological monopolies or digital inequality but by shared innovation, responsible governance, and mutually beneficial partnerships. President Xi’s vision recognises that lasting technological progress depends upon empowering all countries to participate in shaping the AI era.

For Africa, the challenge is clear. The continent must seize this opportunity by investing in its people, strengthening its institutions, expanding scientific research, and engaging constructively with international partners. If these efforts are pursued with strategic commitment, Africa will not merely participate in the global AI revolution; it will help lead it. The AFRICA Framework provides a practical roadmap for achieving that aspiration while reinforcing the principles of openness, cooperation, responsibility, and shared prosperity that underpin President Xi Jinping’s vision.

Initial Investment and Proposed Commercial Framework Further Advance Rocket One's AI Infrastructure Strategy; Placeve's AI Processor Technology Is Currently Under Evaluation Through NASA's MISSE Program

HOBOKEN, N.J., July 21, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- Rocket One Inc. (Nasdaq: RKTO) ("Rocket One" or the "Company"), an AI infrastructure company focused on defense, space and intelligent edge computing, today announced a strategic collaboration with Placeve Inc., an innovator in energy-efficient artificial intelligence processors and hardware platforms, further advancing Rocket One's strategy to build a next-generation AI infrastructure platform serving defense, space and intelligent edge computing markets.

The collaboration is expected to support Rocket One's long-term strategy of building an AI infrastructure platform serving the rapidly expanding defense, space and intelligent edge computing markets, where demand for energy-efficient, high-performance computing continues to accelerate.

As part of the collaboration, Rocket One has made an initial investment in Placeve. The companies intend to jointly evaluate Placeve's proprietary Fourier Processing Unit (FPU)-based AI processor architecture, software platform and machine-learning technologies for applications spanning defense, autonomous systems, commercial edge computing and the expanding space economy.

The collaboration is expected to include joint engineering initiatives focused on AI hardware integration, software optimization, AI workload acceleration, performance-per-watt improvements, validation testing and deployment pathways for commercial, defense and orbital environments.

"Artificial intelligence is rapidly moving beyond traditional data centers into satellites, autonomous systems, defense platforms and critical infrastructure," said Robb Knie, Chief Executive Officer of Rocket One. "Those environments require fundamentally different computing architectures that maximize performance while minimizing power consumption and operating reliably in mission-critical conditions. Our strategy is to build Rocket One into a leading AI infrastructure company serving these rapidly expanding markets. We believe this collaboration broadens our technology ecosystem, accelerates our access to next-generation AI computing platforms and further positions Rocket One at the intersection of artificial intelligence, defense and the space economy."

Rocket One believes the next generation of AI infrastructure will increasingly depend on specialized computing architectures capable of delivering high-performance inference while reducing power consumption in environments where reliability, resilience and efficiency are mission critical.

The collaboration complements Rocket One's previously announced nanomagnetic and spintronic computing initiatives designed to improve AI acceleration, memory performance and energy efficiency, as well as the Company's pending provisional patent applications covering an AI-powered autonomous defense and space security platform intended to protect critical infrastructure across terrestrial and orbital environments. Together, these initiatives reflect Rocket One's broader strategy of building an integrated AI infrastructure platform for government, commercial and space markets.

Under the proposed collaboration, Placeve will contribute its AI processor architecture, software tools and engineering expertise, while Rocket One will contribute application requirements, systems integration priorities and deployment strategies focused on defense, intelligent edge and space infrastructure. Through ongoing engineering collaboration and roadmap development, the companies expect to accelerate validation and potential commercialization of energy-efficient AI computing solutions.

"Rocket One's vision for next-generation AI infrastructure aligns exceptionally well with our mission to deliver high-performance AI computing with dramatically improved energy efficiency," said Marc Hensel, Chief Executive Officer of Placeve. "Working closely with Rocket One allows us to evaluate our technology against demanding real-world defense, space and edge computing requirements while creating a pathway toward broader commercial deployment."

Placeve's photonic-electronic AI processor technology is currently being evaluated through NASA's Materials International Space Station Experiment (MISSE) program aboard the International Space Station, providing operational experience for computing platforms designed to operate in demanding environments.
