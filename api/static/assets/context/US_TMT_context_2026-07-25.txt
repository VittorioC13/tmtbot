[TITLE]IBM to Acquire HRL Laboratories to Power the Future of Quantum:
[TEXT]
HRL's expertise in silicon-spin qubits and quantum sensing will strengthen and extend IBM's world-leading quantum computing mission

ARMONK, N.Y. and MALIBU, Calif., July 23, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- IBM (NYSE: IBM) today announced it has signed a definitive agreement to acquire HRL Laboratories, LLC (HRL), a flagship research and development institution. HRL is a private company jointly owned by Boeing and General Motors. Both Boeing and GM will continue to partner with IBM on quantum applications and advanced technology development following the transaction.

HRL's advanced expertise in silicon-spin qubit engineering will complement and extend IBM's long-term mission to scale increasingly powerful quantum computers and accelerate its quantum vision. Superconducting qubits and spin qubits both leverage state-of-the art silicon fabrication. This shared foundation is amongst the reasons why these two modalities offer credible paths to scaling quantum technologies.

"The HRL team will help IBM push even farther forward toward the frontiers of quantum innovation," said Jay Gambetta, IBM's Director of Research and IBM Fellow. "This talented group of researchers brings a broad portfolio of technologies that will strengthen IBM's long-term plans to deliver useful quantum computing to the world, bringing together advances across quantum computing, quantum sensing, and quantum networking to enable the applications of the future."

"Joining IBM is the natural next chapter for what we have built at HRL, where our team has dedicated years to exploring paths to how future quantum computers could be built at scales that today seem impossible," said Rob Vasquez, President and Chief Executive Officer at HRL "We now look forward to leveraging IBM's industry leadership and working alongside their world-class talent on fundamental infrastructure to take this vision forward. Additionally, our cutting-edge physical and information science innovations will combine with their advanced research capabilities to deliver an unmatched suite of technology solutions for our commercial and government customers."

HRL will also enable IBM to innovate in and industrialize promising technologies such as quantum sensing and drive new research into quantum materials. This includes ultra precise quantum sensors capable of detecting subtle physical phenomena and capturing finely tuned measurements for life sciences, navigation, defense, and scientific applications. Combined with additional capabilities in cryogenics, control electronics, qubit interconnects, and packaging, IBM anticipates that HRL's technical breakthroughs will help fuel its quantum program for decades to come.

Additionally, HRL has developed innovations in novel quantum materials that have the potential to unlock better semiconductors and more sensitive sensors – all of which can optimize the performance and scalability of a wide range of quantum technologies.

Beyond its leadership in quantum computing, HRL brings deep expertise in advanced sensors, high-speed and high-power communications, electronics, advanced manufacturing, and materials science, developed through decades of research and development for both commercial and U.S. government customers. HRL's broad technology portfolio will complement IBM's innovation leadership and help accelerate the development of next-generation computing, communications, and mission-critical systems.

Advancing Quantum Computers for Generations to Come

IBM continues to define the direction for the industry with superconducting qubit-based architectures, including breakthroughs in error correction and new algorithms enabling quantum computers to run harder problems more efficiently. IBM's roadmap to deliver the world's first large-scale, fault-tolerant quantum computers is clear and on course. This includes delivering IBM Quantum Starling by 2029, which will be 20,000 times more powerful than today's quantum computers and capable of running 100 million quantum operations. In the mid-2030s, Starling will be followed by the even more powerful Blue Jay quantum computer, projected to be capable of 1 billion quantum operations.

As IBM looks to further extend quantum computing, HRL will bring robust knowledge of silicon‑based spin qubit platforms and surrounding infrastructure that could offer new insights into how to best scale quantum computers into the next decade.

In May 2026, IBM further expanded its global quantum leadership when the company announced it would establish Anderon, the world's first pure-play quantum wafer foundry. As a standalone IBM company, Anderon is being created with the support of the U.S. Department of Commerce to enable scalable, consistent, and agile manufacturing for a broad range of quantum computing modalities and companies. The acquisition of HRL offers an opportunity to partner even more closely with Anderon, including potential plans to develop spin qubit manufacturing to scale quantum manufacturing and enable faster learning cycles.

Financial details of the transaction were not disclosed, and IBM's acquisition of HRL is subject to customary closing conditions and regulatory approvals. The transaction is anticipated to close by the end of the third quarter of 2026.

[TITLE]Shettima: Africa Must Collaborate to Shape AI, Telecom Polic:
[TEXT]
Vice President Kashim Shettima has called on African countries to deepen cooperation and present a common position in global discussions on artificial intelligence, telecommunications and digital governance.

Shettima said no African country could independently achieve the continent’s digital ambitions, stressing that unity would strengthen Africa’s influence over policies and standards shaping emerging technologies.

He spoke on Thursday at the opening of the seventh Ordinary Session of the Conference of Plenipotentiaries of the African Telecommunications Union at the Bola Ahmed Tinubu International Conference Centre, Abuja.

The Vice President was represented by the Deputy Chief of Staff to the President, Ibrahim Hadejia.

Shettima said the conference offered African countries an opportunity to coordinate their priorities ahead of the 2026 International Telecommunication Union Plenipotentiary Conference and support African candidates seeking positions within the ITU.

“No single nation, regardless of its size or resources, can fully realise Africa’s digital ambitions in isolation. Our future depends on collaboration,” he said.

He urged countries to work together to expand affordable internet access, strengthen cybersecurity, improve spectrum management, harmonise standards and develop rules for responsible artificial intelligence.

“We must work together to expand affordable connectivity, strengthen cybersecurity, promote responsible AI governance, harmonise standards, improve spectrum management and accelerate digital trade across our continent,” Shettima added.

The Vice President said digital technologies had become central to economic competitiveness, public service delivery and employment opportunities, particularly for Africa’s young population.

According to him, broadband networks, artificial intelligence, cloud computing, satellite communications and secure digital infrastructure had become as important to development as roads, power, railways and ports.

Shettima said Africa must move beyond merely participating in international technology debates and begin shaping the decisions that would determine the future of digital development.

“For too long, global technology policies have often been developed without adequate representation of African priorities and perspectives. That must change,” he said.

He added, “When Africa speaks with one voice, our influence is amplified, our priorities receive greater attention and our collective interests are better protected.”

Shettima said Nigeria remained committed to broadband expansion, artificial intelligence, digital public infrastructure, cybersecurity, innovation and the development of a competitive technology workforce under President Bola Tinubu’s administration.

He also linked Africa’s digital transformation to the African Union’s Agenda 2063 and the Digital Transformation Strategy for Africa, noting that technology must translate into jobs, better education, improved healthcare, financial inclusion and stronger participation by women and young people.

“The next chapter of Africa’s integration will not be written by roads and rail alone. It will also be written by fibre, satellites, data, artificial intelligence and the partnerships that connect our people and our economies,” he said.

The Minister of Communications, Innovation and Digital Economy, Bosun Tijani, said Africa must arrive at the ITU conference later in the year prepared, united and credible.

He said the continent had a major opportunity to influence the next phase of the global digital economy because of its young population, growing entrepreneurial base and rapidly expanding technology ecosystem.

“Africa must arrive prepared, united and credible. We must continue to invest in technical preparation, strengthen regional cooperation and speak with one voice where our interests align,” Tijani said.

He said ATU membership had risen to 52 countries, while the union had strengthened its legal framework and made progress in implementing its 2023–2027 Strategic and Financial Plan.

The minister also announced the expansion of Nigeria’s N-ATLAS artificial intelligence project into a continental initiative known as the ATLAS Network.

According to him, fewer than two per cent of African languages are meaningfully supported by modern AI systems, despite the continent accounting for nearly one-third of the world’s languages.

Tijani said Nigeria launched N-ATLAS, described as Africa’s first government-backed open-source large language model, at the United Nations General Assembly in 2025.

“Today, I am pleased to announce that, together with partners across Africa, we are taking the next step by expanding this initiative into a continental framework, the ATLAS Network,” he said.

The minister explained that the network would bring together governments, researchers, innovators, universities and technology partners to develop artificial intelligence systems that reflect African languages, cultures and knowledge.

“Our ambition is for the ATLAS Network to become a platform that accelerates the development of locally relevant large language models in Africa, by Africa and for Africa,” he said.

He invited all ATU member countries to join the project, adding that Africa should not limit itself to adopting artificial intelligence but should participate in determining its direction.

“This is our opportunity not simply to adopt artificial intelligence, but to help shape its future, for Africa and from Africa,” Tijani said.

The Secretary-General of the International Telecommunication Union, Doreen Bogdan-Martin, said Africa was already moving from being a consumer of technology to becoming an innovator, standard setter and global partner.

“Africa will not simply participate in the digital future. Africa will help shape it, not only as a consumer of technology, but as a creator, an innovator, a standard setter and a trusted global partner,” she said.

Bogdan-Martin noted that telecommunications networks were expanding across the continent, while digital public infrastructure was changing the way governments provided services.

She said the ITU and ATU had worked together on radio communications, standardisation, school connectivity, capacity development and sustainable financing for Africa’s digital infrastructure.

According to her, African perspectives would be critical at the ITU Plenipotentiary Conference, where decisions would be taken on the organisation’s priorities and direction for the next four years.

“Africa’s voice has never mattered more. Its experience, its innovation and its leadership will help to shape not only the future of the ITU, but the future of digital cooperation worldwide,” she said.

The Secretary-General of the ATU, John Omo, urged member countries to increase their financial contributions to the union, warning that it could not deliver on its mandate without adequate resources.

“We cannot have a union without resources. Member states’ commitment in terms of contributions will be central to ensuring and fulfilling the plan that we will set before you,” Omo said.

He disclosed that the proposed budget for the 2027–2031 cycle included more than $1.7m for mandatory activities requiring additional funding.

Omo said member-state contributions remained the foundation of the ATU’s operations, although the union had also expanded partnerships with organisations including the United Nations Office for Digital and Emerging Technologies, GSMA, Qualcomm, Helios Towers, SBA Communications, American Tower Corporation and the Nigerian Communications Commission.

He said delegates would consider the ATU’s institutional, financial and strategic direction, as well as leadership, resources and Africa’s preparation for the 2026 ITU conference.

The Executive Vice Chairman of the NCC, Aminu Maida, also called for African unity, particularly as member states prepared to elect a new leadership team for the continental body.

Maida said the meeting was important to Nigeria and the continent because of the decisions and elections expected to shape Africa’s future position in the telecommunications sector.

He said stronger coordination among member countries would help Africa protect its interests and improve its standing in the global telecommunications industry.

“The next chapter of Africa’s integration will not be written by roads and rail alone. It will also be written by fibre, satellites, data, artificial intelligence and the partnerships that connect our people and our economies,” he said.

The Minister of Communications, Innovation and Digital Economy, Bosun Tijani, said Africa must arrive at the ITU conference later in the year prepared, united and credible.

He said the continent had a major opportunity to influence the next phase of the global digital economy because of its young population, growing entrepreneurial base and rapidly expanding technology ecosystem.

“Africa must arrive prepared, united and credible. We must continue to invest in technical preparation, strengthen regional cooperation and speak with one voice where our interests align,” Tijani said.

He said ATU membership had risen to 52 countries, while the union had strengthened its legal framework and made progress in implementing its 2023–2027 Strategic and Financial Plan.

The minister also announced the expansion of Nigeria’s N-ATLAS artificial intelligence project into a continental initiative known as the ATLAS Network.

According to him, fewer than two per cent of African languages are meaningfully supported by modern AI systems, despite the continent accounting for nearly one-third of the world’s languages.

Tijani said Nigeria launched N-ATLAS, described as Africa’s first government-backed open-source large language model, at the United Nations General Assembly in 2025.

“Today, I am pleased to announce that, together with partners across Africa, we are taking the next step by expanding this initiative into a continental framework, the ATLAS Network,” he said.

The minister explained that the network would bring together governments, researchers, innovators, universities and technology partners to develop artificial intelligence systems that reflect African languages, cultures and knowledge.

“Our ambition is for the ATLAS Network to become a platform that accelerates the development of locally relevant large language models in Africa, by Africa and for Africa,” he said.

He invited all ATU member countries to join the project, adding that Africa should not limit itself to adopting artificial intelligence but should participate in determining its direction.

“This is our opportunity not simply to adopt artificial intelligence, but to help shape its future, for Africa and from Africa,” Tijani said.

The Secretary-General of the International Telecommunication Union, Doreen Bogdan-Martin, said Africa was already moving from being a consumer of technology to becoming an innovator, standard setter and global partner.

“Africa will not simply participate in the digital future. Africa will help shape it, not only as a consumer of technology, but as a creator, an innovator, a standard setter and a trusted global partner,” she said.

Bogdan-Martin noted that telecommunications networks were expanding across the continent, while digital public infrastructure was changing the way governments provided services.

She said the ITU and ATU had worked together on radio communications, standardisation, school connectivity, capacity development and sustainable financing for Africa’s digital infrastructure.

According to her, African perspectives would be critical at the ITU Plenipotentiary Conference, where decisions would be taken on the organisation’s priorities and direction for the next four years.

“Africa’s voice has never mattered more. Its experience, its innovation and its leadership will help to shape not only the future of the ITU, but the future of digital cooperation worldwide,” she said.

The Secretary-General of the ATU, John Omo, urged member countries to increase their financial contributions to the union, warning that it could not deliver on its mandate without adequate resources.

“We cannot have a union without resources. Member states’ commitment in terms of contributions will be central to ensuring and fulfilling the plan that we will set before you,” Omo said.

He disclosed that the proposed budget for the 2027–2031 cycle included more than $1.7m for mandatory activities requiring additional funding.

Omo said member-state contributions remained the foundation of the ATU’s operations, although the union had also expanded partnerships with organisations including the United Nations Office for Digital and Emerging Technologies, GSMA, Qualcomm, Helios Towers, SBA Communications, American Tower Corporation and the Nigerian Communications Commission.

He said delegates would consider the ATU’s institutional, financial and strategic direction, as well as leadership, resources and Africa’s preparation for the 2026 ITU conference.

The Executive Vice Chairman of the NCC, Aminu Maida, also called for African unity, particularly as member states prepared to elect a new leadership team for the continental body.

Maida said the meeting was important to Nigeria and the continent because of the decisions and elections expected to shape Africa’s future position in the telecommunications sector.

He said stronger coordination among member countries would help Africa protect its interests and improve its standing in the global telecommunications industry.
